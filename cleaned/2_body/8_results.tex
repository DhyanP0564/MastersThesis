The formulation chapters made their claims about operators. Each claim predicts a filter degree through \Cref{eq:degree}, and the measurements below ask \emph{whether the measured degrees follow those laws, and on which problems}. They use the benchmark problems of \Cref{tab:ladder} with the pipeline and conventions of \Cref{sec:pipeline}, validated here before any cost is compared, and the minimax filter at $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$ unless stated. Every result is a classical computation, by circuit simulation, exact emulation or resource estimation (\Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}), and a gap, a norm or a reference's error is an exact classical computation on the assembled residual. Every problem in both forms is tabulated in \Cref{app:catalogue-results}; the reproduction of Wu et al.'s example problems, which tests their method rather than the questions posed here, is in \Cref{app:reproduction}.

\subsection{Validation of the Pipeline}
\label{sec:results-faithful}

Every number in this chapter is read from one production of $\res{production}{runs}$ runs, each of which left a record of its configuration, its outcome and the source code that produced it (\Cref{app:software-campaigns}). Of these, $\res{production}{ok}$ completed; the other $\res{production}{infeasible}$, all circuit simulations, were refused by their own resource estimators and were not attempted. Solved classically through its reference (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}), the standard form converges spectrally on every linear benchmark problem, its smallest trusted error of the decoded field being $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/best}{field_err_linf}$ on R2a at $n=\res{build/R2a/standard/best}{n}$, $\res[2,sci]{build/R4b/standard/best}{field_err_linf}$ on the inhomogeneous R4b at $n=\res{build/R4b/standard/best}{n}$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R5b/standard/best}{field_err_linf}$ on the heat equation at $n=\res{build/R5b/standard/best}{n}$ per axis; the descriptor reaches $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/descriptor/best}{field_err_linf}$ on R2a. These references are the yardstick for every preparation below.

\paragraph{Verification of the encodings.} Each circuit is compared with an operator assembled independently from the problem, in one of three modes (\Cref{tab:verification-modes}). All $\res{verification/all}{count}$ verifications, $\res{verification/exact}{count}$ exact, $\res{verification/probe}{count}$ by probe and $\res{verification/ir}{count}$ by \gls{ir}, meet their mode's tolerance by at least four orders of magnitude, the least margin a factor of $\res[2,sci]{verification/all}{least_margin}$ (\Cref{fig:verification-accuracy}). The exact mode's worst entrywise error is $\res[2,sci]{encode/R6a/standard/n3}{verify_max_error}$, and its widest verification, a whole reflection of the reproduction on $\res{encode/R4d/faithful/standard/n3}{n_qubits_total}$ qubits, is accurate to $\res[2,sci]{encode/R4d/faithful/standard/n3}{verify_max_error}$. The probe mode's worst is $\res[2,sci]{encode/R10c-M12/descriptor/n2/carleman=2/verify=probe}{verify_max_error}$, on a Navier--Stokes lift's reflection of $\res{encode/R10c-M12/descriptor/n2/carleman=2/verify=probe}{n_qubits_total}$ qubits, and the \gls{ir} mode verifies the tensor layout's coupling on $\res{lift/R10c-M316/standard/n0/carleman=2/lift=tensor/verify=ir}{modes}$ modes to $\res[2,sci]{lift/R10c-M316/standard/n0/carleman=2/lift=tensor/verify=ir}{verify_max_error}$. \paragraph{Circuit simulation against exact emulation.} On all $\res{agreement/T1/minimax}{count}$ circuit simulations with the minimax filter, the simulated output agrees with the exact emulation's before normalising to within the bar of $10^{-10}$. The worst differences are $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R4a/descriptor/n3/T1/filter=minimax}{filtered_agreement}$ in the descriptor (R4a at $n=3$, degree $\res{stateprep/R4a/descriptor/n3/T1/filter=minimax}{degree}$), $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R4a/standard/n2/T1/filter=minimax}{filtered_agreement}$ in the standard form (R4a at $n=2$, degree $\res{stateprep/R4a/standard/n2/T1/filter=minimax}{degree}$) and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R9c-M4/descriptor/n2/T1/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{filtered_agreement}$ on a Carleman lift (Burgers on four modes, $\res{stateprep/R9c-M4/descriptor/n2/T1/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits). Wherever both run, this confirms the argument of \Cref{sec:pipeline-verification} that the exact emulation is the circuit's output, and so licenses the emulation at widths no simulation reaches. The widest circuit simulated for a problem as run has $\res{stateprep/R4d/descriptor/n3/T1/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits. The two-axis descriptor circuits, of $\res{stateprep/R5b/descriptor/n2x2/T1/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ to $\res{stateprep/R5a/descriptor/n2x2/T1/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits, exceed the simulation budget: the narrowest is estimated at $\res[2]{stateprep/R5b/descriptor/n2x2/T1/filter=minimax}{predicted_seconds}$\,s against a budget of $\res[2]{stateprep/R5b/descriptor/n2x2/T1/filter=minimax}{budget_seconds}$\,s, a limit of the classical simulator and not of the circuit. Of the reproduction's $\res{agreement/T1/qite}{count}$ imaginary-time simulations, $\res{agreement/T1/qite}{misses}$, at degrees of order $10^5$, miss the bar; they are reported with their degrees in \Cref{app:repro-t1}.

\paragraph{Accuracy of the prepared states.} Each of the $\res{preparation/T2/minimax}{trusted}$ trusted preparations by exact emulation meets $\varepsilon$ and its a priori bound, in both forms, on every linear problem and every Carleman lift, and its decoded field meets the error budget of \Cref{eq:error-budget} (\Cref{tab:catalogue-results}). The bound is tight: on R2a in the descriptor at $n=8$ the infidelity is $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{infidelity}$ against the bound $(1-\gamma^2)\varepsilon^2/\gamma^2=\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{infidelity_bound}$ of \Cref{sec:pipeline-filter}. \Cref{fig:decoded-field} follows the prepared field's error on R2a against $n$. It falls with the discretisation's error to $n=3$ and then holds, at $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n4/T2/filter=minimax}{field_err_linf}$ at $n=4$ and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{field_err_linf}$ at $n=8$, while the discretisation's own error falls to $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{eps_disc}$. The filter leaves the same infidelity at every $n$, so from $n=4$ the field's accuracy is set by $\varepsilon$, which the degree pays for logarithmically (\Cref{eq:minimax-degree}), and a finer grid gains nothing. The budget, $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{field_budget}$ at $n=8$, overestimates the error by more than an order of magnitude, as a readout bound that takes the state's worst direction must, and the margin widens with $n$: the bound's amplification grows from $\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n4/T2/filter=minimax}{kappa_read}$ at $n=4$ to $\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{kappa_read}$ at $n=8$, while the prepared field's error stays where it is.

\paragraph{Choice of initial state.} The geometric initial state of \Cref{eq:geometric-state} assumes that the solution's coefficients decay geometrically, and overlaps well where they do: on R1 at $n=3$, $\gamma^2=\res[3]{stateprep/R1/standard/n3/T2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$, against $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R1/standard/n3/T2/kind=uniform/filter=minimax/initial-state-control}{gamma2}$ for the uniform state and $\res[2]{stateprep/R1/standard/n3/T2/kind=random/filter=minimax/initial-state-control}{gamma2}$ for a random one. On Legendre's equation R3a, whose solution is the polynomial $P_4$, the uniform state does better, $\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/standard/n3/T2/kind=uniform/filter=minimax/initial-state-control}{gamma2}$ against the geometric state's $\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/standard/n3/T2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ (exact emulation). The degree does not depend on the initial state, so every control meets $\varepsilon$ at the same degree, and a poor guess costs only the amplification factor $1/\gamma$ of \Cref{eq:total-cost}.

\paragraph{The filter and its degree.} On synthetic spectra with the ground state at the edge, imaginary time needs $\res[3]{filters/synthetic/edge}{low}$--$\res[3]{filters/synthetic/edge}{high}$ times the minimax filter's degree at equal suppression, whatever the gap, and on the benchmark problems compared the certified ratio falls inside that range in both forms (exact emulation). At equal measured infidelity the margin is smaller, and at $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$ imaginary time needs the lower degree on R1 in the standard form and on R3a and R5a in the descriptor (\Cref{app:filter-study}). The choice of filter sets the degree's constant and leaves its growth with $N$ unchanged. The degree itself is fixed a priori from the spectral edge, which every run takes from the reference; an underestimated edge raises the degree as the inverse square root of the gap assumed and keeps the target, and the study on R2a, in which Wu et al.'s time rule falls short of $\varepsilon$, is given in \Cref{app:edge-study}. Both forms run with the same filter, initial state and edge, so with the circuit, the emulation and the prepared field checked wherever a check can run, the differences measured in the rest of the chapter are differences between residuals.

\paragraph{Queries and gates.} The degree counts queries, and a query's gates differ between the forms. On R2a one descriptor query costs more two-qubit gates than one of the structured standard form at every resolution built and verified, $\res{encode/R2a/descriptor/n2}{cx_count}$ against $\res{encode/R2a/standard/n2}{cx_count}$ at $n=2$ and $\res{encode/R2a/descriptor/n5/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ against $\res{encode/R2a/standard/n5/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ at $n=5$, since it applies more terms through a block register; its count grows more slowly, and the estimates at $n=8$ put the two level (\Cref{tab:headline}). On R3a, whose lifted multiplier the structured encoding pays for in full, the descriptor's query is the cheaper from $n=4$: $\res{encode/R3a/descriptor/n4/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ against $\res{encode/R3a/standard/n4/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ at $n=4$ and $\res{encode/R3a/descriptor/n5/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ against $\res{encode/R3a/standard/n5/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ at $n=5$. The ancilla's growth, $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ against $n$ (\Cref{sec:descriptor-cost}), first shows at $n=8$ (\Cref{tab:headline}); at $n=2$ and $5$ the descriptor's reflection on R2a needs $\res{encode/R2a/descriptor/n2}{n_anc_qubits}$ and $\res{encode/R2a/descriptor/n5/verify=probe}{n_anc_qubits}$ ancilla against the standard form's $\res{encode/R2a/standard/n2}{n_anc_qubits}$ and $\res{encode/R2a/standard/n5/verify=probe}{n_anc_qubits}$. On constant-coefficient problems the descriptor's advantage lies in the number of queries, which dominates the preparation's gate count (\Cref{tab:headline}). The standard form's degrees at $n=8$ are resource estimates far beyond the $2^{20}$ that exact emulation reaches (\Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}), a ceiling of the classical emulator that the circuit does not share; the descriptor's degrees lie within it and agree with exact emulation through $n=8$.

Measured, the standard form's cost divides as \Cref{sec:standard} argued. With Wu et al.'s encoding most of it is the encoder's, an exact encoding removes that part, and what remains, $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ in the degree for constant coefficients on one axis, is the Hamiltonian's. The descriptor removes the Hamiltonian's part on regular problems; on singular ones its degree still grows as $N^2$, and on R3 at the ideal bound the standard form's is the lower.

\subsection{The Descriptor's Gap and Its Rescaling}
\label{sec:results-gap}

\Cref{sec:descriptor-gap} traced the descriptor's gap to the derivatives it carries: the gap shrinks where the solution's derivatives are large and closes with $N$ on singular problems and on two axes, and block rescaling restores it on one axis only. \Cref{fig:gap-rescaling} measures this on every benchmark problem with a trusted reference. Unscaled, the gap on the physical subspace at $n=8$ is $\res[3]{build/R2a/descriptor/n8/unscaled}{gap}$ on R2a but $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n8/unscaled}{gap}$ on the stiff R2c and $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n8/unscaled}{gap}$ on the singular R3a, and the field block holds as little as $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n8/unscaled}{field_weight}$ of the ground state on R2c. Rescaled, the gaps are $\res[3]{build/R2a/descriptor/n8}{gap}$, $\res[3]{build/R2c/descriptor/n8}{gap}$ and $\res[3]{build/R3a/descriptor/n8}{gap}$, and the field block's weights $\res[3]{build/R2a/descriptor/n8}{field_weight}$, $\res[3]{build/R2c/descriptor/n8}{field_weight}$ and $\res[3]{build/R3a/descriptor/n8}{field_weight}$. The rescaling raises the norm as well, so the degree gains less than the gap does. At the ideal bound the degree scales as the inverse square root of the relative gap $\Delta/\|\Hdesc\|$ (\Cref{eq:minimax-degree}), which rescaling raises from $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n8/unscaled}{relative_gap}$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n8}{relative_gap}$ on R2c and from $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n8/unscaled}{relative_gap}$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n8}{relative_gap}$ on R3a, lowering the ideal-bound degree by about an order of magnitude on R2c and by nearly two on R3a; the as-built degree of an unscaled encoding was not estimated. Every reference here is trusted in both versions, so the rescaling changes the filter's work and leaves the prepared field as it was. It adds no ancilla, though it lengthens each query's Pauli expansion by at most a factor that the equation fixes, whatever the resolution (\Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}). Since the overlap obeys $\gamma\le w_f^{1/2}$ (\Cref{sec:pipeline-filter}), the field weights are also ceilings on $\gamma^2$, and how near the geometric state comes to its ceiling depends on the guess: on R4b at $n=8$, $w_f=\res[3]{build/R4b/descriptor/n8}{field_weight}$ and $\gamma^2=\res[2]{stateprep/R4b/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ (exact emulation).

R2c, $f''+5f'+400f=0$, has the smallest unscaled gap and field weight of the regular problems, and gains the most from rescaling among them. Rescaled, the descriptor prepares it from $n=5$, where its reference is first trusted, with infidelity $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2c/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{infidelity}$ at $n=8$ by exact emulation, and at $n=8$ its degree is $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2c/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$ against the standard form's $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2c/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$ as built and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2c/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$ against $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2c/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$ at the ideal bound (resource estimates). The same rescaling explains R2c's low as-built exponent in \Cref{tab:growth}. Its gap is unchanged between $n=7$ and $8$, at $\res[3]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7}{gap}$ and $\res[3]{build/R2c/descriptor/n8}{gap}$, so the as-built and ideal-bound exponents differ only through $\alpha_R$ against $\|R\|_2$. The subnormalisation is the rescaled recurrence's $2\zeta(N-1)$, with $\zeta=40$ from the rule of \Cref{eq:zeta-rule}, plus a part independent of $N$ that is still the share $\res[2]{alpha-split/R2c/descriptor/n8}{constant_share}$ of $\alpha_R$ at $n=8$, while the norm $\|R\|_2=\res[3]{stateprep/R2c/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{norm_R}$ there is already close to $\zeta\|\tilde D\|_2=2\zeta(N-1)$. We expect the as-built exponent to approach one only once $2\zeta(N-1)$ overtakes the constant, beyond the resolutions estimated. The rule's two constants were set once on these problems, so its cost against the cheapest $\zeta$ on a grid, tabulated in \Cref{app:block-rescaling}, is an in-sample check; the other problems, in both forms and at every tier, are given in \Cref{app:catalogue-results}.

On two axes the gap still falls. The Laplace equation's rescaled gap falls from $\res[3]{build/R5a/descriptor/n3x3}{gap}$ at $n=3$ per axis, where its reference is first trusted, to $\res[2,sci]{build/R5a/descriptor/n5x5}{gap}$ at $n=5$, against $\res[2,sci]{build/R5a/descriptor/n3x3/unscaled}{gap}$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R5a/descriptor/n5x5/unscaled}{gap}$ unscaled, and at $n=5$ per axis the heat and wave equations' rescaled gaps are $\res[2,sci]{build/R5b/descriptor/n5x5}{gap}$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R5c/descriptor/n5x5}{gap}$. Rescaling slows the fall by orders of magnitude without stopping it, consistent with the two-axis degrees of \Cref{tab:growth} growing faster than $N^2$ per axis. On one axis, by contrast, it restores the gap on every problem run.

\subsection{Systems and Nonlinear Problems}
\label{sec:results-nonlinear}

\Cref{sec:extensions} claims that a Carleman lift is prepared where the doubled space only projects, and that the descriptor's lower degree survives on the lifted systems, where it enters through the time axis alone (\Cref{sec:prep-vs-proj}). The coupled oscillator R7 comes first, because every lift is a coupled linear system of its kind.

The harmonic oscillator R7, $u'=v$, $v'=-\pi^2u$, is the realification of $z'=-\mathrm{i}\pi z$ (\Cref{sec:systems-complex}), carried as two fields on one state with its data as ratio rows. Both forms converge to rounding, the descriptor's field error $\res[2,sci]{build/R7/descriptor/n5}{field_err_linf}$ at $n=5$, and both prepare it: from $n=4$ the data-weighted geometric state has $\gamma^2=\res[3]{stateprep/R7/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ in the descriptor and $\res[3]{stateprep/R7/standard/n7/T2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ in the standard form, each at the largest $n$ it emulates, and every trusted exact emulation meets $\varepsilon$. The descriptor carries the system in its mass form, whose gap is about twice the block-rescaled chain's (\Cref{sec:systems-fields}) and which keeps the whole ground state on the field blocks; in the chain the derivative blocks hold part of it, leaving $w_f=\res[3]{build/R7/descriptor/n8/layout=chain/chain}{field_weight}$. The mass form's degree grows as $N$ against the standard form's $N^2$ (\Cref{tab:catalogue-growth}), and at $n=8$ it lies far below the standard form's in both comparisons (\Cref{tab:time-axis}). Simulated at $n=2$ on $\res{stateprep/R7/descriptor/n2/T1/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits, where the reference is not yet trusted, the circuit agrees with the emulation to $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R7/descriptor/n2/T1/filter=minimax}{filtered_agreement}$, which tests the circuit and prepares nothing. What is prepared is the realified real system, at the cost of one field-register qubit; no complex residual was prepared (\Cref{sec:systems-complex}).

The doubled space's kernel on R6a has dimension $\res{build/R6a/standard/n2}{kernel_count}$ at $n=2$ and $\res{build/R6a/standard/n5}{kernel_count}$ at $n=5$, in state spaces of $\res{build/R6a/standard/n2}{dimension}$ and $\res{build/R6a/standard/n5}{dimension}$ (\Cref{tab:doubled-kernel}), so a filter returns the initial state's projection onto it (\Cref{prop:no-penalty}), never a preparation. The Carleman lift of $y'=-y+y^2$ (R8) has a one-dimensional, trusted kernel at every order from $K=2$ to $6$ in both forms, so the filter prepares it, and its field's error splits as \Cref{eq:carleman-error} predicts. At each order the error is flat in $n_t$ from $n_t=3$, the time axis's part having fallen below the lift's, and it falls with the order, from $\res[2,sci]{build/R8/descriptor/n8/carleman=2}{field_err_linf}$ at $K=2$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R8/descriptor/n8/carleman=6}{field_err_linf}$ at $K=6$, faster than the factor $\rho=\tfrac12$ per order that the bound of \Cref{sec:carleman} guarantees. Both forms prepare every order to $\varepsilon$, with infidelity $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R8/descriptor/n8/T2/carleman=4/filter=minimax}{infidelity}$ in the descriptor and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R8/standard/n8/T2/carleman=4/filter=minimax}{infidelity}$ in the standard form at $K=4$ and $n_t=8$. The descriptor's time axis lowers the degree by a margin that widens with $n_t$, as built and at the ideal bound (\Cref{tab:time-axis}).

As on R8, the descriptor's time axis lowers the degree once $n_t$ grows. From $n_t=4$ it is the lower on every Burgers case estimated, $\res{time-axis/R9/n4}{lower}$ of $\res{time-axis/R9/n4}{pairs}$ at $n_t=4$ and $\res{time-axis/R9/n5}{lower}$ of $\res{time-axis/R9/n5}{pairs}$ at $n_t=5$, as built and at the ideal bound. At $n_t=3$ it is the lower on only $\res{time-axis/R9/n3}{lower}$ of $\res{time-axis/R9/n3}{pairs}$ as built and $\res{time-axis/R9/n3}{ideal_lower}$ of $\res{time-axis/R9/n3}{ideal_pairs}$ at the ideal bound; as built, the standard form wins at $a/\nu=10$ from $K=3$ on four modes and at every order on eight (\Cref{tab:time-axis}). The data-weighted initial state's overlap falls with the Carleman order (exact emulation). On R8 it settles, $\gamma^2$ going from $\res[3]{stateprep/R8/descriptor/n8/T2/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ at $K=2$ to $\res[3]{stateprep/R8/descriptor/n8/T2/carleman=6/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ at $K=6$; on Burgers at $a/\nu=1$ it falls from $\res[3]{stateprep/R9c-M4/descriptor/n5/T2/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ at $K=2$ to $\res[3]{stateprep/R9c-M4/descriptor/n5/T2/carleman=5/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ at $K=5$; and at $a/\nu=10$, where the lift diverges, it moves erratically with $K$, reaching the least value over every lift prepared in either form, $\res[2]{overlap/carleman/T2}{gamma2_min}$ at $K=\res{overlap/carleman/T2}{min_carleman}$, against a largest of $\res[2]{overlap/carleman/T2}{gamma2_max}$ across R8, R9 and R10. The amplification $1/\gamma$ of \Cref{eq:total-cost} grows with $K$ accordingly, slowly where the lift converges, and at its worst it is about five.

A Carleman lift is prepared in both forms on every problem run, from R8 to Burgers, where the doubled space only projects. A preparation certifies the truncated lift's linear solution; whether that is the nonlinear solution is measured order by order, and on Burgers the lift fails at $a/\nu=10$ with its kernel still trusted. From $n_t=4$ the descriptor's degree is the lower on every system and lift estimated, as built and at the ideal bound, while on the coarsest time axes the standard form's can be the lower. \Cref{sec:results-ns} tests whether the advantage lasts as the coupling grows with the modes.

\subsection{Two-Dimensional Navier--Stokes}
\label{sec:results-ns}

Navier--Stokes tests whether both results of \Cref{sec:results-nonlinear}, the descriptor's lower degree and a trusted kernel that can hide a diverging lift, survive when the coupling grows with the modes. \Cref{fig:navier-stokes} carries the lift to two-dimensional incompressible flow, the vorticity equation of \Cref{eq:navier-stokes} with $\nu=1$, prepared by exact emulation on $12$ to $48$ Fourier modes and built classically to $80$. The Taylor--Green vortex (R10a), whose two modes do not interact, has an exact lift, and the pipeline returns it to rounding: the lift's error is $\res[2,sci]{build/R10a-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ on twelve modes and $\res[2,sci]{build/R10a-M80/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ on eighty. This checks the Galerkin coefficients, the ratio rows on the stated data and the time axis against a known answer; the lift's convergence is tested on the vortex pair.

A counter-rotating vortex pair of norm $\|w_0\|=a$ excites modes of several $|\mathbf k|$, so its triads are active. On twelve modes, where the lift is built to $K=4$ or $5$, its error is set by the strength, as on Burgers: at $a/\nu=10$ it falls from $\res[2,sci]{build/R10c-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=2$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R10c-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=5}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=5$, and at $a/\nu=30$ from $\res[3]{build/R10d-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ to $\res[3]{build/R10d-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=4}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=4$. Unlike the Burgers lift's, the error also grows with the modes: at $a/\nu=10$ and $K=2$ it is $\res[2]{build/R10c-M48/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ on $48$ modes against $\res[2]{build/R10c-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ on twelve. On $28$ and $48$ modes only $K=2$ and $3$ were built, so each verdict there rests on a single ratio of errors. At $a/\nu=10$ the ratio is below one on $48$ modes, the error falling to $\res[2]{build/R10c-M48/descriptor/n4/carleman=3}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=3$ at a $\rho$ of $\res[2]{panel/R10c-M48}{rho}$. At $a/\nu=30$ it exceeds one from $28$ modes, where the error grows from $\res[3]{build/R10d-M28/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ to $\res[3]{build/R10d-M28/descriptor/n4/carleman=3}{lift_err_linf}$ against $\|w_0\|=30$, and on $48$ (\Cref{fig:navier-stokes}), with every kernel trusted, so trust misses the divergence on a flow as it did on Burgers. A shorter horizon, $T=\tfrac18$, brings the $28$-mode error down from $\res[3]{build/R10d-M28-W8/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=2$ to $\res[3]{build/R10d-M28-W8/descriptor/n4/carleman=3}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=3$, as the lift's time-dependent radius of convergence leads one to expect \cite{forets2017carleman}, though the error remains a sizeable fraction of $\|w_0\|$.

The Galerkin truncation has an error of its own against a direct simulation, relative to the field's largest value: $\res[1,sci]{panel/R10b-M12}{truncation_err}$, $\res[1,sci]{panel/R10c-M12}{truncation_err}$ and $\res[2]{panel/R10d-M12}{truncation_err}$ on twelve modes at $a/\nu=3$, $10$ and $30$. At $a/\nu=30$ it is still $\res[2]{panel/R10d-M48}{truncation_err}$ on $48$ modes, and from $28$ modes the lift diverges, so at that strength no truncation run gives the flow over $T=1$ to better than several per cent. The preparations themselves are sound: every trusted preparation by exact emulation meets $\varepsilon$, the worst at infidelity $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R10d-M12-W8/descriptor/n4/T2/carleman=3/filter=minimax}{infidelity}$.

At one Carleman order the two forms differ only in the time axis (\Cref{sec:prep-vs-proj}), and the descriptor's degree is the lower in all $\res{time-axis/R10/n4}{pairs}$ pairs estimated at $n_t=4$ and all $\res{time-axis/R10/n5}{pairs}$ at $n_t=5$, on $12$ to $48$ modes at $K=2$ to $4$, as built and at the ideal bound. A qubit of $n_t$ multiplies the standard form's degree by about four and the descriptor's by less than two, as on twelve modes at $a/\nu=10$ and $K=2$ (\Cref{tab:time-axis}). At fixed $n_t$ the margin narrows as the modes grow, because the coupling's part of the norm is common to both forms (\Cref{sec:prep-vs-proj}) and the descriptor's encoding of it loosens with its matchings: from twelve modes to $48$ its looseness $\alpha_R/\|R\|_2$ grows from $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R10c-M12/descriptor/n4/T3/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ to $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R10c-M48/descriptor/n4/T3/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$. The margin narrows at the ideal bound too, so part of the narrowing is the Hamiltonian's and part the encoder's. The estimated success probability of the preparation, $p_G$, stays between $\res[2]{overlap/R10/T3}{p_min}$ and $\res[2]{overlap/R10/T3}{p_max}$ across $K=2$ to $4$ and $12$ to $48$ modes, falling slowly with both, and the number of amplification rounds never exceeds $\res{overlap/R10/T3}{max_rounds}$ (resource estimates), so the overlap does not limit the vortex pair's preparation at these sizes; nothing is claimed beyond them.

No circuit was simulated on Navier--Stokes. The narrowest prepared circuit, the descriptor at $n_t=4$ on twelve modes, has $\res{stateprep/R10a-M12/descriptor/n4/T3/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits, wider than any circuit simulated here, and the probe-verified reflections at $n_t=2$ were not prepared. The fluid results are exact emulations, which equal the circuit's output (\Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}), and resource estimates. The tensor layout's coupling, the one native complex encoding built, keeps $2K-1$ terms whatever the number of modes and is verified by \gls{ir} to $\res{lift/R10c-M316/standard/n0/carleman=2/lift=tensor/verify=ir}{modes}$ modes, but prepares no state (\Cref{sec:systems-complex}); its gates per query against the matchings' are given in \Cref{tab:lift-circuits}.
