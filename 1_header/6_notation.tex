A quick reference for symbols used across the thesis; each is properly
introduced at its first use in the text below.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Typeface.} Matrices are italic. Calligraphic letters are the maps that change the register, the lifts $\M$ and the folds $\nfold$, and also $\mathcal{T}$ (Gram map), $\mathcal{H}$ (Hilbert space) and $\mathcal{O}$ (order symbol). The constraint rows $\Brow$, $\Dzero$, $\Slice$ and $\Frat$ are blackboard bold; otherwise blackboard bold is kept for $\mathbb{R}$, $\mathbb{C}$ and the expectation $\mathbb{E}$. Wu et al.\ print every non-unitary matrix in blackboard bold (\Cref{sec:pihm-residual}). The subscripts $\mathrm{std}$ and $\mathrm{desc}$ name the two forms (\Cref{sec:standard-operators}); bare $A$, $H$ and $R$ appear only in statements true of both forms and in \Cref{sec:pihm}, where they are Wu et al.'s own.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Classes of problem.} A linear problem on one axis is \emph{regular} when its coefficients are polynomials, its leading coefficient has no zero on $[-1,1]$, its data are analytic and its solution is unique up to scale, and \emph{singular} when its leading coefficient has a zero there (\Cref{def:regular}). Every degree, gap and subnormalisation claim of the descriptor names its class.
