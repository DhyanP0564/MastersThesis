§.§ Overview

This chapter reports the physics-informed effective Hamiltonian method of Wu, Paine, Philip, Gentile and Kyriienko <cit.> as published, and then judges it; this thesis's own reproduction of the method is reported in <Ref>. The method casts the solution of a differential equation as the ground state of an effective Hamiltonian. It rests on three ingredients. The unknown function is represented by a vector of Chebyshev coefficients, which becomes a quantum state. The differential equation and every boundary or initial condition become linear operators on that vector, each annihilating the coefficients of a true solution. The operators are combined by least squares into a positive semi-definite Hamiltonian, whose ground state is the coefficient vector of the solution. To solve the equation is then to block-encode the Hamiltonian and prepare its ground state, and the field is recovered from the coefficients through the Chebyshev feature map. The lineage runs from differentiable quantum circuits <cit.> through the Chebyshev latent space <cit.> to the effective Hamiltonian. <Ref> judges the method and poses the three questions that <Ref> answer.
