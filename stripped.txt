Differential equations govern how dynamic systems evolve in broad fields of physics and engineering. For a classical numerical solver, the cost grows with the resolution:  unknowns along  axes makes  unknowns in all. A register of  qubits holds a vector of  amplitudes, and this exponential compression is the reason quantum algorithms for differential equations are proposed <cit.>. Such algorithms return a quantum state, from which we measure only a few properties of the solution <cit.>. Cost is primarily determined by asymptotic scaling in circuit depth, gate complexity, qubit count, and/or shots.

Each established route has its own limitation. For quantum linear-system algorithms applied to a discretised equation <cit.>, the cost depends on the condition number of the discretisation, which grows with the resolution. Hamiltonian simulation and its extensions to dissipative dynamics <cit.> solve only linear systems, and the generator's norm times the evolution time also grows with the resolution. A nonlinear equation reaches these linear routes through a linearisation such as Carleman's, whose truncation is controlled only where dissipation dominates <cit.>. Variational circuits trained on the equation's residual <cit.> can solve nonlinear equations, but their training can stall on a barren plateau, where the cost landscape is flat and resolving a descent direction takes exponentially many measurements <cit.>. The remaining route prepares the solution as the ground state of a Hamiltonian built from the equation, with a filter and nothing to train, at a cost set by that Hamiltonian's norm against its gap <cit.>. This thesis takes up that route, following the pihm of Wu, Paine, Philip, Gentile and Kyriienko <cit.>. We compare quantum formulations with one another and leave the quantum advantage claim open.

 §.§ Contributions and Scope

The principal contribution is a reformulation of the residual which the Hamiltonian is built from, which we call the descriptor form. Chebyshev differentiation is dense, but it factorises into two banded matrices which are (up to a factor of two) the conversion and differentiation operators of Olver and Townsend's ultraspherical spectral method <cit.>. We were motivated by the query count, which is set by the Hamiltonian's norm against its gap, and by the observation that forming the dense product inflates the norm. The descriptor form therefore uses the two factors, and carries intermediate derivatives as unknowns, the idea of fosls <cit.>. The reformulation doesn't change the kernel of the Hamiltonian, so the state prepared is the same solution, but its Hamiltonian is banded with a far smaller norm. We also show that the filter needs fewer queries in many cases, and perform a cost-benefit analysis.

We measure the reformulation's gains against a reconstruction of pihm. We first restate their construction as the standard form, and then reconstruct their differentiation operator , the Gram structure, and extension to nonlinear problems. The paper provides an approximate encoding of , but we present an exact structured encoding whose cost is constant for constant coefficients. The encoding serves the comparison and is not offered as a competing solver. One classically verified preparation pipeline serves both forms, so that a difference measured between them is a difference between their residuals.

Both forms are extended to coupled first-order systems, whose fields share one state, and to complex-valued linear equations, by realification into coupled real ones. Nonlinear equations were the harder test, since Wu et al.'s doubled space encodes them exactly but only projects onto a degenerate solution space. We also expolore using Carleman linearisation for nonlinear problems. The culmination of these extensions allows us to reache two-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes, although a one-dimensional kernel certifies the lifted linear problem and not the lift's convergence, which we measure separately.

Every result in the thesis is a classical computation, and nothing ran on quantum hardware. The evidence comes from three methods, named wherever a number is quoted: circuit simulation, exact emulation, which is provably equal to the circuit's post-selected output, and resource estimates beyond them. Readout is a research field of its own and lies outside the scope of this work. Therefore we decode the field from the prepared state classically.

The work departs from its research proposal, which intended an extension of the pihm to complex-valued nonlinear equations, including the nonlinear Schrödinger and complex Ginzburg–Landau equations, through a generalised qsvt. Reproducing Wu et al.'s examples showed that the growth of the query count, which their Discussion leaves open, is set by the dense differentiation matrix. Controlling it matters more than extending the method, since any extension would inherit the cost. The thesis therefore addresses the growth of the query count first. The generalised qsvt also proved unnecessary, since it offers no advantage over gqsp when the operator to be filtered is Hermitian, which our Hamiltonian is. gqsp was therefore enough, and by removing the parity constraint it replaces the mixed-parity lcu that Wu et al. use for their filter.
