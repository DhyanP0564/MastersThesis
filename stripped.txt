This chapter presents and evaluates the physics-informed effective Hamiltonian method of Wu, Paine, Philip, Gentile and Kyriienko <cit.>. <Ref> outline the paper's methodology. We provide our critical assessment in <Ref>, and report our reproduction of results in <Ref>.

The method turns a differential equation into a ground-state problem. The unknown function is expanded in Chebyshev polynomials and its coefficient vector is stored as the amplitudes of an -qubit state , so that the function's value at any point is an overlap of  with a known feature state. The differential equation and each of its boundary or initial conditions are then written as matrix operators that annihilate  when it holds the solution's coefficients. The paper's introductory example  becomes , where  is the matrix that differentiates a Chebyshev series. Finally, a positive semi-definite matrix operator , which we label as the effective Hamiltonian, is created from summing the individual gram matrices of all operators. The ground state of the Hamiltonian is the solution. With this construction, solving the equation becomes preparing that ground state, which needs a block encoding of  and a filter that isolates its lowest eigenvector, after which the field is read back through the same feature map. The construction descends from differentiable quantum circuits <cit.> through their Chebyshev latent space <cit.>.
