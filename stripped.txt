This chapter presents the physics-informed effective Hamiltonian method of Wu, Paine, Philip, Gentile and Kyriienko <cit.> and evaluates it. The method turns a differential equation into a ground-state problem in three steps. The unknown function is expanded in Chebyshev polynomials, and its coefficients are stored as the amplitudes of an -qubit state . The equation and each of its boundary or data conditions are then written as matrix operators that annihilate  when it holds the solution's coefficients: the paper's introductory example  becomes , where  differentiates a Chebyshev series. Finally, the Gram matrices of these operators are summed into a positive semi-definite effective Hamiltonian  whose ground state is the solution. Solving the equation thereby becomes preparing a ground state, which needs a block encoding of , a filter that removes its excited spectrum, and a measurement that reads the field back through the same Chebyshev feature map. The construction descends from differentiable quantum circuits <cit.> through their Chebyshev latent space <cit.>.

The paper's case for the method is that it needs no variational training, that its solution can be evaluated at any point of the domain rather than on a grid, and that “necessary terms have  scaling and can be implemented efficiently” <cit.>. <Ref> present the model, the Hamiltonian, the circuits and the nonlinear extension in the paper's own terms and equations, and <Ref> assesses them. The construction is stated in full, in the form this thesis runs, in <Ref>.
