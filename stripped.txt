The answer it develops is that ground-state preparation of a differential equation costs what the conditioning of its residual costs, and that the formulation, rather than the circuit, decides that conditioning.

 §.§ Limitations

Every result is a classical computation, and nothing ran on hardware. The claims are cost laws of operators and filters, which classical computation tests exactly. It cannot test noise, and the method's robustness to it is unknown. Exact emulation stands in for the circuit beyond  qubits by an argument that the agreement of the two confirms only where both run, the widest reflections, of the two-axis descriptor problems and the larger lifts, are built and counted but not verified whole, and Navier–Stokes is reached by emulation and estimate alone (<Ref>). The growth exponents are two-point slopes between stated resolutions, consistent with the laws rather than fits of them, and the two-axis slopes, between  and  per axis, are the least settled. Where  must grow with the problem, as on a stiff equation, the dependence of the gap's constant on the stiffness was measured on R2c alone. Beyond  physical columns the reference is iterative, and the error bound it reports is computed from the residual of the returned vector rather than proved, so it is reliable only if the iteration has converged and no singular value below  has been missed (<Ref>).

The descriptor's scope ends where its gap does. The gap is proved flat only on regular problems, measured flat after rescaling on singular ones with a simple zero, and falls on two axes. A double zero needs the substitution of <Ref>, whose form presumes that the zero's order is known from the equation, and mixed derivatives have no block and are not supported (<Ref>). The full ultraspherical reduction, a banded residual on  unknowns with no block register, would remove the inverse without carrying any derivative, but it was neither built nor compared (<Ref>). Its differentiation operator is a single diagonal whose norm grows linearly in  at every order, and for a leading coefficient that does not vanish, as many boundary conditions as the order and a uniquely solvable problem, Olver and Townsend bound its condition number after a diagonal preconditioner, in the 2-norm for Dirichlet conditions, so that without one it grows linearly with the truncation <cit.>. On a regular problem this suggests that the reduction would reach the descriptor's  degree on  unknowns, without the block register, and the ultraspherical descriptor of <Ref>, which keeps a flat gap on regular problems, is consistent with that. The expectation is untested. The bound concerns a square system with conditions at the ends of the interval rather than a least-squares residual whose rows the conversions weight (<Ref>), and the reduction's encoding with variable coefficients and its gap on singular problems and on two axes were not measured.

Of the extensions, only first-order systems were run. Complex-valued fields are carried by realification alone; no complex residual was prepared. The tensor layout's native complex encoding is verified but prepares nothing (<Ref>). The thesis presents no test of a lift's divergence that reads the residual alone (<Ref>). Wu et al.'s partial differential and nonlinear examples, their Figs 6 and 7, were not reproduced under any hypothesis tested, and the authors were not contacted for clarification (<Ref>).
