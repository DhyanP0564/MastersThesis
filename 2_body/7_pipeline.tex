\Cref{sec:standard,sec:descriptor,sec:extensions} each end with a residual and a prediction: the filter's degree grows as $\sqrt{\alpha_H/\Delta}$ (\Cref{eq:degree}), with a norm and a gap that each chapter derived for its own form. \emph{Can both terms of that ratio be measured the same way for both forms?} Only if each form is judged against the same classical reference, filtered to the same suppression from the same answer-independent initial state, and costed with the encoder's share of $\alpha_H$ kept apart from the Hamiltonian's. One pipeline therefore serves every residual (\Cref{fig:pipeline}). The two forms enter only through the residual they hand to it, so a difference measured between them in \Cref{sec:results} is a difference between their Hamiltonians $H=R^\dagger R$ or between their encodings, which the design of the experiment separates (\Cref{sec:pipeline-design}). The statements hold for any stacked residual, so $R$, $H$ and $\alpha_R$ carry no form subscript here.

\subsection{Problems and Model Inputs}
\label{sec:pipeline-inputs}

%   StructuralReview.md F7); the constraint kinds named (def:standard-form),

A problem is data: its fields and axes; its equation, as a sum of terms, each a coefficient function, a derivative order on an axis, a field and possibly a source; its constraint rows; its regular datum; and its reference solution. Nothing downstream depends on a problem except through these. The benchmark problems R1--R10 of \Cref{tab:ladder} are given in this way, each with its equation, reference, constraint rows, resolutions and parameters in \Cref{app:catalogue}; the panels as printed are run only to test the published method (\Cref{app:reproduction}). The data enter as rows of the four kinds of \Cref{def:standard-form}, the model's boundary and initial conditions, each the same in both forms and listed with the problems that use them in \Cref{tab:constraint-kinds}. Two kinds of datum are set up from the reference solution when the problem is built, for the reasons of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints}: the exact constraint points of R4 and R6b, and the heat equation's initial function at $t=-1$. Every other datum is the problem's statement.


A run is a problem at a resolution with a set of options: the form and, for the descriptor, its layout and block rescaling; the encoding of $\G$; the treatment of a nonlinearity; the filter; the target $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$; and the initial state. Their values and defaults are listed in \Cref{tab:run-inputs} (\Cref{app:run-options}). The defaults are the configuration every comparison of \Cref{sec:results} uses; the alternatives are controls, labelled ablations, or the printed choices kept to test the published method.

\subsection{Classical Reference}
\label{sec:pipeline-reference}
% ~0.5 pp. LAND: the smallest right singular vector; Wedin's bound against the eigensolver's (the
%   bounds and the table -> app:eigh-vs-svd; R2a's relative gap is sec:descriptor-spectrum's); the
%   iterative reference beyond 8,192 physical columns (its reach -> app:iterative-reference); trust
%   at gap ratio 20 (the placement rule and the angle bound -> app:trust-threshold); three metric
%   levels.
% NOTE (standing fact): the code trusts a gap ratio of AT LEAST 20 (classical.reference,
%   DEFAULT_MIN_GAP_RATIO); CLAUDE.md says "above 20" (plan §2.2 TODO).

Every result is judged against a classical reference: the ground state of $H$, computed from $R$ and never from $H$, a departure from Wu et al.'s eigenvector of the formed Hamiltonian (\Cref{tab:deviations}). With the \gls{svd} $R=\sum_j\sigma_j\,u_jv_j^\dagger$, $\sigma_0\le\sigma_1\le\dots\le\sigma_{\max}$, the reference is $\ket{\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}}=v_0=\operatorname{arg\,min}_{\|\psi\|=1}\|R\psi\|_2$, the eigenvalues of $H$ are $\lambda_j=\sigma_j^2$ and its gap is $\Delta=\sigma_1^2-\sigma_0^2$. Working from $R$ keeps its conditioning unsquared. A backward-stable \gls{svd} of $R$ resolves the ground state to about $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\sigma_{\max}/(\sigma_1-\sigma_0)$ by Wedin's theorem \cite{wedin1972perturbation}, an eigensolver on the formed $H$ only to about $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}\sigma_{\max}^2/(\sigma_1^2-\sigma_0^2)$ by the Davis--Kahan theorem \cite{daviskahan1970rotation}, larger by about the residual's effective condition number $\sigma_{\max}/\sigma_1$ and above one once the relative gap $\Delta/\|H\|$ falls below $\epsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}$, as the standard form's does at moderate $n$ (\Cref{sec:descriptor-spectrum,prop:wedin,app:eigh-vs-svd}). The dense \gls{svd} is used up to $8{,}192$ physical columns, the descriptor's decoupled padding excluded (\Cref{prop:padding-decouples}); beyond, shift-invert Lanczos iteration on an augmented system that never forms $R^\dagger R$ gives the smallest singular triplets, with an a posteriori bound on the ground state's error (\Cref{app:iterative-reference}).

A computed spectrum always has a smallest value, and the reference must decide whether it is a kernel. The kernel is placed at the largest ratio $\sigma_k/\sigma_{k-1}$ among the lowest singular values, and the reference is trusted (\Cref{sec:standard-constraints}) when that kernel is one-dimensional with gap ratio at least $20$, an empirical threshold set above every ordinary spacing in the benchmark problems' spectra (\Cref{app:trust-threshold}). Trust detects a kernel; the field's convergence is measured separately. A preparation is judged only where its reference is trusted, at three levels: the operator (each encoding against the assembled operator, \Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}), the state (the infidelity $1-|\braket{\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}|\psi}|^2$ in the form's own space) and the field (the $L^\infty$ and relative $L^2$ errors of the decoded field against the reference solution on a uniform grid, \Cref{sec:pipeline-readout}).

\subsection{Block Encoding and the Quantum Walk}
\label{sec:pipeline-walk}
% ~0.75 pp. LAND: the stacked encoding (eq:stacked-encoding), a quadrature combination; the
%   proposition (C-03; the construction credited to qubitisation/QSVT), proof ->
%   app:proof-stacked-reflection; the walk (eq:walk); the spectrum on [-1, 1] and the gap divided by
%   alpha_R; controlled V from controlled reflections.
% CUT 2026-10-13 (review 2): the hedged comparison with a Gram-term combination (App. I does not
%   record Wu et al.'s); the padding sentence (sec:pipeline-reference and App. D carry it).

Each block $R_j$ of the residual, the equation's rows and each constraint's, is block-encoded by its form's circuits at a subnormalisation $\alpha_j$ (\Cref{sec:standard-cost,sec:descriptor-block-encoding,sec:ext-circuits}). The blocks are stacked rather than summed: a row-select register of $\lceil\log_2L\rceil$ qubits is prepared in $\sum_j(\alpha_j/\alpha_R)\ket{j}$ and $U_j$ is applied when it holds $j$, and unlike the selector of a \gls{lcu} the register is never returned by an inverse preparation, so that
\begin{equation}
    \bigl(\bra{j}\otimes\bra{0_a}\bigr)\,U_R\,\bigl(\ket{0}\otimes\ket{0_a}\bigr)=\frac{R_j}{\alpha_R},
    \qquad
    \alpha_R=\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^{L}\alpha_j^2\Bigr)^{1/2},
    \label{eq:stacked-encoding}
\end{equation}
with $a$ the blocks' shared ancilla and the row select read as part of the output. The subnormalisations add in quadrature, $\alpha_R=\|(\alpha_j)\|_2$, the form of the bound $\|R\|_2\le(\sum_j\|R_j\|_2^2)^{1/2}$ itself, so a stack of tight blocks is loose only by as much as $\|R\|$ falls below that bound. A reflection then turns the encoding of $R$ into one of $H$: the standard construction of $A^\dagger A$ from a block encoding of $A$, which underlies qubitisation and the \gls{qsvt} \cite{lowchuang2019qubitization,gilyen2019qsvt}, applied here to a stacked residual and verified on the whole Hamiltonian it encodes (\Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}).

\begin{proposition}[Stacked-residual reflection]
\label{prop:stacked-reflection}
Let $U_R$ satisfy \Cref{eq:stacked-encoding}, let $\Pi_a$ project the shared ancilla onto $\ket{0_a}$, and let $\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}$ project the shared ancilla and the row select onto zero. Then
\begin{equation}
    V=U_R^{\dagger}\,\bigl(2\Pi_a-I\bigr)\,U_R
    \quad\text{satisfies}\quad
    V^{\dagger}=V,\quad V^2=I,\quad
    \Pi_{\mathrm{in}}\,V\,\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}=\frac{2H}{\alpha_R^2}-I\equiv H',
    \label{eq:stacked-reflection}
\end{equation}
with $H=R^\dagger R$, and $V$ controlled on further qubits is $U_R^\dagger\,C(2\Pi_a-I)\,U_R$: only the reflection carries the control.
\end{proposition}

The proof is given in \Cref{app:proof-stacked-reflection}. The reflection block-encodes $H'$ at subnormalisation one and $H$ at $\alpha_H=\alpha_R^2$, at the cost of the stack, its inverse and one multi-controlled $Z$ gate. Its spectrum needs no rescaling: $H\succeq0$ and $\|H\|=\|R\|^2\le\alpha_R^2$, so $H'$ has its spectrum in $[-1,1]$, its eigenvectors are the right singular vectors of $R$, and its ground value sits at $x_0=-1+2\sigma_0^2/\alpha_R^2$, at the edge of the interval when the kernel is exact. Its gap is $2\Delta/\alpha_R^2$: the encoding's subnormalisation divides the gap the filter must resolve, which is how the encoder's cost of \Cref{sec:standard} enters the degree. Qubitisation \cite{lowchuang2019qubitization} turns the reflection into a walk whose powers are Chebyshev polynomials of $H'$ (\Cref{app:qubitisation}),
\begin{equation}
    W=\bigl(2\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}-I\bigr)\,V,
    \qquad
    \Pi_{\mathrm{in}}\,W^{k}\,\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}=T_k(H') ,
    \label{eq:walk}
\end{equation}
for one power of $W$ followed by one projection; controlling $W$ controls only its two reflections (\Cref{app:controlled-walk}).

\subsection{Filter and Parameters}
\label{sec:pipeline-filter}

A filter is a polynomial $P$ of $H'$. \Gls{gqsp} \cite{motlagh2024gqsp} applies $d$ walks, each controlled on one signal qubit, between single-qubit rotations of that qubit, with no parity constraint (\Cref{sec:bg-qsp}). On the signal's zero, restricted once by $\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}$, the circuit block-encodes
\begin{equation}
    \Pi_{\mathrm{in}}\,P(W)\,\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}=\sum_{k=0}^{d}q_k\,T_k(H'),
    \qquad
    P(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{d}q_kz^k,
    \qquad
    \sup_{|z|=1}|P(z)|\le1,
    \label{eq:gqsp-block}
\end{equation}
by \Cref{eq:walk}, so a filter given by its Chebyshev coefficients $q_k$ is realised by the analytic polynomial with the same coefficients. The phases are computed from $P$ alone, and the polynomial they realise is checked against $P$ (\Cref{app:gqsp-phases}).

\paragraph{Minimax Edge Filter.} The filter that prepares every comparison and extension is the polynomial of degree $d$ that is smallest on the excited spectrum relative to its value at the ground state: the Chebyshev polynomial of that spectrum, optimal by Chebyshev's inequality (\Cref{sec:bg-filtering}). With $x_1$ the first excited value of $H'$ and $w$ the affine map of $[x_1,1]$ onto $[-1,1]$,
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
        P_d(x)=\frac{T_d\bigl(w(x)\bigr)}{T_d\bigl(w(-1)\bigr)},
        \qquad
        w(x)=\frac{2x-(x_1+1)}{1-x_1},
        \\
        \sup_{x\in[x_1,1]}\frac{|P_d(x)|}{|P_d(x_0)|}=\frac{1}{\cosh(d\theta_0)},
        \qquad
        \theta_0=\operatorname{arccosh}\bigl(-w(x_0)\bigr).
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:minimax-filter}
\end{equation}
Its Chebyshev coefficients alternate in sign and sum in magnitude to one, so \Cref{eq:gqsp-block} realises it at full weight (\Cref{prop:filter-normalisation}); they are computed by exact interpolation, so nothing is truncated. We take the least degree that suppresses the excited spectrum to $\varepsilon$ relative to the ground state,
\begin{equation}
    d=\left\lceil\frac{\operatorname{arccosh}(1/\varepsilon)}{\theta_0}\right\rceil
    \approx\frac{\alpha_R}{2\sqrt\Delta}\,\ln\frac{2}{\varepsilon},
    \label{eq:minimax-degree}
\end{equation}
an instance of \Cref{eq:degree}. The approximation holds for $\sigma_1\ll\alpha_R$, where $x_0$ and $x_1$ round to $-1$ in floating point, so $\theta_0$ is evaluated from the heights $1+x_j=2\sigma_j^2/\alpha_R^2$ above the edge (\Cref{app:minimax-filter}). For an initial state of overlap $\gamma$ with a one-dimensional target, the prepared state's infidelity is at most $(1-\gamma^2)\varepsilon^2/\gamma^2$ (\Cref{prop:minimax-guarantee}).

\paragraph{A Priori Parameters.} The degree needs $\varepsilon$, the circuit's own $\alpha_R$ and the spectrum's edge, $\sigma_0$ and $\sigma_1$ from the classical reference; it never reads the solution. The edge is assumed known, the standard assumption of filtering methods (\Cref{sec:bg-filtering}), and is given exactly to both forms; finding it on the circuit, or replacing it by a lower bound on the gap at a degree proportional to the bound's inverse square root, is beyond our scope (\Cref{sec:limitations}). Wu et al.'s time rule, \Cref{eq:pihm-time-rule}, contains no $\varepsilon$ and falls short of it (\Cref{tab:deviations}); it is kept only as a tested variant (\Cref{app:paper-claims}).

\paragraph{Imaginary Time.} Wu et al.'s filter, imaginary time $e^{-\beta(1+x)}$ in the variable of $H'$, has modified Bessel functions for its Chebyshev coefficients, which also sum in magnitude to one, and a degree with the same factor $\alpha_R/\sqrt\Delta$, larger than the minimax filter's by a constant factor at equal suppression (\Cref{app:qite-filter,prop:filter-normalisation}). It runs only in the filter study, which measures that factor in both forms (\Cref{sec:results-faithful}), and in the reproduction of Wu et al.\ (\Cref{app:reproduction}).

\paragraph{Initial State.} The filter acts on the geometric product state,
\begin{equation}
    \ket{\psi_{\mathrm{init}}}\propto\bigotimes_{j=0}^{n-1}\Bigl(\ket{0}+r^{2^j}\ket{1}\Bigr)=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}r^k\ket{k},
    \qquad
    r=\pm\tfrac12,
    \qquad
    \gamma=\bigl|\braket{\psi_{\mathrm{ref}}|\psi_{\mathrm{init}}}\bigr| ,
    \label{eq:geometric-state}
\end{equation}
on the field block and zero on every other block: $n$ single-qubit rotations and no entangling gate. Its rate and sign pattern guess the geometric decay of a smooth function's Chebyshev coefficients; neither is fitted. Both signs of $r$ are run and the one with the larger measured success probability is kept, a rule that needs only what a quantum computer observes. A system's fields each carry the state weighted by the problem's stated initial data, $w_0/\|w_0\|$ (\Cref{sec:systems-data}). In a Carleman lift the fields are the level-one blocks, so the state sits there, and every higher level starts at zero, as the descriptor's derivative blocks and padding do. The uniform state and a seeded random state are controls, and $\gamma$ is reported for every run. Where the ground state has weight outside the field block, in the descriptor's derivative blocks or a lift's higher levels, $\gamma$ is at most the square root of the field block's weight (\Cref{sec:descriptor-gap}).

\paragraph{Success and Total Cost.} The post-selection succeeds with probability $p_G=\|P_d(H')\psi_{\mathrm{init}}\|^2\ge\gamma^2P_d(x_0)^2$, which tends to $\gamma^2$ as the ground value approaches the edge. Amplitude amplification \cite{brassard2000amplitude} with $\lfloor\pi/(4\arcsin\gamma)\rfloor$ rounds raises it to at least one half, so with $g(W)$ the two-qubit gate count of one walk a preparation costs
\begin{equation}
    \text{queries}=(2k_{\mathrm{aa}}+1)\,d,
    \qquad
    \text{gates}=(2k_{\mathrm{aa}}+1)\,d\;g(W),
    \qquad
    k_{\mathrm{aa}}=\Bigl\lfloor\frac{\pi}{4\arcsin\gamma}\Bigr\rfloor,
    \label{eq:total-cost}
\end{equation}
linear in $1/\gamma$ for small $\gamma$. The count takes $\gamma$ from the classical reference, as the degree takes the edge, and leaves out the one-qubit rotations, the initial state, the amplification's reflections and the second sign of $r$ (\Cref{app:amplification}). The departures of this section from Wu et al.'s filter, phases, normalisation and initial state are collected with the others in \Cref{tab:deviations}.

Two questions are kept apart: whether each circuit encodes the operator it claims to, and whether the filter prepares the ground state. The first, \emph{operator verification}, targets the reflection's whole block $2H/\alpha_R^2-I$, with $H$ formed from the residual assembled from the problem, never from the compiler that produced the circuit, and runs in three modes (\Cref{tab:verification-modes}). The \emph{exact} mode reconstructs the block, one statevector evolution per input column, and certifies the circuit completely at that size. The \emph{probe} mode compares the circuit's action on twenty structured and Haar-random vectors with sparse products of the target; it estimates the root-mean-square error without bounding the worst entry (\Cref{prop:haar-probes}), and covers every circuit of at most 24 qubits that the exact mode does not. Where these two modes reach, the composed Hamiltonian is verified whole. The \emph{\gls{ir}} mode sums the compiled terms of a lift or coupling into a sparse matrix and compares it with the assembled operator at any size; the two sides are independent constructions from the problem, so the comparison is not a tautology, and the exact and probe modes close the step from terms to gates where a circuit can be simulated. Wider circuits are recorded for their subnormalisation and gate counts alone.

The second question is \emph{state-preparation verification}, at three tiers. \emph{Circuit simulation} runs the \gls{gqsp} circuit on a statevector simulator, one controlled walk at a time. \emph{Exact emulation} computes $\sum_kq_kT_k(H')\psi_{\mathrm{init}}$ by the Chebyshev three-term recurrence with $H'v=2R^\dagger(Rv)/\alpha_R^2-v$, two sparse products a step and $H$ never formed, up to degree $2^{20}$. A \emph{resource estimate} takes $\alpha_R$ and the walk's gate count from the circuit, the edge and $\gamma$ from the reference and the degree from \Cref{eq:minimax-degree}, and prepares nothing.

By \Cref{prop:stacked-reflection}, \Cref{eq:walk} and \Cref{eq:gqsp-block}, the circuit's post-selected output is $\sum_kq_kT_k(H')\psi_{\mathrm{init}}$, which depends on the encoding only through $\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}V\Pi_{\mathrm{in}}=H'$; two encodings with the same $H'$ give the same output for every filter. Exact emulation therefore computes the circuit's output exactly, up to rounding, for the circuit's own $\alpha_R$, and circuit simulation tests only that the circuit as built implements it, wherever both run. Each circuit simulation is preceded by an estimate of its memory and time, and a run beyond its budget is recorded with the estimate and not attempted (\Cref{app:t1-model}). Circuit simulation therefore covers small widths only, a limit of the simulation budget and not of the method: a circuit that implements $V$ outputs the emulated state at any size.

Each preparation is accepted against bars at every level (\Cref{tab:acceptance}): the state's infidelity at most $\varepsilon$ and within ten times its a priori bound, the circuit against its emulation within $10^{-10}$ before normalising, and the decoded field within the reference's own field error plus a budget for the prepared state's. The budget splits the state's distance from the reference into the filter's, the truncation's and the realised phases' terms, and carries their sum to the field by an exact readout bound (\Cref{eq:error-budget}). Every encoding is exact and the field is decoded classically, so no block-encoding or sampling term enters.

\subsection{Readout}
\label{sec:pipeline-readout}
% ~0.25 pp (plan §5.7 item 7: ONE PARAGRAPH, T-24). Classical decoding first; Wu et al.'s protocol
%   attributed, built and verified at small n, its correction for p_G < 1 (eq:readout-identity and
%   its derivation -> app:readout-identity), further work.

The field is read out classically: it is decoded from the prepared state's field blocks and scaled by the regular datum. With $\ell_x(\psi)=\braket{\tau(x)|D\psi}$ the field's value functional, $D$ the map from the state to the field's coefficients (the field block; for a lift, its first level times the scale $s$),
\begin{equation}
    f_Q(x)=f(x_s)\,\frac{\ell_x(\psi)}{\ell_{x_s}(\psi)},
    \qquad
    \eta=\Bigl(\frac{f(x_s)}{\ell_{x_s}(\psi)}\Bigr)^{2},
    \label{eq:readout-field}
\end{equation}
so the state's global phase and norm cancel and the decoded field carries the sign of the datum; $\eta$ is the linear model's scale of \Cref{sec:standard-model}. Wu et al.\ read the field out point by point instead, by an interferometric measurement (\Cref{sec:pihm-encoding}) whose identity assumes a deterministic preparation: after post-selection with probability $p_G<1$ the field, a ratio, is unaffected and the scale is wrong by the factor $p_G$ (\Cref{eq:readout-identity}). The protocol is built and verified with this correction on R1, R2a and R5a at small $n$; its study at scale is further work (\Cref{sec:future-work}), not the method's readout.

\subsection{Experimental Design}
\label{sec:pipeline-design}
% ~0.5 pp. LAND: what varies and what is fixed; what counts as a gate and an ancilla; FAIRNESS,
%   DECLARED, with its true bias: as built favours the descriptor (its encoder is the tighter); the
%   ideal bound alpha = ||R|| isolates the Hamiltonian (T-25); the tier on every number.

The study varies the resolution ($n$ per axis, $n_t$, the modes $M$ and the Carleman order $K$), the form, the encoding of $\G$, the filter, the target $\varepsilon$ and the initial state, one at a time from the defaults of \Cref{tab:run-inputs}. Within a comparison of the forms everything else is fixed: the problem and its constraint rows, $\varepsilon=10^{-6}$, the minimax filter, the geometric initial state on the field block and the readout.

\paragraph{Cost Conventions.} A query is one application of the walk $W$; a preparation costs $d$ queries and an amplified one $(2k_{\mathrm{aa}}+1)d$ (\Cref{eq:total-cost}). Gates per query are the two-qubit gates of one walk after transpiling to $\{\mathrm{CX},U\}$ \cite{qiskit2024}, with one-qubit gates counted separately and dense primitives that are cited rather than built, the doubled space's discrete cosine transform, counted apart. Ancilla are counted on the composed reflection $V$: the row select, every block's ancilla and, in the descriptor's chain, nothing for the block register, which is part of the state.

\paragraph{Fair Comparison.} The standard form is costed with its structured encoding, the cheapest exact encoding of it built (\Cref{sec:standard-exact}), which is looser than the descriptor's (\Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian,sec:descriptor-cost}). The degree is linear in $\alpha_R$ (\Cref{eq:degree}), so an as-built comparison favours the descriptor by about the ratio of the two encodings' looseness, a factor of the encodings and not of the Hamiltonians. Every comparison is therefore quoted twice: as built, and at the ideal-encoding bound of \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian}, the only one that compares the Hamiltonians. Where the order of the forms reverses at the ideal bound, the reversal is reported as a finding (\Cref{sec:results-scaling}).

\paragraph{Tiers and Fits.} Every number states its tier. Degrees beyond the emulation's reach are resource estimates: exact for this filter, given the spectrum and the circuit's $\alpha_R$, but with no state prepared, and compared with exact emulation where both exist. Every resource estimate is computed at its own resolution, from that resolution's circuit and reference, and none is extrapolated; the growth of a degree with $N$ is quoted as a measured exponent between stated resolutions.

\subsection{Software and Computation}
\label{sec:pipeline-software}

The pipeline is implemented in \texttt{pihm}, a Python package we wrote (\Cref{app:software}). Each concept has one implementation, shared by both forms and every problem, so a difference between the forms cannot come from different code for the same step, and every number and figure is regenerated from the archived records of one tagged version.

Within this pipeline the forms differ in their residual alone, and a comparison read at the ideal-encoding bound compares their Hamiltonians. Two things rest on assumption or argument: the spectral edge, supplied by the classical reference rather than found on the circuit, and the circuit's output beyond small widths, which the exact emulation supplies by the argument of \Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}. \Cref{sec:results-faithful} tests the pipeline against its reference, its emulation and its error budget before \Cref{sec:results-scaling} measures the degree's two terms in both forms.
