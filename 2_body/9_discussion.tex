The measured degrees of \Cref{sec:results} follow the laws of the formulation chapters within their stated scope and depart from them on singular problems (\Cref{def:regular}), on two axes and on a lift past its boundary. The exponents say how fast the degree grows; they do not say why carrying the derivatives lowers the norm and puts the gap at risk at once, or what a lower degree is worth once gates, amplification and readout are counted. This chapter asks \emph{what mechanism produces the descriptor's advantage and its limits, and what they imply beyond the benchmark problems.}

\subsection{The Descriptor Form: Advantages and Costs}
\label{sec:disc-tradeoff}
% ~1 pp
% LAND: norm, not encoding -- eliminating the derivatives multiplies norms
%   (||G|| = Theta(N^2), squared by the Gram product and again per derivative),
%   while carrying them keeps each block O(N) in norm. The same mechanism that
%   buys the norm weighs the gap (the norm carries every derivative): one idea,
%   two consequences. The price: the block register, the per-query constant,
%   rescaling, and no remedy on two axes or at a double zero. The classical
%   mirror: first-order least squares and the ultraspherical method found the
%   same trade.

The advantage belongs to the operator. With its subnormalisation held within a constant of its norm by the structured encoding, the standard form's degree still grows as $N^{2k}$ (\Cref{tab:growth}), and the exponent can be read off the factorisation $\G=\Bt^{-1}\Dt$ of \Cref{eq:G-banded}. The Gram product $H=R^\dagger R$ doubles the exponent of the residual's norm in both forms, so the difference lies in $\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2=\Theta(N^{2k})$. Of the two powers of $N$ in $\|\G\|_2$, one is $\|\Dt\|_2=2(N-1)$ and the other the inverse's, $\Bt^{-1}$ being a triangle of ones on each parity class; composing $\G$ once per eliminated derivative multiplies the exponent by $k$. Keeping the banded factors avoids the inverse, and carrying each derivative as its own unknown avoids the composition, so each recurrence row applies $\Dt$ and $\Bt$ once and uninverted. No block of the descriptor then exceeds $\mathcal O(N)$ at fixed $\zeta$, whatever the order, and $\|\Hdesc\|=\Theta(N^2)$ (\Cref{sec:descriptor-spectrum}).

The same norms return on the other side of the spectrum. By \Cref{eq:descriptor-quotient} the descriptor measures a state's residual against $\sum_m\|\G^mp\|^2$, the quantities the standard form pays in its norm, so a state whose derivatives are large against its residual has a small quotient. Carrying the derivatives thus relocates the conditioning of differentiation from a norm that grows with $N$ on every problem to a gap that falls only where the equation fails to control a carried derivative. \Cref{thm:flat-gap} states that the control holds on a regular problem, and both ways the gap can fall are failures of it: a leading coefficient that vanishes at an endpoint leaves the top derivative of a state concentrated there unpenalised, and on two axes the equation constrains only a sum of top derivatives (\Cref{sec:descriptor-gap}). The mass form of a first-order equation removes the inverse without carrying a derivative, so its state is measured by the field alone, consistent with its gap exceeding a chain's on R7 (\Cref{sec:systems-fields}). At higher order the chain lowers the norm only by carrying the derivatives, and so only at a risk to the gap.

Classical numerical analysis met the same trade. First-order-system least squares carries the derivatives so as not to square a high-order operator's condition number \cite{cai1994fosls}, and its theory rests on norm equivalence: the functional must bound every carried unknown in its own norm, which for an elliptic problem asks for the boundary residuals in fractional-order norms that practical methods replace by weighted $L^2$ terms.
% CITE-NEEDED: source for the replacement of fractional boundary norms by weighted L2 terms; Bochev-Gunzburger (2009 book, 1998 review) not confirmed to cover it.
\Cref{thm:flat-gap} is a discrete, one-axis instance of the lower half of that equivalence, coercivity, and the collapse on two axes is consistent with its failure. The datum rows of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints} weigh a boundary function equally at every frequency, too weakly to control second derivatives, so a harmonic or diffusive state of high frequency can meet them with a small residual. That reading is inferred from the mechanism of \Cref{sec:descriptor-gap} rather than measured, and it makes datum rows weighted towards high frequencies the first remedy to test (\Cref{sec:future-work}). The ultraspherical method met the trade from the other side: its banded systems are well conditioned once diagonally preconditioned \cite{olver2013}, the role block rescaling takes here.

The rest of the price is a set of factors independent of $N$. The block register multiplies the state by $2^{n_f}$, four blocks at second order and eight for Laplace's and the wave equation (\Cref{sec:app-variable-coeff}). A query applies more terms through that register, so on constant-coefficient problems it costs more two-qubit gates than the structured standard form's at the sizes built and verified (\Cref{sec:results-scaling}), and block rescaling, which adds no ancilla, lengthens the Pauli expansion of the register's patterns by a factor fixed by the equation. These factors decide whether the reformulation pays at small $n$; the exponents decide whether it pays at all, and they depend on the class of problem. On a singular problem the rule's $\zeta\propto N$ raises the descriptor's norm to $\Theta(N^4)$ at second order, and on two axes or at a double zero no block weight restores the gap (\Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling,sec:disc-applicability}).

\subsection{End-to-End Complexity}
\label{sec:disc-complexity}

% LAND, in order:

% CHECKED 2026-10-14: Lin & Tong's lower bounds (their Theorem 8, by unstructured search and

Neither form is polylogarithmic in $N$. The degree of \Cref{eq:degree}, $d=\Theta\bigl(\sqrt{\alpha_H/\Delta}\,\log(1/\varepsilon)\bigr)$ with $\alpha_H\ge\|H\|$ for any block encoding, is the residual's effective condition number times $\log(1/\varepsilon)$. It has the form of the optimal linear-systems law, $\mathcal O(\kappa\log(1/\varepsilon))$ queries \cite{costa2022optimal}, which no algorithm improves to $\kappa^{1-\delta}$ unless BQP $=$ PSPACE \cite{harrow2009hhl}, so the two laws are statements about conditioning. The standard form's $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$, which holds for constant coefficients with the best exact encoding (\Cref{eq:standard-wall}), is that of an order-$k$ least-squares discretisation. The descriptor's $\tilde\Theta(N)$ on a regular problem (\Cref{eq:descriptor-degree}) is that of a first-order one, the conditioning that the linear-systems solvers for ordinary equations also pay by reducing the order before discretising (\Cref{sec:bg-de-linear}); on singular problems and two axes it is about $\tilde\Theta(N^2)$ or steeper. Without a source, or with a polynomial source of fixed degree, a query costs $\mathcal O(n^2)$ two-qubit gates in both forms (\Cref{prop:standard-cost,prop:descriptor-cost}), a general source adding $\mathcal O(N)$, and amplification multiplies the queries by $2k_{\mathrm{aa}}+1=\mathcal O(1/\gamma)$ (\Cref{eq:total-cost}). On a regular problem with constant coefficients a preparation therefore costs $\tilde\Theta(N\,\mathrm{poly}(n)/\gamma)$ gates in the descriptor against $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k}\,\mathrm{poly}(n)/\gamma)$ in the standard form. The overlap $\gamma$ is measured rather than bounded and differs between the forms, the descriptor's being capped by the field block's weight, which the rescaling restores (\Cref{sec:results-gap}). The filter sets only the constant (\Cref{sec:results-faithful}), and both laws take the spectral edge as known (\Cref{sec:limitations}).

How far the floor extends is a separate question. \Cref{eq:standard-wall} holds for every block encoding of $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ under any polynomial filter of the walk, since by Chebyshev's inequality none suppresses the excited spectrum at a lower degree than the minimax filter (\Cref{sec:bg-filtering}). Over all algorithms the evidence is weaker. Lin and Tong prove, by reduction from unstructured search, that their ground-state algorithm's dependence on the gap and the overlap is essentially optimal for an oracle that block-encodes $H$ \cite{lintong2020groundstate}. The walk here is built from an encoding of $R$, which supplies the square root in \Cref{eq:degree}, so their bound does not apply as it stands, and whether their hard instances arise as stacked residuals is not shown. The thesis claims the floor for polynomial filters of the walk. For Wu et al.'s second-order examples with constant coefficients it follows that $\|\Hstd\|=\Theta(N^8)$ sets the number of queries under any block encoding, whatever circuit implements one query (\Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian}). The descriptor lowers the floor by replacing $R$, which no statement about the encodings of a fixed $R$ excludes.

What $N$ costs depends on what fixes it. For an analytic solution the Chebyshev coefficients decay geometrically \cite{trefethen2013approximation}, so a field accurate to $\delta$ needs $N$ growing only as $\log(1/\delta)$, and both degrees are then polylogarithmic in the precision, which implies no speed-up. On one axis a degree linear in $N$ is no asymptotic advantage in any case: the ultraspherical method solves such an equation classically in $\mathcal O(N)$ operations once its coefficients are resolved \cite{olver2013}, and decoding the whole field from a quantum state needs of order $N$ estimates (\Cref{sec:bg-filtering}). An advantage could appear only on many axes, where a classical solver's cost grows at least as the number of unknowns, $N^D$ on $D$ axes. There the descriptor's gap falls, and on the two axes measured its estimated degree grows as $N^{\res[2]{stateprep/R5b/descriptor/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}}$ per axis on the heat equation and $N^{\res[2]{stateprep/R5a/descriptor/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}}$ on Laplace's (\Cref{tab:growth}), faster than the $N^2$ unknowns, though between the least settled resolutions of the study. Whether a reformulation can keep the gap on several axes therefore decides whether this route can compete with classical spectral solvers at all, and the thesis leaves that open.

Three rows of \Cref{tab:applicability} rest on mechanisms that \Cref{sec:results} measured without explaining. The split in the simple-zero row turns on where the coefficient vanishes. The standard form's norm is set by the top Chebyshev modes, whose derivatives are largest at the ends of the interval by Markov's inequality \cite{borweinerdelyi1995}. A coefficient vanishing at both ends, $1-x^2$ on R3, suppresses them where they peak, and $\|\Hstd\|$ grows as $N^4$; $x-1$ on R4a vanishes at one end only, and the $N^8$ law holds (\Cref{sec:results-scaling}). The rescaled descriptor's norm grows as $N^4$ on both, so it gains on R4a and not on R3. This is an inference from two problems, consistent with \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian} but not a theorem; it predicts that an interior zero, which suppresses neither end, leaves the standard form's $N^{4k}$ law and with it the descriptor's advantage in place, which no benchmark problem tests.

On the nonlinear rows the guidance follows $a/\nu$ rather than $\rho$. The ratio $\rho=a\|F_2\|/\nu$ is a worst case over all data of norm $a$, and $\|F_2\|$ grows with the modes because the advection term couples into a mode with a strength that rises with its wavenumber (\Cref{sec:fluids}). Burgers' data $a\sin x$ occupy one mode and reach the others only through the nonlinearity, so at moderate strength the high modes, where $F_2$ is largest, may carry too little of the solution to move the lift's error, which would explain why that error is the same on four modes as on sixteen (\Cref{sec:results-nonlinear}). The vortex pair puts several wavenumbers into its data, and there the error does grow with the modes (\Cref{sec:results-ns}). Both readings are inferences from the measured errors, consistent with $\rho<1$ being sufficient and far from necessary, and they are why the table reads convergence from the lift's own error per order.

The time axis decides the gain on those rows. At one Carleman order the forms differ in the time derivative, of norm $2(N_t-1)$ in the mass form against $\Theta(N_t^2)$, and share the coupling term, which the mass form multiplies by $\|\Bt_\xi\|_2\in[2,2+\sqrt2]$ (\Cref{sec:prep-vs-proj}). On a coarse time axis, at strong forcing and on many modes, the coupling dominates and that factor makes the mass form's norm the larger, which accounts for the standard form's wins at $n_t=3$ on Burgers and for the descriptor's gain from $n_t=4$. The same balance, with the loosening of the descriptor's encoding of the coupling, narrows the Navier--Stokes margin as the modes grow at fixed $n_t$ and predicts a reversal there on enough modes, not reached on up to $48$ (\Cref{sec:results-ns}). The account assumes comparable gaps in the two forms and was not checked term by term; its recommendation, to raise $n_t$ with the truncation, does not depend on that.

Every cost here is that of a fault-tolerant machine (\Cref{sec:bg-qc}). On R2a at $n=8$ the descriptor's preparation is estimated at $\res{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ logical qubits and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{gate_count}$ two-qubit gates, $\res[1]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{preparations}$ amplified repetitions of a filter of degree $\res{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$, against $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{gate_count}$ for the structured standard form and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax/encoder=published}{gate_count}$ with Wu et al.'s encoding (T3, \Cref{tab:headline}). The widths stay modest, the widest circuits estimated, Navier--Stokes on twelve modes at $K=4$ and $n_t=5$, needing $\res{stateprep/R10c-M12/descriptor/n5/T3/carleman=4/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits in the descriptor and $\res{stateprep/R10c-M12/standard/n5/T3/carleman=4/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ in the standard form; the obstacle is the gate count. The descriptor's count on R2a buys one second-order equation on $N=2^8$ coefficients, a problem of no classical difficulty, and the reformulation divides the count by about the ratio of the two degrees, which grows as $N^{2k-1}$ on such a problem; that alone does not make the problem worth a quantum computer. The estimates count logical two-qubit gates after transpiling to $\{\mathrm{CX},U\}$ (\Cref{sec:pipeline-design}). They omit the non-Clifford gates from which an error-corrected machine synthesises the rotations $U$, whose count dominates its cost, as well as the code's overhead and routing, and they record no depth, which grows with the degree since the walks run in sequence. Jennings et al.\ cost a linear-systems route to linear \glspl{ode} in logical qubits and queries to the block encoding of the system's matrix, with their constant factors \cite{jennings2023cost}; their counts and these meet at the query, whose gates we supply for both forms (\Cref{tab:headline}), but their examples are time evolutions of stable systems and are not compared with ours. A filter of $\res{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$ sequential walks of $\res{query/stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{gates}$ two-qubit gates each lies far beyond the depth that uncorrected processors execute reliably \cite{preskill2018nisq}, which is why noisy hardware is out of scope.

Every result is a classical computation, and nothing ran on hardware. The claims are cost laws of operators and filters, which classical computation tests exactly; it cannot test noise, and the method's robustness to it is unknown. Beyond the $\res{stateprep/R4d/descriptor/n3/T1/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits of circuit simulation, exact emulation stands in for the circuit by the argument of \Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}, which the agreement of the two confirms only where both run; the widest reflections, of the two-axis descriptor problems and the larger lifts, are built and counted but not verified whole, and Navier--Stokes is reached by emulation and estimate alone (\Cref{sec:results-ns}). The growth exponents are two-point slopes between stated resolutions, consistent with the laws rather than fits of them, and the two-axis slopes, between $n=4$ and $5$ per axis, are the least settled. Where $N$ must grow with the problem, as on a stiff equation, the dependence of the gap's constant on the stiffness was measured on R2c alone, and beyond $8{,}192$ columns the reference's error bound is an a posteriori estimate (\Cref{app:iterative-reference}).

Two inputs are supplied rather than found. Every degree uses the spectral edge $\sigma_0,\sigma_1$ of the classical reference (\Cref{sec:pipeline-filter}). An underestimated gap raises the degree only as the inverse square root of the bound and still meets $\varepsilon$ (\Cref{app:edge-study}), so the laws survive any lower bound of the right order in $N$; \Cref{thm:flat-gap}, proved by compactness, gives that order on regular problems without its constant, which is measured here and supplied nowhere. The exact constraint points of R4 and R6b and the heat equation's initial function at $t=-1$ are set up from the reference solution when the problem is built, an answer-dependent set-up that a new problem must supply for itself (\Cref{sec:pipeline-inputs}), and the rescaling rule's two constants were set once on the benchmark problems, so its check against the best $\zeta$ is in-sample (\Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}). The output is a state. The field is decoded classically from amplitudes that a quantum computer does not expose, and Wu et al.'s interferometric readout is built and verified, with its correction for post-selection, only at small $n$ (\Cref{sec:pipeline-readout}).

The descriptor's scope ends where its gap does. The gap is proved flat only on regular problems, measured flat after rescaling on singular ones with a simple zero, and falls on two axes; a double zero needs the substitution of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints}, whose form presumes the zero's order is known from the equation, and mixed derivatives have no block and are not supported (\Cref{sec:descriptor-constraints}). The full ultraspherical reduction, a banded residual on $N$ unknowns with no block register, would remove the inverse without carrying any derivative, but it was neither built nor compared (\Cref{sec:bg-ultraspherical}), and nothing here predicts whether it keeps the descriptor's norm without eroding the gap.

Of the extensions, only first-order systems were run. Complex-valued fields are carried by realification alone; no complex residual was prepared, and the one native complex encoding, the tensor layout's coupling, is verified but prepares nothing (\Cref{sec:systems-complex}). The doubled space projects, so the \gls{dct} that its circuits cite rather than build matters to no preparation (\Cref{sec:pipeline-design}). A trusted kernel certifies the lifted linear problem and not the lift, so every Carleman preparation needs the lift's measured error beside it, and the thesis has no test of divergence that reads the residual alone (\Cref{sec:results-nonlinear}). Wu et al.'s partial differential and nonlinear examples, their Figs~6 and~7, were not reproduced under any hypothesis tested, and the authors were not contacted (\Cref{app:repro-unreproduced}). This bears on the audit of the paper rather than on the comparison, since the standard form is specified independently of those panels.

Both the advantage and its limits come from the norms of the carried derivatives, which the standard form pays in its Hamiltonian's norm and the descriptor in its gap. That settles the one-axis case, where the degree falls from $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ to $\tilde\Theta(N)$ on regular problems with constant coefficients, and leaves open the case of several axes, where the gap and any competition with classical solvers are decided. Those two limits, the gap on several axes and the edge supplied by the reference, bound the verdicts of \Cref{sec:conclusions} and are where its further work begins.
