\subsection{Overview}
This chapter recreates the physics-informed effective Hamiltonian framework of \cite{wu2025pihm} exactly as published, then subjects it to the same numerical scrutiny this thesis applies to its own contribution. The framework encodes a differential equation's solution as the ground state of a residual Hamiltonian built from a dense Chebyshev differentiation matrix, via a construction reproduced here without modification. \Cref{sec:pihm-residual} states that construction in full; \Cref{sec:pihm-encoding,sec:pihm-verification} give the published encoding circuit and this thesis's own reconstruction, verification and measurement of it; \Cref{sec:pihm-encoding-wall} diagnoses where the construction fails and motivates the descriptor reformulation of \Cref{sec:descriptor-construction}.

\subsection{The Residual Construction and Its Ground State}
\label{sec:pihm-residual}
Wu et al.\ recast solving a differential equation as three linked moves \cite{wu2025pihm}: represent a candidate solution as a quantum state carrying its Chebyshev coefficients; write the differential equation and every boundary or initial condition as linear operators that must annihilate that state; and assemble a Hamiltonian from those operators whose ground state is exactly the state satisfying all of them at once.

\paragraph{The Chebyshev feature map.} A solution $f$ is approximated by a degree-$(N-1)$ truncated Chebyshev series, $N = 2^n$, carried by an $n$-qubit state
\begin{equation}
    \ket{\tau(x)}_n = \frac{1}{2^{n/2}}T_0(x)\ket{0}^{\otimes n} + \frac{1}{2^{(n-1)/2}}\sum_{k=1}^{N-1} T_k(x)\ket{k},
    \label{eq:pihm-tau}
\end{equation}
so that a coefficient vector $\ket{\psi}_n \in \mathbb{C}^N$ (unit norm, $\braket{\psi|\psi}=1$) represents the model
\begin{equation}
    f_Q(x) = \sqrt{\eta}\,\braket{\tau(x)|\psi}_n
    \label{eq:pihm-fq}
\end{equation}
for some positive scale $\eta$. Separating the \emph{direction} $\ket{\psi}$ from the \emph{scale} $\sqrt{\eta}$ this way matters because everything from here to the ground-state eigenvalue problem below depends only on $\ket{\psi}$: $\eta$ cancels out of every constraint $A\ket{\psi}=0$ and out of the Hamiltonian built from such constraints, so it plays no role until \Cref{eq:pihm-regular} fixes it, once, from a single known data point, entirely after the ground state has been found. Every equation from here on is written for $\ket{\psi}$ alone, with $\eta$ suppressed accordingly.

\paragraph{The derivative operator.} Differentiating a Chebyshev series produces another Chebyshev series whose coefficients are a fixed linear function of the original ones \cite{trefethen2000spectral}: writing this map in the normalised basis of \Cref{eq:pihm-tau} gives an $N \times N$ matrix $G$ with
\begin{equation}
    G_{k,p} =
    \begin{cases}
        2p, & p > k \ge 1,\ p-k\ \text{odd}, \\
        \sqrt{2}\,p, & k = 0,\ p\ \text{odd},\ p \ge 1, \\
        0, & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:pihm-G-entries}
\end{equation}
so that $f_Q'(x) = \braket{\tau(x)|G^\top|\psi}_n$, and the $m$-th derivative follows from the power $G^{\top m}$. \Cref{eq:pihm-G-entries} is dense and upper triangular: row $k$ is non-zero at every column $p > k$ of the right parity, so the lowest-order coefficient can depend on as many as half of the others, and $G$ carries $\Theta(N^2)$ non-zero entries in total. \Cref{app:cheb} gives this fill-in explicitly at $N=4$, alongside the banded factors $\tilde{B}, \tilde{D}$ that \Cref{sec:descriptor-construction} builds from the same recurrence.

\paragraph{The differential-equation row.} For a linear equation of order $k$ with constant coefficients, $\sum_{j=0}^k a_j f^{(j)} = 0$, direct substitution of $G^{\top j}$ for each derivative gives
\begin{equation}
    A = \sum_{j=0}^k a_j\, G^{\top j},
    \label{eq:pihm-A}
\end{equation}
Wu et al.'s own construction \cite{wu2025pihm}; a second-order equation, for instance, gives $A = a_2 G^{\top 2} + a_1 G^\top + a_0 I$ directly.

A coefficient $a_j(x)$ that varies with $x$ cannot be substituted this way: $a_j(x) f^{(j)}(x)$ is a product of two functions, and the Chebyshev coefficients of a product are not the product of the coefficients. Wu et al.\ resolve this with a second operator, the lifting operator $M_{x^p}$, built from the general Chebyshev product-to-sum identity
\begin{equation}
    x^p\, T_k(x) = 2^{-p}\sum_{i=0}^{p}\binom{p}{i}\, T_{|k+p-2i|}(x),
    \label{eq:pihm-product-to-sum}
\end{equation}
the $p$-fold iterate of the pairwise rule $T_jT_k = \tfrac12(T_{j+k}+T_{|j-k|})$ that reappears below in the nonlinear extension. Conjugating \Cref{eq:pihm-product-to-sum} into the normalised bases of \Cref{eq:pihm-tau} gives an operator $M_{x^p}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}
    x^p\,\bra{\tau(x)}_n = \bra{\tau(x)}_{n+1}\,M_{x^p}
    \label{eq:pihm-Mxp}
\end{equation}
(entries: \Cref{app:pihm-residual}). Multiplying a degree-$(N-1)$ model by $x^p$ can raise its degree by as much as $p$, so $M_{x^p}$ is exact and rectangular, $2N \times N$: no truncation is needed once the result is represented in the one-qubit-wider register \Cref{eq:pihm-Mxp} already targets. A general coefficient, expanded to a finite power series $a_j(x) = \sum_p c_{j,p}\,x^p$ (a Maclaurin truncation, for a non-polynomial coefficient), lifts by linearity to $M_{a_j} = \sum_p c_{j,p}\, M_{x^p}$, and the differential-equation row for the general, variable-coefficient case follows by composing $M_{a_j}$ and $G^{\top j}$ term-by-term,
\begin{equation}
    A = \sum_{j=0}^k M_{a_j}\, G^{\top j},
    \label{eq:pihm-A-general}
\end{equation}
with $M_{a_j} = a_j I$ recovering \Cref{eq:pihm-A} whenever $a_j$ is constant.

\paragraph{Data constraints.} Boundary and initial conditions are built from one rank-one point-evaluation generator, $B_n(x) = \sqrt{2^n}\,\ket{0}^{\otimes n}\bra{\tau(x)}_n$, non-zero only in its first row. A condition with a zero target is \emph{invariant} -- unchanged under $\ket{\psi} \to c\ket{\psi}$ -- and enters the Hamiltonian directly as one further linear constraint, $B\ket{\psi}=0$, e.g.\ a Dirichlet condition $f(x_z)=0$ giving $B_n(x_z)\ket{\psi}=0$ and a Neumann condition $f'(x_m)=0$ giving $B_n(x_m)G^\top\ket{\psi}=0$. A condition with a non-zero target, $f(x_s)=y_s$, is not scale-covariant and instead fixes the amplitude $\sqrt{\eta}$ of \Cref{eq:pihm-fq} directly, after the ground state has been found:
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{\eta} = \frac{y_s}{\braket{\tau(x_s)|\psi_g}_n}.
    \label{eq:pihm-regular}
\end{equation}
Only one such \emph{regular} constraint is used per solve, and \Cref{eq:pihm-regular} is the only place $\eta$ re-enters the pipeline.

\begin{definition}[Physics-informed effective Hamiltonian]
\label{def:pihm-hamiltonian}
Collecting the differential-equation row $A$ and every invariant data constraint $B_i$,
\begin{equation}
    H = A^\top A + \sum_i B_i^\top B_i, \qquad \braket{\psi|H|\psi} = \|A\psi\|^2 + \sum_i \|B_i\psi\|^2.
    \label{eq:pihm-hamiltonian}
\end{equation}
$H$ is Hermitian and positive semi-definite, so $\braket{\psi|H|\psi} \ge 0$ with equality exactly when every constraint holds simultaneously. The differential equation problem is thereby an eigenvalue problem, $H\ket{\psi_g} = \lambda_{\min}\ket{\psi_g}$, $\lambda_{\min}\approx0$, whose ground state $\ket{\psi_g}$ is the solution.
\end{definition}

Forming $A^\top A$ squares $A$'s own condition number, so $H$ is markedly more ill-conditioned than $A$ itself, and its ground state sits at the base of a highly compressed spectrum. \Cref{sec:pihm-encoding-wall} traces this to the measured spectral-gap collapse of \Cref{sec:pihm-verification}.

\paragraph{The nonlinear extension.} Wu et al.\ extend \Cref{def:pihm-hamiltonian} to polynomial nonlinearity by doubling the Hilbert space, $\ket{\Psi} = \ket{\psi}\otimes\ket{\psi}$, and folding a product of two coefficient vectors back into one register via the same product-to-sum identity, $T_jT_k = \tfrac12(T_{j+k}+T_{|j-k|})$ \cite{wu2025pihm}. A residual quadratic in $\ket{\psi}$ of this kind annihilates a subspace of the $2N$-dimensional $\ket{\Psi}$ far larger than the single physical direction: Wu et al.\ themselves note that the resulting $H$ carries \emph{at least} $2^{2n-1}$ degenerate zero-energy states, half of the doubled Hilbert space at minimum. No imaginary-time filter can select a direction inside a degenerate zero-energy cluster, so extracting the physical solution from this construction is classical post-selection within a known kernel rather than genuine state preparation; \Cref{sec:nonlinear-extension} addresses this, for a restricted but practically important class of problems, with a different construction.

\subsection{The Published Collocation Encoding}
\label{sec:pihm-encoding}
Realising $H$ as a circuit requires a unitary $U_H$ that block-encodes it, $\bra{0}_aU_H\ket{0}_a = H/\alpha$ for some $\alpha \ge \|H\|$, so that ground-state preparation and readout can act on $U_H$ without reference to how $H$ was built. The published route encodes $A$, and hence $H$, exactly as \Cref{sec:pihm-residual} assembles it: as a dense operator, with no attempt to expose or exploit any further structure.

A block encoding of a general dense matrix costs at least as many data-loading gates as the matrix has distinct entries that must be individually addressed, realised as a rotation controlled on the matrix's row and column indices \cite{sunderhauf2024blockencoding,camps2024explicit}. $G^\top$ is not sparse in this sense: by \Cref{eq:pihm-G-entries}, column $p$ of $G^\top$ takes only the two values $\{2p, \sqrt2\,p\}$, but which of $N$ possible columns is being addressed still varies from entry to entry, so a column of $G^\top$ still requires $\Theta(N)$ distinct addressed values rather than the $O(1)$ a banded or Toeplitz structure would permit.

Wu et al.'s own circuit avoids an exhaustive $\Theta(N)$-branch multiplexed rotation by loading this near-linear dependence on the column index approximately, via a ladder of $n$ singly-controlled rotations on one flag qubit \cite{wu2025pihm}. Because rotations on the same qubit compose by exact angle addition, the ladder's net rotation for column index $p$ is a sine of a sum of arcsines of $p$'s bits, which only approximates the desired value $2p$ -- $\sin$ of a sum is not the sum of sines -- with an error that shrinks rapidly as the per-bit angle shrinks.

This thesis reconstructs that circuit gate-for-gate from the source paper's own description, since its precise two-ancilla wiring is not fully recoverable from the published figure at the resolution available, and verifies the reconstruction bit-exact against an independently built \emph{exact} register-arithmetic alternative -- an $O(2^n)$-depth circuit with no approximation at all, kept purely as a verification reference -- for $n$ up to $5$. Where the source paper states only that its errors are ``negligible'' and decrease ``exponentially'' with $n$, \Cref{app:pihm-circuit} derives a rigorous closed-form entrywise error bound from the same small-angle mechanism and verifies it against the measured error at every $n$ tested from $2$ to $8$: the bound falls by roughly a further order of magnitude with each additional qubit, from $1.9\times10^{-2}$ at $n=2$ to $1.4\times10^{-9}$ at $n=8$ (\Cref{tab:app-epsilon-G}), making the qualitative published claim quantitative.

The reproduced circuit uses $n+4$ ancilla qubits (the exact register-arithmetic alternative needs $n+5$), within one qubit of the $n+3$ resource count reported in the source paper's own figure -- \Cref{app:pihm-circuit} attributes the discrepancy to this reconstruction keeping two flag qubits live where the published circuit appears to combine them into one. Composing this encoding of $G$ into $A$, and $A$ into $H$ via \Cref{eq:pihm-hamiltonian}, follows the standard linear-combination-of-unitaries and Gram-squaring algebra common to every block encoding built in this thesis: forming a Gram matrix from an encoded operator roughly doubles its ancilla count and squares its subnormalisation, and summing several such terms for the differential-equation row and every boundary condition adds their individual weights. \Cref{sec:pihm-verification} reports what this composition costs once carried through to the fully assembled $H$.

\subsection{Numerical Verification: Reproducing and Characterising the Published Encoding}
\label{sec:pihm-verification}
Verification proceeds in two stages, each checked against an independent classical reference so that a construction error in $H$ cannot be mistaken for a state-preparation error downstream. First, encoding exactness: every block-encoded atom -- $G$, each $M_{x^p}$, each boundary-condition generator -- is verified column-by-column against its reference matrix to $10^{-9}$--$10^{-11}$, and the Gram-and-sum recipe that combines them into $H$ is verified to $<10^{-10}$ on small test cases. Second, state preparation: the prepared ground state is compared to the classical \texttt{eigh} ground state of the same assembled $H$ by cosine similarity, with that classical target itself cross-checked against an independent reference solver, separating a construction error in $H$ from a filter-convergence error in the preparation stage. For the nonlinear extension of \Cref{sec:pihm-residual}, the same protocol measures the degenerate kernel directly: null spaces occupy as much as $98\%$ of $\dim(H)$ on the cases tested, well past the $2^{2n-1}$ floor Wu et al.\ state.

The composed $H$ is not verified end-to-end -- at the qubit counts the headline cases in \Cref{sec:results} require, a dense classical simulation would need hundreds of gigabytes and is not attempted -- so correctness beyond a handful of qubits rests on this compositional argument rather than a direct check of the assembled operator. Within that scope, three measurements characterise the published route empirically rather than merely reproduce the source paper's own qualitative claims.

First, conditioning: for the stiff pilot equation of \Cref{eq:pihm-A} (worked at $N=4$ in \Cref{app:pihm-residual}), the ground eigenvalue $\lambda_{\min}$ is not yet numerically zero at $n=4$ ($\lambda_{\min}/\alpha \approx 3.9\times10^{-5}$, an under-resolved discretisation rather than a genuine kernel), but reaches $\lambda_{\min}/\alpha \approx 1.4\times10^{-14}$ by $n=5$ -- the same equation moving, within a single extra qubit, from an unreliable ground state to a numerically exact one. Second, subnormalisation: $G^\top$'s own $S$-norm subnormalisation was verified directly against \texttt{pihm.chebyshev\_derivative\_matrix} for $n=1$ through $8$ and grows empirically as $\Theta(N^4)$ (\Cref{app:pihm-circuit}); composing this into $H$ via a Gram square inflates it to $\alpha_H \approx \Theta(N^8)$ against $\|H\| = \Theta(N^2)$, a subnormalisation tax that grows polynomially in $N$ rather than staying tight, with a Haar-average post-selection probability that correspondingly falls like $N^{-3/2}$ rather than remaining constant. Third, and most consequential: the relative spectral gap of the fully assembled $H$, computed classically, falls to $\sim10^{-15}$ by $n=7$ -- below double-precision machine epsilon, so the ground state this Hamiltonian encodes is not numerically resolvable at all at that resolution, independent of how it is later block-encoded into a circuit. \Cref{sec:results} reports the full per-case measurement and cost table.

\subsection{Critical Assessment: The Encoding Wall}
\label{sec:pihm-encoding-wall}
Three separate measurements in \Cref{sec:pihm-verification} point at the same structural cause. $G$'s $\Theta(N)$ distinct column values force any dense encoding of it to pay a data-loading cost that scales with $N$ rather than with $\log N$; squaring $A$ into $H = A^\top A$ squares that cost again in the subnormalisation; and summing the differential-equation row against every boundary-condition row compounds it further. The result is a block-encoded $H$ whose subnormalisation grows like $\Theta(N^8)$ against an operator norm of only $\Theta(N^2)$ -- a construction that, per query, is exponentially worse than it needs to be in the qubit count $n$, and polynomially worse than it needs to be in the Chebyshev resolution $N$.

This is not merely a cost inefficiency. Ground-state preparation by imaginary-time filtering needs a number of block-encoding queries that scales with $\alpha_H/\Delta$, where $\Delta$ is the spectral gap \cite{gilyen2019qsvt}; for any block encoding of this particular $H$, the gap itself is $\Delta = \Theta(1)$ against $\|H\| = \Theta(N^2)$, so $\alpha_H \ge \|H\|$ already forces at least $\tilde\Theta(N^2)$ queries before a single gate of the encoding is counted -- neither route examined in this thesis is poly-logarithmic end to end (\Cref{sec:discussion} completes this argument). The published collocation route misses even this floor by a further factor of $\approx N^6$, and the deficit is not merely asymptotic: at $n \gtrsim 7$, the composed $H$'s relative spectral gap has collapsed below double-precision machine epsilon, so the ground state it encodes cannot be resolved in simulation at any cost, let alone prepared efficiently on a quantum computer.

This chapter has reproduced the physics-informed effective Hamiltonian framework of Wu et al.\ exactly as published; the diagnosis above is a property of that published construction, not of any choice made in reproducing it. What makes $A$ dense is the same thing that makes it hard to encode: $G$ substitutes each derivative directly for the coefficients of the function it differentiates, in one step, and that substitution is exactly what a well-conditioned, banded alternative would decline to take. \Cref{sec:descriptor-construction} develops that alternative, carrying the intermediate derivatives $G$ eliminates as their own unknowns rather than substituting them away, so that the whole encoding-and-preparation pipeline inherits a structure the published route never had access to.
