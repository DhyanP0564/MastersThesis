In the standard form the state holds the field alone, and every derivative is represented through the dense differentiation matrix $\G$, so that a term $f^{(j)}$ acts as $\G^j$ on the field's coefficients. \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian} found the cost of that form in its operator: with the encoding already tight, $\|\G^k\|_2=\Theta(N^{2k})$ makes $\|\Hstd\|=\Theta(N^{4k})$ for constant coefficients, and the filter's degree grows as $N^{2k}$ against a gap that stays flat. A lower degree therefore needs a different residual, which leaves the question \emph{can the residual be reformulated so that its Hamiltonian is banded and well conditioned without changing its solution, and what does the reformulation cost?}

\subsection{The Banded Factorisation}
\label{sec:descriptor-construction}

Differentiation of a Chebyshev series is dense because the derivative is expanded in the basis of the function, which is also why the tau and collocation methods produce dense, ill-conditioned matrices \cite{olver2013,boyd2001Chebyshev}; into the neighbouring basis, differentiation is banded. The coefficients of a function and of its derivative are tied by a three-term recurrence, each row of which ties one coefficient of the function to two of the derivative (\Cref{app:cheb}). Truncated to $N$ coefficients and written in the normalised basis of \Cref{sec:standard-model}, with $\fhat{0}$ and $\fhat{1}$ the coefficient vectors of $f$ and $f'$, it is the matrix identity
\begin{equation}
    \Bt\,\fhat{1} = \Dt\,\fhat{0} .
    \label{eq:banded-factorisation}
\end{equation}
$\Dt$ has a single non-zero diagonal, the first superdiagonal, with entries $(2\sqrt2,4,6,\dots,2(N-1))$; $\Bt$ has the diagonal $(2,1,1,\dots)$ and the second superdiagonal $(-\sqrt2,-1,-1,\dots)$, the irregular first entries coming from the normalisation of $T_0$. Both hold $\mathcal{O}(N)$ non-zero entries against the $N^2/4$ of $\G$. Since $\Bt$ is upper triangular with a non-zero diagonal, it is invertible, and
\begin{equation}
    \G = \Bt^{-1}\Dt
    \label{eq:G-banded}
\end{equation}
exactly at every $N$ (\Cref{app:cheb}). Away from its first row, $\Bt^{-1}$ is a triangle of ones on each parity class: the density of $\G$ is that inverse, and \Cref{eq:banded-factorisation} is the same map before the inverse is taken. The singular values of a single-diagonal matrix are its entries' magnitudes, so $\|\Dt\|_2=2(N-1)$, while $\|\Bt\|_2\le2+\sqrt2$.

Up to the basis normalisation, $\Bt$ and $\Dt$ are twice the ultraspherical method's operators for conversion from first-kind to second-kind Chebyshev polynomials and for differentiation into the second kind \cite{olver2013}, and the factorisation is that method's discrete shadow (\Cref{sec:bg-ultraspherical}). In the normalised basis the two factors return the coefficients of a function and of its derivative in the second-kind polynomials $U_m$ (\Cref{lem:second-kind}), so \Cref{eq:banded-factorisation} equates two expansions of $f'$ in that basis. The ultraspherical method uses such operators to eliminate the derivatives and solves a banded system in $f$ alone; that reduction is not built or compared here, because its conversions weight the rows, so that it poses a differently weighted least-squares problem, their entries would have to be encoded, and the gap mechanism of \Cref{sec:descriptor-gap} would not carry over to it without a measurement (\Cref{app:ultraspherical}). What Olver and Townsend's conditioning bound leads one to expect of it is discussed in \Cref{sec:limitations}.

The descriptor form keeps \Cref{eq:banded-factorisation} as a row and never forms the quotient \Cref{eq:G-banded}. It carries $f',\dots,f^{(k)}$ as unknowns beside $f$, ties consecutive derivatives by the factors, and writes the equation over all of them, so that one pair of factors serves every order, axis and coupled field. For order $k\ge2$ these unknowns form the \emph{chain}; a first-order equation or system needs none and takes the \emph{mass form} (\Cref{sec:descriptor-layouts}). Carrying derivatives as unknowns so as not to square the condition number of a least-squares functional is the idea of first-order-system least squares \cite{cai1994fosls} (\Cref{sec:bg-fosls}), and the physics-informed Hamiltonian is such a functional. Our increment is the transfer of both ideas to a block-encoded physics-informed Hamiltonian and the measurement of its consequences for ground-state preparation.

\Cref{fig:sparsity} shows the effect on the running example $f''+4f'+4f=0$ (R2a). At $n=7$ the standard form's stacked residual holds \res{build/R2a/standard/n7}{nonzeros} non-zero entries on \res{build/R2a/standard/n7}{dimension} unknowns, and the descriptor's holds \res{build/R2a/descriptor/n7}{nonzeros} on \res{build/R2a/descriptor/n7}{dimension}: four times the unknowns, from the block register that \Cref{sec:descriptor-layouts} counts, and six times fewer entries. With the kernel unchanged, the Hamiltonian's norm falls from $\Theta(N^{4k})$, which is $\Theta(N^8)$ at second order, to $\Theta(N^2)$ at fixed block weights; the price is a block register and a gap that the derivative blocks erode, which a weight per block, set from the equation and the resolution, restores on one axis without ancilla.

\subsection{The Descriptor Residual and Its Kernel}
\label{sec:descriptor-layouts}

Consider the linear \gls{ode} of order $k$
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{j=0}^{k} a_j(x)\, f^{(j)}(x) = r(x), \qquad x \in [-1,1],
\end{equation}
with the constraints of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints}. Every function here is truncated to $N$ normalised Chebyshev coefficients.

\paragraph{The chain ($k\ge2$).} Let $\fhat{j}\in\mathbb{C}^N$ be the coefficient vector of $f^{(j)}$ for $j=0,\dots,k$, stacked into the state
\begin{equation}
  w = (\fhat{0};\, \fhat{1};\, \dots;\, \fhat{k}).
\end{equation}
Two kinds of row couple the blocks. The \emph{recurrence rows}
\begin{equation}
  -\Dt\, \fhat{j} + \Bt\, \fhat{j+1} = 0, \qquad j = 0, \dots, k-1,
  \label{eq:descriptor-recurrence-rows}
\end{equation}
apply \Cref{eq:banded-factorisation} block by block. They contain no derivative operator, and on the kernel they are equivalent to $\fhat{j+1}=\G\fhat{j}$. The \emph{equation row} is the equation itself, $\sum_j\M_{a_j}\fhat{j}-\M_r\Dzero(x_s)\fhat{0}=0$, with $\M_{a_j}$ the multiplication operator of \Cref{sec:standard-operators}, lifted exactly where the standard form lifts it, and the source entering through the collapse row as in \Cref{eq:standard-equation}. The derivatives are unknowns of $w$ and are never replaced by $\G^j\fhat{0}$, so the dense quotient \Cref{eq:G-banded} never appears. The name is borrowed from descriptor systems in control theory \cite{luenberger1977descriptor,dai1989singular}, which likewise keep intermediate quantities as unknowns beside the state and tie them by algebraic rows.

\begin{definition}[Descriptor equation block]
\label{def:descriptor-residual-operator}
Collecting the recurrence rows and the equation row, the descriptor's equation block $\Adesc$ is the block matrix
\begin{equation}
\Adesc =
\begin{pmatrix}
-\Dt & \Bt &        &        &          \\
          & -\Dt & \Bt &      &          \\
          &          & \ddots & \ddots &          \\
          &          &        & -\Dt & \Bt \\
\M_{a_0} - \M_r\Dzero & \M_{a_1}  & \cdots & \cdots & \M_{a_k}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
with $(k+1)N$ columns before padding, so that $\Adesc\,w=0$ is the equation.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{The padded block register.} The block index is held by $n_f=\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil$ qubits, so the state is padded to $2^{n_f}$ blocks, each block $b>k$ pinned to zero by its own row $\fhat{b}=0$. No other row reads them, so the padding decouples and leaves the physical spectrum unchanged (\Cref{prop:padding-decouples}). It costs the state $2^{n_f}N$ amplitudes against the standard form's $N$, up to twice the unpadded $(k+1)N$, so a second-order equation carries $f$, $f'$ and $f''$ in four blocks. The row blocks, the several-axis case and the padded counts are in \Cref{app:descriptor-rows}.

\paragraph{The mass form ($k=1$).} A first-order system $z'=\Acoup z$ of $C$ components with a constant coupling matrix $\Acoup$ ($C=1$ for a single equation) has in the standard form the equation rows $I_C\otimes\G-\Acoup\otimes I_N$, one block per component. Its equation already gives each derivative, so the mass form carries no derivative block; it multiplies these rows on the left by $I_C\otimes\Bt$ and uses $\Bt\G=\Dt$,
\begin{equation}
    (I_C\otimes\Bt)\bigl(I_C\otimes\G-\Acoup\otimes I_N\bigr)
    = I_C\otimes\Dt-\Acoup\otimes\Bt .
    \label{eq:mass-form}
\end{equation}
which is banded and free of $\G$; $\Bt$ plays the mass matrix $M$ of $M\dot y=F(y)$ \cite{hairerwanner1996}, hence the name. For a scalar equation with a constant coefficient it is, up to normalisation, the ultraspherical method's own first-order operator: R1, $f'+2f=0$, becomes $\Dt+2\Bt$ where the standard form has the dense $\G+2I$ (both at $N=4$, with the chain, in \Cref{app:cheb}). The mass form keeps the standard form's state, initial state and readout, so it adds no qubits and has no derivative block to rescale (\Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}). Coupled fields and Carleman lifts are such systems, and \Cref{sec:systems-fields,sec:carleman} carry them in this form.

\paragraph{Constraints.}\label{sec:descriptor-constraints} A data constraint sits on the block that holds the quantity it constrains. A Dirichlet condition $f(x_z)=0$ reads block~$0$ with the zero row $\Brow(x_z)$, as in the standard form. A Neumann condition $f'(x_m)=0$ reads block~$1$ with the same row, $\Brow(x_m)\fhat{1}=0$, where the standard form must write $\Brow(x_m)\G$. The regular datum reads block~$0$, so the field is decoded and scaled exactly as in the standard form (\Cref{sec:standard-model}), and the two forms differ only in how the derivatives are carried.

\begin{theorem}[Kernel equivalence]
\label{thm:kernel-equivalence}
Let $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ be the standard form's stacked residual (\Cref{def:standard-form}) of a linear problem on one axis. Let $R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ stack the descriptor's recurrence, equation and padding rows with the same constraint rows, each reading the block of the derivative it constrains, the equation row applying the standard form's multiplication operators. Let $R_{\mathrm{mass}}$ be the mass form's stacked residual of a first-order system with a constant coupling matrix. Then
\begin{equation}
    \ker R_{\mathrm{desc}} = \bigl\{\, (p,\ \G p,\ \dots,\ \G^{k} p,\ 0) \;:\; p \in \ker R_{\mathrm{std}} \,\bigr\},
    \qquad
    \ker R_{\mathrm{mass}} = \ker R_{\mathrm{std}} .
    \label{eq:kernel-equivalence}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $Ep=(p;\G p;\dots;\G^kp;0)$. Chain: the padding rows force the padding blocks of a kernel vector $w$ to zero, and since $\Bt$ is invertible each recurrence row gives $w_{j+1}=\Bt^{-1}\Dt\,w_j=\G w_j$, so $w=Ew_0$. The recurrence rows vanish on $Ep$ for every $p$, because $(\Bt\G-\Dt)\G^jp=0$, and every other row of $R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ acts on $Ep$ as the standard form's row of the same name acts on $p$: the equation row gives $\sum_j\M_{a_j}\G^jp-\M_r\Dzero(x_s)p$, the standard form's equation block, and a constraint row reads the block that holds $\G^jp$. Hence $R_{\mathrm{desc}}Ep$ vanishes exactly when $R_{\mathrm{std}}p$ does, and $E$ is injective because its first block is the identity. Mass form: $R_{\mathrm{mass}}$ is $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ with its equation rows multiplied on the left by the invertible $I_C\otimes\Bt$ (\Cref{eq:mass-form}) and its constraint rows unchanged.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Several axes.} On several axes the field is block~$0$, and each axis $a$ owns a chain $\partial_af,\dots,\partial_a^{k_a}f$ up to the highest order that the equation or a constraint takes along it, tied by that axis's factors $\Bt_a$ and $\Dt_a$ tensored with the identity on the other axes. A mixed derivative would need a block of its own (which we do not build here). Initial and boundary functions enter both forms as the datum slices of \Cref{eq:datum-slice}, read on the field block. Every axis adds its chain to the one block register, so the state grows with the total order: the register takes $n_f=\lceil\log_2(1+\sum_ak_a)\rceil$ qubits, the one-axis count with the total order in place of $k$, and the state holds $2^{n_f}\prod_aN_a$ amplitudes against the standard form's $\prod_aN_a$ (\Cref{sec:app-variable-coeff}). The theorem holds on several axes, with $E$ carrying $\G_a^{j}$ along each axis's chain, and for the block-rescaled residual of \Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}, whose kernel is the image of this one under a diagonal map; both extensions are proved in \Cref{app:proof-kernel-equivalence}.

An exact kernel exists only where the solution lies in the span of the basis and meets every constraint exactly, as Legendre's $P_4$ does on R3a; otherwise both residuals have full rank, and their ground states are the smallest right singular vectors of different matrices. The descriptor measures a state's residual against a norm that carries its derivatives (\Cref{sec:descriptor-gap}), and the mass form weights the equation rows by $\Bt$. We therefore claim equal kernels only. Both decoded fields converge to the solution, at rates measured in \Cref{sec:results-faithful} and not proved.

\subsection{The Descriptor's Gap}
\label{sec:descriptor-gap}
% v3.11 (2026-10-14): def:regular names thm:flat-gap's hypotheses once ("regular", "singular");
%   every later scope by the leading coefficient cites the class by name (plan §3.4).

While the kernel is the standard form's, the spectrum above it is not. With $E$ the map of \Cref{thm:kernel-equivalence}, the descriptor's Rayleigh quotient on the states that satisfy the recurrences is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}Ep\|^2}{\|Ep\|^2}
    = \frac{\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\,p\|^2}{\sum_{m=0}^{k}\|\G^{m}p\|^2},
    \label{eq:descriptor-quotient}
\end{equation}
because the recurrence and padding rows vanish on $Ep$ and the remaining rows act as the standard form's. By the min--max principle the quotient bounds the descriptor's singular values from above (\Cref{prop:descriptor-quotient}). Its numerator is the standard form's, but its denominator adds every carried derivative, so the gap stays flat in $N$ only where the equation controls each of them: where no state orthogonal to the kernel has a residual small against its largest derivative. On one axis the equation row recovers the top derivative from the lower ones by dividing by the leading coefficient, so the control holds where that coefficient has no zero. Every later degree, gap and subnormalisation claim is scoped by the class this singles out.
\begin{definition}[Regular and singular problems]
\label{def:regular}
A linear problem on one axis, an equation of order $k$ or a first-order system $z'=\Acoup z$ with a constant coupling matrix, is \emph{regular} if its coefficients are polynomials and its leading coefficient ($a_k$, or the identity for a system) has no zero on $[-1,1]$; its source and its solution $f$ are analytic on $[-1,1]$; and its constraint rows read $f,\dots,f^{(k-1)}$ at fixed points, every $g\in C^k[-1,1]$ on which its equation and constraint rows vanish being a multiple of $f$. An equation on one axis whose $a_k$ has a zero on $[-1,1]$ is \emph{singular}.
\end{definition}
Of the benchmark problems, R1, R2, R4b--d, R7 and the truncated Carleman lifts (\Cref{sec:carleman}) are regular, and R3 and R4a singular.
\begin{theorem}[Single-axis gap]
\label{thm:flat-gap}
The gap of the descriptor residual of a regular problem, in the chain unscaled or block-rescaled at a fixed $\zeta$, or in the mass form for a first-order equation or system, is $\Theta(1)$ as $N\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}
The proof (\Cref{app:flat-gap}) reads each recurrence row as the defect between block $j+1$ and the derivative of block $j$, measured in the norm of the second-kind polynomials, integrates it, and closes by compactness, so it bounds the gap away from zero without giving the bound. For a homogeneous equation the condition on $g$ is the rank condition of \Cref{prop:uniqueness-count}. The size of the gap is left to the equation. The norm weighs block $m$ by the size of $f^{(m)}$, so where the solution's derivatives are large the derivative blocks dominate it, and the ground state's weight on the field block, $w_f=\|\fhat{0}\|^2/\|w\|^2$, is small; for an exact kernel it is $\|b\|^2/\sum_m\|\G^mb\|^2$, with $b$ the solution's coefficients. An initial state prepared on the field block then overlaps the ground state by $\gamma\le w_f^{1/2}$, and amplification costs $1/\gamma$. On R2a the unscaled gap is $\res[3]{build/R2a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$ at both $n=3$ and $n=10$. On the stiff equation $f''+5f'+400f=0$ (R2c, Wu et al.'s Fig.~3d), whose solution oscillates at an angular frequency near $20$, the gap is $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$ at both $n=5$ and $n=10$, flat and small, and $w_f$ is $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{field_weight}$, against $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{field_weight}$ on R2a.

The gap falls with $N$ in two cases.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{A singular problem.} A polynomial of degree $N$ can concentrate within $\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})$ of an endpoint, where the Chebyshev points cluster, and each of its derivatives grows by a factor $\mathcal{O}(N^{2})$ there, the extremal behaviour of Markov's inequality \cite{borweinerdelyi1995}. Where $a_k$ vanishes at that endpoint, the leading term $a_kp^{(k)}$ of such a state is small, the lower-order terms are small as well, and the quotient is small against the top block. The measured gap falls as about $N^{-4}$: on Legendre's equation $(1-x^2)f''-2xf'+20f=0$ (R3a), whose leading coefficient vanishes at both ends, it is $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n5/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=5$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=10$, and on $(x-1)f''-xf'+f=(x-1)^2$ (R4a, Wu et al.'s Fig.~5a), whose leading coefficient vanishes at $x=1$, $\res[2,sci]{build/R4a/descriptor/n5/unscaled}{gap}$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R4a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$. At an interior zero, where the resolution is $\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})$, Bernstein's inequality in place of Markov's gives $N^{-2}$ \cite{borweinerdelyi1995}; no benchmark problem has one, and \Cref{app:descriptor-gap} measures the rate on a controlled equation.
    \item \emph{Two axes.} The equation constrains only a sum of top derivatives, $f_{xx}+f_{yy}$ or $f_t-\kappa f_{xx}$. A high-frequency state with $\G_x^2p\approx-\G_y^2p$, or with its weight in the block the equation weights least, has a small residual against large blocks, and only the datum rows penalise it. On the heat equation $\partial_tf-\tfrac{1}{25}\partial_x^2f=0$ (R5b, Wu et al.'s Fig.~6d) the gap is $\res[2,sci]{build/R5b/descriptor/n4x4/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=4$ per axis, the first resolution at which both forms' references are trusted, and $\res[2,sci]{build/R5b/descriptor/n5x5/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=5$, where the standard form's is $\res[3]{build/R5b/standard/n4x4}{gap}$ and $\res[3]{build/R5b/standard/n5x5}{gap}$.
\end{enumerate}
The collapse is not an artefact of the lift, the padding, the arithmetic precision, the constraints, the basis or the classical reference: each was tested and ruled out, and a descriptor built in the ultraspherical basis collapses at the same rates (\Cref{app:descriptor-gap}).

\subsection{Block Rescaling}
\label{sec:descriptor-rescaling}

The remedy is to weigh each carried derivative at the equation's own scale. Carrying block $m$ as $\zeta^{-m}\fhat{m}$ replaces $R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ by $R_{\mathrm{desc}}S$, with $S$ diagonal, $\zeta^m$ on block $m$ and one on the padding. $S$ is invertible, so the kernel becomes $S^{-1}\ker R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ and \Cref{thm:kernel-equivalence} survives; the field block keeps weight one, so the readout is unchanged, and the padding still decouples. Only the spectrum moves: the Hamiltonian becomes the congruence $SR_{\mathrm{desc}}^\dagger R_{\mathrm{desc}}S$, and the denominator of \Cref{eq:descriptor-quotient} becomes $\sum_m\zeta^{-2m}\|\G^mp\|^2$. From here on $R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ and $\Hdesc$ denote the rescaled residual and its Hamiltonian, and the residual of \Cref{sec:descriptor-gap} is called \emph{unscaled}.

In the circuit the weights multiply the bands before the block register's patterns are expanded in Pauli words, so they add no ancilla but lengthen the expansion wherever a pattern's entries were equal across its blocks: the terms, and with them the gates per query, grow by at most the $4^{n_f}$ words of the block register, a factor fixed by the equation and not by $n$ (\Cref{app:block-rescaling}; \Cref{sec:results-scaling} counts the rescaled circuit). One weight per block is what keeps that factor bounded; a per-amplitude equilibration \cite{vandersluis1969} would need a diagonal block encoding of its own, at $\Theta(\dim w)$ gates, and no circuit here uses one.

The weight $\zeta$ is computed from the equation's coefficients and the resolution, never from a solution or a spectrum. For a regular problem,
\begin{equation}
    \zeta = 2\,\bigl(\bar a_j/\bar a_k\bigr)^{1/(k-j)},
    \label{eq:zeta-rule}
\end{equation}
with $j<k$ the lowest order present and $\bar a$ the mean of $|a|$ over the interval. Half of $\zeta$ is the equation's own scale: for constant coefficients and $j=0$ it is the geometric mean $\rho$ of the characteristic roots' magnitudes, a solution's derivatives grow as $\|f^{(m)}\|\approx\rho^m\|f\|$, and dividing block $m$ by $\zeta^m$ keeps every block at the field block's order. A singular problem needs a weight that grows with the resolution. The concentrated states of \Cref{sec:descriptor-gap} have a rescaled quotient of order $N^{-4}\zeta^{2k}$ against their top block (\Cref{app:block-rescaling}), so bringing it to order one needs $\zeta\propto N^{2/k}$, which is $N$ at second order, the order of every singular benchmark problem; hence $\zeta=N/2$. A double zero shrinks those states' quotient by a further $N^{-4}$ and needs $N^{4/k}$, the case of item~3 below. On several axes $\zeta_a=N_a$, weighted on an axis of second order so that the top-order terms balance, as $(cN,N)$ for the wave equation $f_{tt}=c^2f_{xx}$. The two constants, the $2$ and the $\tfrac12$, were selected by calibration on the benchmark problems, where the cost over a sweep of $\zeta$ is least, and then held fixed, never refitted; the check of the resulting weight against the best $\zeta$ on a grid is therefore in-sample and says nothing of a class of equation the set lacks. The cost stays within twice its least over a window of $\zeta$, so $\zeta$ need only be right to about a factor of two (\Cref{app:block-rescaling}). The two classes are asymptotic, and the weight jumps from one to the other while the equation changes continuously: a regular leading coefficient that dips close to zero behaves as a singular one once the resolution sees the dip, and there the regular weight loses, its cost on $(\delta+x^2)f''+f=0$ at $\delta=10^{-3}$ reaching $\res[2]{zeta-dip/delta=0.001/n5}{ratio}$ times the grid's best at $n=5$ and $\res[2]{zeta-dip/delta=0.001/n7}{ratio}$ times at $n=7$. Since the quotient already fixes how $\zeta$ must grow with $N$, deriving the weight from the equation, constants and crossover included, rather than calibrating it, should be possible; it is left to further work (\Cref{sec:future-work}).

On every one-axis problem the descriptor runs, the rescaled gap no longer falls with $N$, and the field block regains its weight. The gap is the physical subspace's, the one the filter meets, since the padding alone caps the whole spectrum's gap at one. At $n=7$ on R2c the gap rises from $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7/unscaled}{gap}$ to $\res[3]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7}{gap}$ and $w_f$ from $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7/unscaled}{field_weight}$ to $\res[3]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7}{field_weight}$. On R3a the rescaled gap rises from $\res[3]{build/R3a/descriptor/n5}{gap}$ at $n=5$ to $\res[3]{build/R3a/descriptor/n7}{gap}$ at $n=7$ and levels off, $\res[3]{build/R3a/descriptor/n10}{gap}$ at $n=10$, against $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$ unscaled.

%   build keys), x16 on R3a; a vanishing coefficient lowers the standard form's norm only where it
%   vanishes at both ends (R3). Item 1, tab:descriptor-regimes' caption and the summary scoped to that.
\paragraph{Remaining limits.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{The norm grows with $\zeta$.} Block $m$ carries $\zeta^m$, so $\|\Hdesc\|\gtrsim\zeta^{2k}\bar a_k^2$. A fixed $\zeta$ changes only the constant; the $\zeta\propto N$ of a simple zero gives $\|\Hdesc\|=\Theta(N^{2k})$ and, at a flat gap, a degree of about $\tilde\Theta(N^{2})$ at second order. A zero at both ends, where the Chebyshev polynomials' derivatives are largest, lowers $\|\Hstd\|$ to the same $N^4$ (R3a, coefficient $1-x^2$; \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian}), so there the descriptor gains no exponent at the ideal-encoding bound, only as built, where the structured encoding pays for the lifted multiplier in full; a zero at one end leaves $\|\Hstd\|$ at $N^8$ (R4a, coefficient $x-1$), and the lower exponent should survive the bound. \Cref{sec:results-scaling} measures both.
    \item \emph{Two axes.} The rescaled gap still falls with $N$, more slowly than unscaled: on R5b it is $\res[3]{build/R5b/descriptor/n4x4}{gap}$ at $n=4$ per axis and $\res[3]{build/R5b/descriptor/n5x5}{gap}$ at $n=5$, against the unscaled values of \Cref{sec:descriptor-gap}; \Cref{sec:results-gap} measures the same on Laplace's equation (R5a).
    \item \emph{A double zero is beyond any block rescaling.} If $a_k$ vanishes to second order at an endpoint, balancing the concentrated states needs $\zeta\propto N^{4/k}$, which at $k=2$ makes $\|\Hdesc\|=\Theta(N^8)$, the constant-coefficient standard form's norm, and the measurement in \Cref{app:block-rescaling} finds no $\zeta$ that brings the descriptor's cost level with the standard form's. R3c and R3d have this double zero, so both forms run the equivalent problem for $v=f/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ (\Cref{sec:standard-constraints}), whose zeros are simple; no claim is made for the double zero beyond that measurement.
\end{enumerate}
Block rescaling is the descriptor's counterpart of the ultraspherical method's diagonal preconditioner \cite{olver2013}, which bounds the condition number of its banded system; preconditioning a block-encoded system is established quantum practice \cite{clader2013preconditioned,tong2021fastinversion}.

\subsection{The Block Encoding}
\label{sec:descriptor-block-encoding}

A norm of $\Theta(N^2)$ lowers the degree only as far as the subnormalisation follows it, so the encoding must keep $\alpha_R$ within a small factor of $\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}\|_2$, or the encoder's cost of \Cref{sec:standard} returns. Apart from a source's rank-one term, every block of $\Adesc$ is a short sum of \emph{bands}, each a diagonal followed by a truncated shift, the device of the explicit block encodings of banded circulant matrices \cite{camps2024explicit}. Each diagonal is affine in the column index up to a few exceptions near the ends: $\Dt$'s is $2\Lambda$, with $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\dots,N-1)$ the index diagonal of \Cref{prop:G-factorisation}, and the others are constant. The affine part is $n+1$ Pauli-$Z$ terms (\Cref{eq:lambda-lcu}) and each exception one reflection $Q_m=I-2\ket m\bra m$ (\Cref{lem:affine-band}), so without a source $\Adesc$ is one flat \gls{lcu} of $\mathcal{O}(n)$ terms,
\begin{equation}
    \Adesc = \sum_{t=1}^{L} c_t\,U_t,
    \qquad
    U_t = P_t \otimes \mathrm{SHIFT}^{\ell_t}\,D_t,
    \qquad
    \alpha_A = \sum_{t=1}^{L} |c_t|,
    \label{eq:descriptor-lcu}
\end{equation}
where the block word $P_t$, a Pauli word $X^xZ^z$ on the block register or $X^x\Pi_c$, with $\Pi_c$ realised by a flag qubit, for a term that reads block $c$ alone, places the term's row and column blocks, $\mathrm{SHIFT}^{\ell_t}$ is the truncated shift by $\ell_t$, and $D_t$ is the identity, a $Z_j$ or a reflection. The truncated shift is not unitary on the axis register's $n$ qubits; it is applied as a cyclic addition on that register widened by one overflow qubit, where rows falling outside the interval land in the half that the final projection discards, so that qubit is the shift's only ancilla.

Two choices in the compilation set the cost of \Cref{fig:descriptor-lcu}. Bands whose axis factors agree are merged across the rows and columns they join, so the recurrences' common $\Dt$ is paid for once, not $k$ times. The selectors are factored, one for the block word and, on each axis, one for the diagonal and one for the shift, each only as wide as the distinct values its terms use, so SELECT applies each distinct operation once rather than once per term and the ancilla stay $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ (\Cref{sec:descriptor-cost}). Two pieces that R2 lacks enter apart: a source's rank-one term $\ket{\hat r}\bra{\tau(x_s)}$, compiled through the feature map and a rotation tree that loads $\hat r$ and added by one further combination, and a system's coupling, a sum of matchings (\Cref{sec:ext-circuits}). The algorithm, its compilation line by line and the check of the terms against the assembled rows are given in \Cref{app:term-ir}.

\subsection{Cost of the Descriptor Encoding}
\label{sec:descriptor-cost}

\begin{proposition}[Cost of the descriptor encoding]
\label{prop:descriptor-cost}
For a descriptor residual of a fixed problem, the \gls{lcu} of \Cref{eq:descriptor-lcu}, with a source's rank-one term added by one further combination, is an exact block encoding of the system rows (the recurrence, equation and padding rows) at $\alpha_A=\sum_t|c_t|$, with $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ ancilla. Without a source, or with a polynomial source of fixed degree, it has $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ gates; a general source adds $\mathcal{O}(N)$. For the second-order family R2, which has no source,
\begin{equation}
    \lceil \log_2(n+4) \rceil + 8\ \text{ancilla},
    \qquad
    \mathcal{O}(n^2)\ \text{gates} .
    \label{eq:descriptor-cost}
\end{equation}
For the mass form of a scalar first-order equation, and for the chain of a regular second-order problem at a fixed $\zeta\ge1$, $\alpha_A/\|\Adesc\|_2=1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})$ without a source and $1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})$ with one.
\end{proposition}

The ancilla are the selector registers, the overflow qubit and a flag, and only the diagonal selector's width depends on $n$: for R2 it indexes the identity, the $n$ gates $Z_j$ and three reflections, $\lceil\log_2(n+4)\rceil$ qubits. The gate count follows because each distinct operation is applied once and the shift's multi-controlled ladder costs $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$; a source's rotation tree loads its $N$ coefficients with $\mathcal{O}(N)$ gates, as for any general state, and with $\mathrm{poly}(n)$ when only a fixed number of them is non-zero. The subnormalisation tends to the norm because an affine diagonal whose end values share a sign is encoded at its own norm (\Cref{lem:affine-tight}), so the dominant terms, the recurrences' $\zeta^j\Dt$, contribute $2\zeta^{k-1}(N-1)$ to $\alpha_A$, which is also a lower bound on $\|\Adesc\|_2$, while every other term is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ in $N$ at fixed $\zeta$ except a source's rank-one term, which grows as $N^{1/2}$. The proofs are given in \Cref{app:proof-descriptor-cost}, and the ancilla count is checked on the compiled circuit of R2's system rows at $n=2$, $4$, $5$ and $12$.

The count is of the least order among exact \gls{lcu} encodings that expand their diagonals in Pauli strings, since $\Lambda$ alone has $n+1$ non-zero Walsh coefficients and a combination of $L$ distinct operations needs $\lceil\log_2L\rceil$ selector qubits \cite{chakraborty2025qsvt}; it is not claimed against encodings built otherwise, by arithmetic on the index for instance. The structured encoding of the standard form needs $n-1$ selector qubits for $\G$ alone, because it writes $\Uodd$ as one combination of its $N/2$ shifts (\Cref{sec:standard-cost}), so its ancilla grow as $n$ where the descriptor's grow as $\log n$, though an encoding of $\Uodd$ built otherwise is not excluded. At the sizes circuits reach the difference is not yet visible in the whole reflection's ancilla (\Cref{sec:results-scaling}).

The proposition concerns the system rows; for the whole stacked residual, constraints included, the ratio is computed exactly from the built circuit's $\alpha_R$ and the assembled residual's norm. It approaches one on regular problems, $\alpha_R/\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}\|_2=\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ on R2a at $n=8$. It stays near two on singular ones, $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R3a/descriptor/n7/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ on R3a at $n=7$, because $\zeta\Dt$, $\zeta^2\Bt$ and $\zeta^2a_2$ are then comparable and comparable terms add in $\ell^1$ even when each is tight. On two axes it is $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R5b/descriptor/n4x4/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ on R5b at $n=4$ per axis. In the terms of \Cref{sec:standard}, the encoder's cost $\alpha_H/\|H\|$ on R2a at $n=8$ is then $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{hamiltonian_ratio}$ for the descriptor against $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{hamiltonian_ratio}$ for the structured standard form, which is why an as-built comparison favours the descriptor (\Cref{sec:pipeline-design}).

The gates per query need not follow the subnormalisation. A descriptor query applies more terms than a structured standard-form query, through a block register, so on constant-coefficient problems its advantage is expected in the filter's degree rather than in the gates per query; on a lifted variable coefficient, which the structured encoding pays for in full, its query can be the cheaper too. \Cref{sec:results-scaling} counts both on the rescaled circuit.

\subsection{Consequences for the Spectrum and the Filter}
\label{sec:descriptor-spectrum}

Every block of the rescaled descriptor is $\mathcal{O}(N)$ at fixed $\zeta$, the recurrences' $\zeta^j\Dt$ being the largest, and the block $\zeta^{k-1}\Dt$ alone has norm $2\zeta^{k-1}(N-1)$, so $\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}\|_2=\Theta(N)$ and $\|\Hdesc\|=\Theta(N^2)$ whatever the order $k$: the derivative is banded, and its norm is that of $\Dt$ rather than of $\G^k$. Through \Cref{eq:degree}, with an encoding whose subnormalisation stays within a constant of that norm, the gap of \Cref{thm:flat-gap} gives, for a regular problem,
\begin{equation}
    \|\Hdesc\| = \Theta(N^2),
    \qquad
    \Delta = \Theta(1)
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    d = \tilde\Theta(N),
    \label{eq:descriptor-degree}
\end{equation}
against $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ for the standard form with the best exact encoding (\Cref{eq:standard-wall}). On a singular problem and on two axes the rescaling's weights raise the norm, and the degree is about $\tilde\Theta(N^2)$ or grows more steeply; for a double zero no claim is made (\Cref{tab:descriptor-regimes}). Neither form is polylogarithmic in $N$: $\tilde\Theta(N)$ is exponential in $n$, and the claim is a lower exponent only. The degrees are the minimax edge filter's, the least of any polynomial filter of the walk, with the hedge of \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian} for other algorithms; \Cref{sec:results-scaling} measures them, as built and at the ideal-encoding bound, and \Cref{sec:results-gap} the gaps.

On a regular problem the relative gap $\Delta/\|H\|$ is $\Theta(N^{-2})$ in the descriptor against $\Theta(N^{-4k})$ in the standard form: on R2a at $n=8$ the descriptor's is $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/descriptor/n8}{relative_gap}$ and the standard form's $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/n8}{relative_gap}$, below double precision. \subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:descriptor-summary}

Carrying the derivatives instead of eliminating them makes the residual banded with its solution unchanged, at a price.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The chain, for order two and above, and the mass form, for first-order equations and systems, keep the banded factors of $\G$; the residual is banded apart from the rank-one constraint rows, and its kernel is the standard form's (\Cref{thm:kernel-equivalence}).
    \item Its block encoding is one flat \gls{lcu} with $\lceil\log_2(n+4)\rceil+8$ ancilla for R2's system rows, of the least order for an exact \gls{lcu} that expands its diagonals in Pauli strings, with $\alpha_A/\|\Adesc\|_2\to1$ on regular scalar problems of order one and two (\Cref{prop:descriptor-cost}). It pays with the chain's block register and, expected on constant-coefficient problems, more gates per query.
    \item For a regular problem, $\|\Hdesc\|=\Theta(N^2)$ and $\Delta=\Theta(1)$ (\Cref{thm:flat-gap}), so the filter's degree is $\tilde\Theta(N)$ against the standard form's $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ for constant coefficients. Neither is polylogarithmic.
    \item \emph{The limit.} The carried derivatives shrink the gap where the solution's derivatives are large, and make it fall with $N$ on singular problems and on two axes. Block rescaling restores it on one axis without ancilla, at the price of a larger norm on a singular problem; on two axes the gap still falls, and a double zero is beyond any block rescaling.
\end{enumerate}
All of this concerns one real scalar field and a linear equation, the scope of Wu et al.'s construction. Coupled fields, complex-valued fields and nonlinear terms each change what the residual must carry, and the nonlinear route Wu et al.\ propose gives the filter no unique ground state to find. \Cref{sec:extensions} takes the reformulation there; \Cref{sec:results-scaling,sec:results-gap} test the cost and the gap on the benchmark problems.
