In the standard form the state holds the field alone, and every derivative is eliminated through the dense differentiation matrix $\G$, so that a term $f^{(j)}$ acts as $\G^j$ on the field's coefficients. \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian} found the cost of that form in its operator: with the encoding already tight, $\|\G^k\|_2=\Theta(N^{2k})$ makes $\|\Hstd\|=\Theta(N^{4k})$ for constant coefficients, and the filter's degree grows as $N^{2k}$ against a gap that stays flat. A lower degree therefore needs a different residual, one whose Hamiltonian is banded and well conditioned, whose kernel is still the solution, and whose price, in the circuit, the state and the gap, can be stated. Whether such a residual exists, and what it costs, is the second question of \Cref{sec:pihm-assessment}. The descriptor form answers it: its norm falls to $\Theta(N^2)$ at fixed block weights, and its gap, flat in the standard form, becomes the quantity the reformulation must defend.

\subsection{The Idea}
\label{sec:descriptor-idea}

The density of $\G$ is a property of the representation, familiar from the dense, ill-conditioned matrices of the tau and collocation methods \cite{olver2013}: it arises because the derivative is required to be expanded in the same basis as the function. If the derivative may instead live in a neighbouring polynomial basis, differentiation is banded, and $\G$ factors into two banded matrices whose product is dense only because one of them is inverted. We therefore change the form in which the equation is written and leave the equation, its data and its solution as they were.

The descriptor form keeps the factors instead of the product. Where the standard form substitutes $\G^j$ for each derivative, the descriptor form carries $f',\dots,f^{(k)}$ as unknowns beside $f$, ties consecutive derivatives by banded recurrences, and writes the equation over all of them. A first-order equation or system needs no extra unknowns, because the equation itself gives the derivative: multiplying the standard rows through by one banded factor is enough. We call the first layout the \emph{chain} and the second the \emph{mass form} (\Cref{sec:descriptor-layouts}).

Neither ingredient is new to numerical analysis. Banded differentiation is the ultraspherical spectral method of Olver and Townsend \cite{olver2013}, of which the factors of \Cref{sec:descriptor-construction} are the discrete shadow (\Cref{sec:bg-ultraspherical}). Carrying derivatives as unknowns so as not to square the condition number of a least-squares functional is the idea of first-order-system least squares \cite{cai1994fosls} (\Cref{sec:bg-fosls}). The physics-informed Hamiltonian is a least-squares functional, so both apply to it. The contribution of this thesis is their transfer to a block-encoded physics-informed Hamiltonian: a proof that the kernel is unchanged, an encoding of the residual as one flat linear combination of unitaries, an a priori rescaling that restores the gap the new unknowns erode, and a measurement of what all of this does to ground-state preparation.

\Cref{fig:sparsity} shows the effect on the running example $f''+4f'+4f=0$ (R2a). At $n=7$ the standard form's stacked residual holds \res{build/R2a/standard/n7}{nonzeros} non-zero entries on \res{build/R2a/standard/n7}{dimension} unknowns, and the descriptor's holds \res{build/R2a/descriptor/n7}{nonzeros} on \res{build/R2a/descriptor/n7}{dimension}: four times as many unknowns, because of the block register that \Cref{sec:descriptor-layouts} counts, and six times fewer entries. Apart from the rank-one constraint rows, which both forms share, the descriptor's residual is banded. Carrying the intermediate derivatives instead of eliminating them turns the residual from dense to banded with its kernel unchanged, so that the Hamiltonian's norm falls from $\Theta(N^{4k})$, which is $\Theta(N^8)$ at second order, to $\Theta(N^2)$ at fixed block weights; the price is a block register and a gap that the derivative blocks erode, which an a priori rescaling restores on one axis at no gate cost.

\subsection{The Banded Factorisation}
\label{sec:descriptor-construction}

The Chebyshev coefficients of a function and of its derivative are tied by a three-term recurrence, each row of which ties one coefficient of the function to two of the derivative; the recurrence and its derivation are given in \Cref{app:cheb}. Truncated to $N$ coefficients and written in the normalised basis of \Cref{sec:standard-model}, with $\fhat{0}$ and $\fhat{1}$ the coefficient vectors of $f$ and $f'$, it is the matrix identity
\begin{equation}
    \Bt\,\fhat{1} = \Dt\,\fhat{0} .
    \label{eq:banded-factorisation}
\end{equation}
$\Dt$ has a single non-zero diagonal, the first superdiagonal, with entries $(2\sqrt2,4,6,\dots,2(N-1))$; $\Bt$ has the diagonal $(2,1,1,\dots)$ and the second superdiagonal $(-\sqrt2,-1,-1,\dots)$, the irregular first entries coming from the normalisation of $T_0$. Both hold $\mathcal{O}(N)$ non-zero entries against the $N^2/4$ of $\G$. Two properties are used throughout: $\Bt$ is upper triangular with a non-zero diagonal, so it is invertible; and since the singular values of a single-diagonal matrix are its entries' magnitudes, $\|\Dt\|_2=2(N-1)$, while $\|\Bt\|_2\le2+\sqrt2$.

The dense differentiation matrix is therefore the quotient
\begin{equation}
    \G = \Bt^{-1}\Dt ,
    \label{eq:G-banded}
\end{equation}
exactly at every $N$ (\Cref{app:cheb}). Away from its first row, $\Bt^{-1}$ is a triangle of ones on each parity class, and the density of $\G$ is that inverse; \Cref{eq:banded-factorisation} is the same linear map before the inverse is taken. Up to the basis normalisation, $\Bt$ and $\Dt$ are twice the ultraspherical method's operators for conversion from first-kind to second-kind Chebyshev polynomials and for differentiation into the second kind \cite{olver2013} (\Cref{app:ultraspherical}). The ultraspherical method uses such operators to eliminate the derivatives, raising the basis once per derivative, and solves a banded system in $f$ alone. We instead keep \Cref{eq:banded-factorisation} as a row of the system and each derivative as an unknown, which lets one pair of factors serve every order, every axis and every coupled field. The method's own reduction of an equation of order $k\ge2$ to a banded operator on the $N$ coefficients of $f$ would also keep the standard form's kernel; it is not built here, and what it would change is stated in \Cref{app:ultraspherical}.

\subsection{Two Layouts and Kernel Equivalence}
\label{sec:descriptor-layouts}

Consider the linear \gls{ode} of order $k$
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{j=0}^{k} a_j(x)\, f^{(j)}(x) = r(x), \qquad x \in [-1,1],
\end{equation}
with the data of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints}, every function in it represented by its first $N$ coefficients in the normalised Chebyshev basis.

\paragraph{The chain ($k\ge2$).} Let $\fhat{j}\in\mathbb{C}^N$ be the coefficient vector of $f^{(j)}$ for $j=0,\dots,k$, stacked into the state
\begin{equation}
  w = (\fhat{0};\, \fhat{1};\, \dots;\, \fhat{k}).
\end{equation}
Two kinds of row couple the blocks. The \emph{recurrence rows}
\begin{equation}
  -\Dt\, \fhat{j} + \Bt\, \fhat{j+1} = 0, \qquad j = 0, \dots, k-1,
  \label{eq:descriptor-recurrence-rows}
\end{equation}
apply \Cref{eq:banded-factorisation} block by block. They contain no derivative operator, and on the kernel they are equivalent to $\fhat{j+1}=\G\fhat{j}$. The \emph{equation row} is the equation itself, $\sum_j\M_{a_j}\fhat{j}-\M_r\Dzero(x_s)\fhat{0}=0$, with $\M_{a_j}$ the multiplication operator of \Cref{sec:standard-operators}, lifted exactly where the standard form lifts it, and the source entering through the collapse row as in \Cref{eq:standard-equation}. The derivatives are unknowns of $w$ and are never replaced by $\G^j\fhat{0}$, so the dense quotient \Cref{eq:G-banded} never appears. The name is borrowed from descriptor systems in control theory \cite{luenberger1977descriptor,dai1989singular}, which likewise keep intermediate quantities as unknowns beside the state and tie them by algebraic rows.

\begin{definition}[Descriptor residual operator]
\label{def:descriptor-residual-operator}
Collecting the recurrence rows and the equation row, the descriptor residual operator $\Adesc$ is the block matrix
\begin{equation}
\Adesc =
\begin{pmatrix}
-\Dt & \Bt &        &        &          \\
          & -\Dt & \Bt &      &          \\
          &          & \ddots & \ddots &          \\
          &          &        & -\Dt & \Bt \\
\M_{a_0} - \M_r\Dzero & \M_{a_1}  & \cdots & \cdots & \M_{a_k}
\end{pmatrix}
\end{equation}
with $(k+1)N$ columns before padding, so that $\Adesc\,w=0$ is the equation.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{The padded block register.} On a quantum register the block index is held by $n_f=\lceil\log_2(k+1)\rceil$ qubits, which address $2^{n_f}$ blocks rather than $k+1$. The state is therefore padded to $2^{n_f}N$ amplitudes by blocks $b>k$, each pinned to zero by its own row $c_{\mathrm{pad}}\fhat{b}=0$ with $c_{\mathrm{pad}}=1$. No other row reads a padding block, so the padding decouples: the Hamiltonian is block diagonal, and the spectrum of the physical blocks is unaffected. The padding still costs state: the chain's state has $2^{n_f}N$ amplitudes against the standard form's $N$, which is $n_f$ more qubits and up to twice the unpadded $(k+1)N$ when $k+1$ is not a power of two: a second-order equation carries $f$, $f'$ and $f''$ in four blocks. The constraint rows of \Cref{sec:descriptor-constraints} are added to these block rows, and every row block, with the several-axis case and the padded count, is given in full in \Cref{app:descriptor-rows}.

\paragraph{The mass form ($k=1$).} A first-order system $z'=\Acoup z$ with $C$ components and a constant coupling matrix $\Acoup$, a single first-order equation being the case $C=1$, has in the standard form the rows $I_C\otimes\G-\Acoup\otimes I_N$, one block per component. The equation already determines each derivative, so a derivative block would carry nothing new; the mass form instead multiplies these rows on the left by $I_C\otimes\Bt$ and uses $\Bt\G=\Dt$,
\begin{equation}
    (I_C\otimes\Bt)\bigl(I_C\otimes\G-\Acoup\otimes I_N\bigr)
    = I_C\otimes\Dt-\Acoup\otimes\Bt .
    \label{eq:mass-form}
\end{equation}
Both factors are banded and neither contains $\G$. The name is taken from the \gls{ode} literature \cite{hairerwanner1996}, $\Bt$ playing the mass matrix of $\Bt\dot z=\dots$. For $f'+2f=0$ with $f(-1)=1$ (R1) the mass form is $\Dt+2\Bt$ on $N$ unknowns, where a chain would need $2N$ unknowns and elimination through $\G$ gives the dense $\G+2I$; all three are written out at $N=4$ in \Cref{app:cheb}. For a scalar equation with a constant coefficient the mass form is the ultraspherical method's own first-order operator. It has the standard form's state, initial state and readout, adds no qubits and has no derivative block to rescale (\Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}). Coupled fields and the Carleman lifts of \Cref{sec:carleman} are first-order systems with a constant coupling matrix, so in the descriptor form they take the mass form, which \Cref{sec:systems-fields} applies.


\begin{theorem}[Kernel equivalence]
\label{thm:kernel-equivalence}
Let $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ be the standard form's stacked residual (\Cref{def:standard-form}) of a linear problem on one axis. Let $R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ stack the descriptor's recurrence, equation and padding rows with the same constraint rows, each reading the block of the derivative it constrains (\Cref{sec:descriptor-constraints}), the equation row applying the standard form's multiplication operators. Let $R_{\mathrm{mass}}$ be the mass form's stacked residual of a first-order system with a constant coupling matrix. Then
\begin{equation}
    \ker R_{\mathrm{desc}} = \bigl\{\, (p,\ \G p,\ \dots,\ \G^{k} p,\ 0) \;:\; p \in \ker R_{\mathrm{std}} \,\bigr\},
    \qquad
    \ker R_{\mathrm{mass}} = \ker R_{\mathrm{std}} .
    \label{eq:kernel-equivalence}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Write $Ep=(p;\G p;\dots;\G^kp;0)$. Chain: the padding rows force the padding blocks of a kernel vector $w$ to zero, and since $\Bt$ is invertible each recurrence row gives $w_{j+1}=\Bt^{-1}\Dt\,w_j=\G w_j$, so $w=Ew_0$. The recurrence rows vanish on $Ep$ for every $p$, because $(\Bt\G-\Dt)\G^jp=0$, and every other row of $R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ acts on $Ep$ as the standard form's row of the same name acts on $p$: the equation row gives $\sum_j\M_{a_j}\G^jp-\M_r\Dzero(x_s)p$, the standard form's equation block, and a constraint row reads the block that holds $\G^jp$. Hence $R_{\mathrm{desc}}Ep$ vanishes exactly when $R_{\mathrm{std}}p$ does, and $E$ is injective because its first block is the identity. Mass form: $R_{\mathrm{mass}}$ is $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ with its equation rows multiplied on the left by the invertible $I_C\otimes\Bt$ (\Cref{eq:mass-form}) and its constraint rows unchanged.
\end{proof}

The theorem holds on several axes, with $E$ carrying $\G_a^{j}$ along each axis's chain, and for the block-rescaled residual of \Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}, whose kernel is the image of this one under a diagonal map; both extensions are proved in \Cref{app:proof-kernel-equivalence}.

An exact kernel exists only where the solution lies in the span of the basis and meets every constraint exactly, as Legendre's $P_4$ does on R3a; otherwise both residuals have full rank, and their ground states are the smallest right singular vectors of different matrices. The descriptor measures a state's residual against a norm that carries its derivatives (\Cref{sec:descriptor-gap}), and the mass form weights the equation rows by $\Bt$. We therefore claim equal kernels only. Both decoded fields converge to the solution, at rates measured in \Cref{sec:results-faithful} and not proved; at high resolution the computed singular vector's rounding error grows with the residual's norm relative to its gap, $\Theta(N^{2k})$ in the standard form against $\Theta(N)$ here (\Cref{sec:descriptor-spectrum}), which favours the descriptor's reference there.

\subsection{Constraints and Partial Differential Equations}
\label{sec:descriptor-constraints}

A data constraint sits on the block that holds the quantity it constrains. A Dirichlet condition $f(x_z)=0$ reads block~$0$ with the zero row $\Brow(x_z)$, as in the standard form. A Neumann condition $f'(x_m)=0$ reads block~$1$ with the same row, $\Brow(x_m)\fhat{1}=0$, where the standard form must write $\Brow(x_m)\G$. The regular datum reads block~$0$, so the field is decoded and scaled exactly as in the standard form (\Cref{sec:standard-model}). A variable coefficient and a source enter the equation row as they enter the standard form's equation block, so the two forms differ only in how the derivatives are carried.

On several axes the field is block~$0$, and each axis $a$ owns a chain $\partial_af,\dots,\partial_a^{k_a}f$ up to the highest order that the equation or a constraint takes along it, tied by that axis's factors $\Bt_a$ and $\Dt_a$ tensored with the identity on the other axes. A mixed derivative would need a block of its own; the construction does not build one, which limits it to equations without mixed derivatives. Initial and boundary functions enter both forms as the datum slices of \Cref{eq:datum-slice}, read on the field block. The descriptor pays a larger state here: the heat equation ($k_t=1$, $k_x=2$) carries four blocks, and Laplace's and the wave equation ($k=2$ on each axis) five, padded to eight, so their states are four and eight times the standard form's, two and three more qubits.

\subsection{The Descriptor's Gap}
\label{sec:descriptor-gap}

The kernel is the standard form's; the spectrum above it is not, because the descriptor's state carries every derivative and its norm counts them all. The gap, which the standard form keeps flat on the problems of \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian} while its norm grows, is therefore where the reformulation can lose what its norm has gained. With $E$ the map of \Cref{thm:kernel-equivalence}, the descriptor's Rayleigh quotient on the states that satisfy the recurrences is
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}Ep\|^2}{\|Ep\|^2}
    = \frac{\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\,p\|^2}{\sum_{m=0}^{k}\|\G^{m}p\|^2},
    \label{eq:descriptor-quotient}
\end{equation}
because the recurrence and padding rows vanish on $Ep$ and the remaining rows act as the standard form's. By the min--max principle, restricting the trial states to the range of $E$ can only raise the quotient, so the descriptor's singular values are bounded above by the generalised singular values of the pair $(R_{\mathrm{std}},E)$ \cite{vanloan1976gsvd}. The denominator carries every derivative, so the gap can be flat in $N$ only where the equation controls each carried derivative: where no state $p$ independent of the solution has a residual small against its largest carried derivative. The bound is an upper one in two senses. It does not prove flatness, and states that break the recurrences can lie below it, since the least singular value of $\Bt$ falls with $N$, its vector at the ends of the interval (\Cref{app:descriptor-gap}). The gap is therefore measured.

On a one-axis equation whose leading coefficient has no zero on $[-1,1]$, the continuum a priori estimate $\|f^{(k)}\|\le(\|\sum_ja_jf^{(j)}\|+\|\sum_{j<k}a_jf^{(j)}\|)/\min|a_k|$ suggests that the quotient stays bounded away from zero on the complement of the kernel, and the measured gap, without rescaling, is flat: on R2a it is $\res[3]{build/R2a/descriptor/n3/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=3$ and $\res[3]{build/R2a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=10$. The norm weighs block $m$ by the size of $f^{(m)}$, so where the solution's derivatives are large the derivative blocks dominate it, and the ground state's weight on the field block, $w_f=\|\fhat{0}\|^2/\|w\|^2$, is small; for an exact kernel it is $\|b\|^2/\sum_m\|\G^mb\|^2$, with $b$ the solution's coefficients. An initial state prepared on the field block then overlaps the ground state by $\gamma\le w_f^{1/2}$, and amplification costs $1/\gamma$. On the stiff equation $f''+5f'+400f=0$ (R2c, Wu et al.'s Fig.~3d), whose solution oscillates at an angular frequency near $20$, the gap is $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n5/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=5$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=10$, flat and small, and $w_f$ is $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{field_weight}$, against $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{field_weight}$ on R2a.

The gap falls with $N$ in two cases.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{A leading coefficient that vanishes.} A polynomial of degree $N$ can concentrate within $\mathcal{O}(N^{-2})$ of an endpoint, where the Chebyshev points cluster, and each of its derivatives grows by a factor $\mathcal{O}(N^{2})$ there, the extremal behaviour of Markov's inequality \cite{borweinerdelyi1995}. Where $a_k$ vanishes at that endpoint, the leading term $a_kp^{(k)}$ of such a state is small, the lower-order terms are small as well, and the quotient is small against the top block. The measured gap falls as about $N^{-4}$: on Legendre's equation $(1-x^2)f''-2xf'+20f=0$ (R3a), whose leading coefficient vanishes at both ends, it is $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n5/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=5$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=10$, and on $(x-1)f''-xf'+f=(x-1)^2$ (R4a, Wu et al.'s Fig.~5a), whose leading coefficient vanishes at $x=1$, $\res[2,sci]{build/R4a/descriptor/n5/unscaled}{gap}$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R4a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$. At an interior zero, where the Chebyshev resolution is $\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})$, the same argument, with Bernstein's inequality in place of Markov's \cite{borweinerdelyi1995}, gives $N^{-2}$; no benchmark problem has one, and the rate is measured on a controlled equation in \Cref{app:descriptor-gap}.
    \item \emph{Two axes.} The equation constrains only a sum of top derivatives, $f_{xx}+f_{yy}$ or $f_t-\kappa f_{xx}$. A high-frequency state with $\G_x^2p\approx-\G_y^2p$, or with its weight in the block the equation weights least, has a small residual against large blocks, and only the datum rows penalise it. On the heat equation $\partial_tf-\tfrac{1}{25}\partial_x^2f=0$ (R5b, Wu et al.'s Fig.~6d) the gap is $\res[2,sci]{build/R5b/descriptor/n3x3/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=3$ per axis and $\res[2,sci]{build/R5b/descriptor/n5x5/unscaled}{gap}$ at $n=5$, where the standard form's is $\res[3]{build/R5b/standard/n3x3}{gap}$ and $\res[3]{build/R5b/standard/n5x5}{gap}$.
\end{enumerate}
The collapse is not an artefact of the lift, the padding, the arithmetic precision, the constraints, the basis or the classical reference: each was tested and ruled out, and a descriptor built in the ultraspherical basis collapses at the same rates where the leading coefficient vanishes. The tests and the full mechanism are given in \Cref{app:descriptor-gap}.

\subsection{Block Rescaling}
\label{sec:descriptor-rescaling}

The remedy is to rescale the carried derivatives so that no block outweighs the equation's own scale. The block of order $m$ is carried divided by $\zeta^m$,
\begin{equation}
    \fhat{m} \;\longmapsto\; \zeta^{-m}\,\fhat{m},
    \qquad m = 0, \dots, k ,
    \label{eq:block-rescaling}
\end{equation}
which replaces $R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ by $R_{\mathrm{desc}}S$, with $S$ diagonal, equal to $\zeta^m$ on block $m$ and to one on the padding blocks. Because $S$ is invertible, the kernel becomes $S^{-1}\ker R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ and \Cref{thm:kernel-equivalence} survives. The field block has weight one, so the readout is unchanged, and the padding keeps its weight and still decouples. What changes is the Hamiltonian, $SR_{\mathrm{desc}}^\dagger R_{\mathrm{desc}}S$. This is a congruence, so the spectrum moves: by \Cref{eq:descriptor-quotient} the denominator becomes $\sum_m\zeta^{-2m}\|\G^mp\|^2$, and $\zeta$ sets how much each carried derivative weighs against the residual. From here on $R_{\mathrm{desc}}$ and $\Hdesc$ denote the rescaled residual and its Hamiltonian, and the residual of \Cref{sec:descriptor-gap} is called \emph{unscaled}.

In the circuit the rescaling adds nothing. Scaling the columns of block $m$ by $\zeta^m$ multiplies the coefficient of every term of the block encoding of \Cref{sec:descriptor-block-encoding} by $\zeta$ raised to the order of its column block, which adds no gate and no ancilla and changes only the subnormalisation. A general diagonal equilibration \cite{vandersluis1969}, with a different weight for each amplitude, would need a diagonal block encoding of its own at $\Theta(\dim w)$ gates; that is why the weights are constant on each block, and general equilibration appears in no circuit in this thesis.

\paragraph{The a priori rule.} $\zeta$ is computed from the equation's coefficients and the resolution, never from a solution or a spectrum. For a one-axis equation whose leading coefficient $a_k$ has no zero on $[-1,1]$,
\begin{equation}
    \zeta = 2\,\bigl(\bar a_j/\bar a_k\bigr)^{1/(k-j)},
    \label{eq:zeta-rule}
\end{equation}
with $j$ the lowest order present and $\bar a$ the mean of $|a|$ over the interval. For constant coefficients and $j=0$, $(\bar a_0/\bar a_k)^{1/k}$ is the geometric mean of the magnitudes of the characteristic roots: the scale at which a solution $e^{\lambda x}$ has $\|f^{(m)}\|\approx|\lambda|^m\|f\|$, so that dividing block $m$ by its $m$-th power balances the blocks at the equation's own scale and returns the field block's weight to order one. Where $a_k$ has a zero on the interval, $\zeta=N/2$, which raises the quotient of the concentrated states of \Cref{sec:descriptor-gap} to order one. On several axes $\zeta_a=N_a$, weighted on an axis of second order so that the top-order terms balance across axes: the wave equation $f_{tt}=c^2f_{xx}$, with wave speed $c$, takes $(cN,N)$. Whether $a_k$ vanishes is read from the equation.

The rule's two constants, the $2$ in \Cref{eq:zeta-rule} and the $\tfrac12$ in $N/2$, were set once from a sweep of $\zeta$ over the benchmark problems and are never refitted to a problem. The check of the rule against the cheapest $\zeta$ on a grid is therefore in-sample: it shows that one rule serves the whole benchmark set, within a small factor of the best weight, and says nothing about a class of equation the set does not contain. The cost is flat over a window of $\zeta$, so $\zeta$ need only be right to about a factor of two; the sweep, the window and the check are given in \Cref{app:block-rescaling}.

\paragraph{What it restores.} On every one-axis problem the descriptor runs, the rescaled gap no longer falls with $N$, and the field block regains its weight. The gap meant here is that of the physical subspace, the one the filter meets, since the padding alone would cap the gap of the whole spectrum at one. On R2c the gap rises from $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7/unscaled}{gap}$ to $\res[3]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7}{gap}$ and $w_f$ from $\res[2,sci]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7/unscaled}{field_weight}$ to $\res[3]{build/R2c/descriptor/n7}{field_weight}$ at $n=7$. On R3a the rescaled gap is $\res[3]{build/R3a/descriptor/n5}{gap}$ at $n=5$ and $\res[3]{build/R3a/descriptor/n10}{gap}$ at $n=10$, against $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/descriptor/n10/unscaled}{gap}$ unscaled: it rises with $n$ and levels off.

\paragraph{What it does not remove.} Three limits remain, each a finding.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \emph{The norm grows with $\zeta$.} Block $m$ carries $\zeta^m$, so $\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}\|\gtrsim\zeta^k\bar a_k$ and $\|\Hdesc\|\gtrsim\zeta^{2k}$. With $\zeta$ constant this costs nothing. With $\zeta\propto N$, which a simple zero requires, $\|\Hdesc\|=\Theta(N^{2k})$, and at a flat gap the degree at second order is about $\tilde\Theta(N^{2})$. Whether this is an advantage over the standard form depends on where the coefficient vanishes. A coefficient that vanishes at both ends of the interval, where the derivatives of the Chebyshev polynomials are largest, lowers the standard form's norm too: on R3a, whose coefficient is $1-x^2$, $\|\Hstd\|$ grows as $N^4$ where $N^8$ is the constant-coefficient law (\Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian}). One that vanishes at one end only does not: on R4a, whose coefficient is $x-1$, $\|\Hstd\|$ keeps the $N^8$ law. On R3, then, both norms grow as $N^4$ and the comparison at the ideal-encoding bound can go either way, while as built the structured encoding pays for the lifted multiplier in full; on R4a the descriptor's lower norm should hold at the bound as well. \Cref{sec:results-scaling} measures both, and reports the reversal it finds on R3 as a finding.
    \item \emph{Two axes.} The rescaled gap still falls with $N$, though far more slowly than unscaled: on R5b it is $\res[3]{build/R5b/descriptor/n3x3}{gap}$ at $n=3$ per axis and $\res[3]{build/R5b/descriptor/n5x5}{gap}$ at $n=5$, and on Laplace's equation (R5a) $\res[3]{build/R5a/descriptor/n3x3}{gap}$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R5a/descriptor/n5x5}{gap}$, against $\res[2,sci]{build/R5a/descriptor/n3x3/unscaled}{gap}$ and $\res[2,sci]{build/R5a/descriptor/n5x5/unscaled}{gap}$ unscaled.
    \item \emph{A double zero is beyond any block rescaling.} If $a_k$ vanishes to second order at an endpoint, the concentrated states have quotient $\mathcal{O}(N^{-8}\zeta^{2k})$, which is the unscaled $N^{-8}$ at $\zeta=1$, so balancing them needs $\zeta\propto N^{4/k}$. At $k=2$ that is $\zeta\propto N^2$, at which $\|\Hdesc\|=\Theta(N^8)$: the norm of a second-order standard form with constant coefficients, and more than the standard form's own norm on such a problem, which the vanishing coefficient lowers. Wu et al.'s associated Legendre examples R3c and R3d (their Figs~4c and~4d) multiply the operator by $1-x^2$ and so have exactly this double zero. Both forms instead run the equivalent problem for $v=f/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints}, whose leading coefficient has simple zeros, and we make no claim for the double zero beyond the measurement on Wu et al.'s form in \Cref{app:block-rescaling}.
\end{enumerate}
Block rescaling is the descriptor's counterpart of the diagonal preconditioner of the ultraspherical method \cite{olver2013}, which bounds the condition number of its banded system, and preconditioning a block-encoded system is established quantum practice \cite{clader2013preconditioned,tong2021fastinversion}.

\subsection{The Block Encoding}
\label{sec:descriptor-block-encoding}

A norm of $\Theta(N^2)$ lowers the degree only as far as the circuit's subnormalisation follows it, so the descriptor's encoding must keep $\alpha_R$ within a small factor of $\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}\|_2$, or the encoder's cost of \Cref{sec:standard} returns. Every block of $\Adesc$ is a short sum of \emph{bands}, each a diagonal followed by a truncated shift, the device used for the explicit block encodings of banded circulant matrices \cite{camps2024explicit}. Write $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(0,1,\dots,N-1)$ for the index diagonal of \Cref{prop:G-factorisation}. The diagonal of $\Dt$ is $2\Lambda$ except at column~1, where the normalisation of $T_0$ makes it $2\sqrt2$; the diagonals of $\Bt$ and of a constant multiplication are constant except at their ends; and a variable coefficient's multiplication and its truncation at the edge of the register contribute a few further exceptions. An affine diagonal $aI+b\Lambda$ is a sum of $n+1$ Pauli-$Z$ terms, and each exception is one reflection $Q_m$:
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda = \frac{N-1}{2}\,I-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1} 2^{j-1}\,Z_j,
    \qquad
    Q_m = I - 2\ket{m}\bra{m} .
    \label{eq:diag-index}
\end{equation}
$\Adesc$ is therefore one flat \gls{lcu} of $\mathcal{O}(n)$ terms,
\begin{equation}
    \Adesc = \sum_{t=1}^{L} c_t\,U_t,
    \qquad
    U_t = (\text{field word}) \otimes \mathrm{SHIFT}^{\ell_t}\,P_t,
    \qquad
    \alpha_A = \sum_{t=1}^{L} |c_t|,
    \label{eq:descriptor-lcu}
\end{equation}
where the field word, a Pauli word on the block register, places the term's row and column blocks, $\mathrm{SHIFT}^{\ell_t}$ is the truncated shift by $\ell_t$, and $P_t$ is the identity, a $Z_j$ or a reflection. An affine diagonal whose end values $a$ and $a+b(N-1)$ share a sign is encoded tightly, at $\alpha=\max(|a|,|a+b(N-1)|)=\|aI+b\Lambda\|_2$, so the dominant term $\Dt$ is encoded at its own norm $2(N-1)$ and its exception costs $\mathcal{O}(1)$. A truncated shift of the $N$-dimensional register is not unitary; it is applied on the register widened by one overflow qubit, so that rows falling outside the interval land in the half that the final projection discards. That qubit is the shift's only ancilla.

\Cref{alg:ir-to-lcu} compiles the terms into the circuit of \Cref{fig:descriptor-lcu}.

\placeholderfigure[6cm][The descriptor residual as one LCU circuit]{\textbf{The whole descriptor residual is one preparation, one SELECT and one unpreparation.} PREPARE$_R$ loads the signed amplitudes $\operatorname{sgn}(c_t)\sqrt{|c_t|/\alpha_A}$ over the joint index of the factored selector registers, and PREPARE$_L^\dagger$ unloads the unsigned $\sqrt{|c_t|/\alpha_A}$, so that the signs of the terms survive. SELECT applies each term's field word on the block register ($n_f$ qubits), then on the system register ($n$ qubits, widened by one overflow qubit) its diagonal, a $Z$-string or a reflection, and its truncated shift. Post-selecting the selectors, the overflow qubit and the flag on $\ket{0}$ leaves the system rows of $\Adesc$ divided by $\alpha_A$, with the block rescaling carried entirely in the coefficients $c_t$.}{fig:descriptor-lcu}{Quantikz circuit: selector registers (PREPARE$_R$ loads $\operatorname{sgn}(c_t)\sqrt{|c_t|/\alpha_A}$ over their joint index), field register ($n_f$ qubits), system register ($n$ qubits) widened by one overflow qubit; SELECT applies each term's field word, diagonal ($Z$-string or reflection) and truncated shift (a ladder of multi-controlled-$X$ gates); PREPARE$_L^\dagger$ unloading $\sqrt{|c_t|/\alpha_A}$; post-selection on the selectors, the overflow and the flag.}

\begin{algorithm}[htbp]
    \caption{Compiling the rows of $\Adesc$ into two preparations and one SELECT.}
    \label{alg:ir-to-lcu}
    \KwIn{the blocks of $\Adesc$ as bands, the block weights $\zeta$}
    \KwOut{PREPARE$_R$, SELECT and PREPARE$_L^\dagger$; the subnormalisation $\alpha_A$}
    Write every block as a sum of bands: a diagonal and a truncated shift\;
    Fit each diagonal as affine in the column index plus a few exceptions, \Cref{eq:diag-index}\;
    Merge terms with equal axis factors across blocks; expand the resulting block-register pattern in Pauli words\;
    Multiply the coefficient of each term by $\zeta^{m}$ for its column block $m$\;
    $\alpha_A \leftarrow \sum_{t} |c_t|$; on the joint selector index of term $t$, PREPARE$_R$ loads $\operatorname{sgn}(c_t)\sqrt{|c_t|/\alpha_A}$ and PREPARE$_L$ loads $\sqrt{|c_t|/\alpha_A}$\;
    SELECT: controlled on each selector register, apply the field word, then on each axis the diagonal and the shift\;
    \Return{PREPARE$_R$, SELECT, PREPARE$_L^\dagger$, $\alpha_A$}\;
\end{algorithm}

Two choices in it set the cost. Line 3 merges terms whose axis factors agree across the rows and columns they join, so the recurrences' common $\Dt$ is paid for once and not $k$ times. And the selectors are factored, one register for the field operation and, on each axis, one for the diagonal and one for the shift, so that SELECT applies each distinct operation once rather than once per term, which keeps the ancilla at $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ (\Cref{sec:descriptor-cost}). The rank-one rows are encoded through the feature map, with a rotation tree loading a source's coefficients, so no dense state preparation appears anywhere. The compilation line by line, and the check of the terms against the assembled rows, are given in \Cref{app:term-ir}.

\subsection{Cost of the Descriptor Encoding}
\label{sec:descriptor-cost}

\begin{proposition}[Cost of the descriptor encoding]
\label{prop:descriptor-cost}
For a descriptor residual of a fixed problem, the flat \gls{lcu} of \Cref{eq:descriptor-lcu} is an exact block encoding of the system rows (the recurrence, equation and padding rows) at $\alpha_A=\sum_t|c_t|$, with $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ ancilla and $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ gates. For the second-order family R2,
\begin{equation}
    \lceil \log_2(n+4) \rceil + 8\ \text{ancilla},
    \qquad
    \mathcal{O}(n^2)\ \text{gates} .
    \label{eq:descriptor-cost}
\end{equation}
For the mass form of a scalar first-order equation, and for a second-order chain with polynomial coefficients whose leading coefficient has no zero on $[-1,1]$, at a fixed $\zeta\ge1$, $\alpha_A/\|\Adesc\|_2=1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1})$ without a source and $1+\mathcal{O}(N^{-1/2})$ with one.
\end{proposition}

The ancilla are the selector registers, the overflow qubit and a flag. Only the diagonal selector's width depends on $n$: for R2 it indexes the identity, the $n$ gates $Z_j$ and three reflections ($\Bt$'s diagonal and second superdiagonal and the first entry of $\Dt$), so it has $\lceil\log_2(n+4)\rceil$ qubits, while the shift selector holds the offsets $0$, $1$ and $2$ on two qubits and the field selector has four. The gate count follows because each distinct operation is applied once and the shift's multi-controlled ladder costs $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$. The subnormalisation tends to the norm because the dominant terms, the recurrences' $\zeta^j\Dt$, share one pattern on the block register that its Pauli expansion encodes at its largest entry, so their contribution to $\alpha_A$ is $2\zeta^{k-1}(N-1)$, which is also a lower bound on $\|\Adesc\|_2$; every other term is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ in $N$ at fixed $\zeta$, except a source's rank-one term, whose coefficient vector grows as $N^{1/2}$. The proofs are given in \Cref{app:proof-descriptor-cost}, and the ancilla count is verified for R2's system rows at $n=2$, $4$, $5$ and $12$.

The ancilla count is what expanding the diagonals in Pauli strings forces, and no more. A combination whose SELECT indexes $L$ distinct operations needs $\lceil\log_2L\rceil$ selector qubits \cite{chakraborty2025qsvt}, and the index diagonal $\Lambda$ has exactly $n+1$ non-zero Walsh coefficients, so an \gls{lcu} that expands it in Pauli-$Z$ strings has at least $n+1$ terms and $\Omega(\log n)$ selector qubits. The count is therefore of the least order among exact \gls{lcu} encodings that expand their diagonals in Pauli strings; it is not claimed against encodings built otherwise, by arithmetic on the index for instance. The structured encoding of the standard form needs $n-1$ selector qubits for $\G$ alone, because it writes $\Uodd$ as one combination of its $N/2$ shifts (\Cref{sec:standard-cost}), so its ancilla grow as $n$ where the descriptor's grow as $\log n$; the descriptor never forms that sum, though an encoding of $\Uodd$ that is not such a combination is not excluded. The better scaling is not yet visible at the sizes that circuits reach, where the whole reflection's ancilla are counted in \Cref{sec:results-scaling}.

The proposition concerns the system rows; for the whole stacked residual, constraints included, the ratio is computed exactly from the built circuit's $\alpha_R$ and the assembled residual's norm. It approaches one on regular one-axis problems, $\alpha_R/\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}\|_2=\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ on R2a at $n=8$. It stays near two where the leading coefficient vanishes, $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R3a/descriptor/n7/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ on R3a at $n=7$, because $\zeta\Dt$, $\zeta^2\Bt$ and $\zeta^2a_2$ are then comparable and comparable terms add in $\ell^1$ even when each is tight. On two axes it is $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R5b/descriptor/n4x4/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ on R5b at $n=4$ per axis. The structured standard-form encoding's ratio on R2a is given in \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian}, and what the difference does to an as-built comparison in \Cref{sec:pipeline-design}.

The gates per query need not follow the subnormalisation. One query of the descriptor applies more terms than one of the structured standard form, through a block register, so on constant-coefficient problems its advantage is expected in the number of queries, the filter's degree, rather than in the gates per query; on a lifted variable coefficient, which the structured encoding pays for in full, its query can be the cheaper as well. \Cref{sec:results-scaling} counts both.

\subsection{Consequences for the Spectrum and the Filter}
\label{sec:descriptor-spectrum}

Every block of the rescaled descriptor is $\mathcal{O}(N)$ at fixed $\zeta$, the recurrences' $\zeta^j\Dt$ being the largest, and the block $\zeta^{k-1}\Dt$ alone has norm $2\zeta^{k-1}(N-1)$, so $\|R_{\mathrm{desc}}\|_2=\Theta(N)$ and $\|\Hdesc\|=\Theta(N^2)$ whatever the order $k$: the derivative is banded, and its norm is that of $\Dt$ rather than of $\G^k$. Through \Cref{eq:degree}, with an encoding whose subnormalisation stays within a constant of that norm, the rescaled gap, which is flat on a one-axis \gls{ode} whose leading coefficient does not vanish, gives for that class of problems
\begin{equation}
    \|\Hdesc\| = \Theta(N^2),
    \qquad
    \Delta = \Theta(1)\ \text{(measured)}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    d = \tilde\Theta(N),
    \label{eq:descriptor-degree}
\end{equation}
against $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ for the standard form with the best exact encoding (\Cref{eq:standard-wall}). Where the leading coefficient vanishes, or on two axes, the rescaling's weights raise the norm, and the degree is about $\tilde\Theta(N^2)$ or grows more steeply; for a double zero no claim is made (\Cref{tab:descriptor-regimes}). Neither form is polylogarithmic in $N$: $\tilde\Theta(N)$ is exponential in $n$, and the claim is a lower exponent only. The degrees are the minimax edge filter's, with the hedge of \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian} for polynomial filters in general.

The encoding sits at that norm. In the terms of \Cref{sec:standard}, the encoder's cost $\alpha_H/\|H\|$ on R2a at $n=8$ is $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{hamiltonian_ratio}$ for the descriptor against $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{hamiltonian_ratio}$ for the structured standard form, so the descriptor lowers the Hamiltonian's cost and keeps the encoder's near one. \Cref{sec:results-scaling} measures the degrees that result, as built and at the ideal-encoding bound, and \Cref{sec:results-gap} the gap on which \Cref{eq:descriptor-degree} rests.

The relative gap $\Delta/\|H\|$ is $\Theta(N^{-2})$ here and $\Theta(N^{-4k})$ in the standard form: on R2a at $n=8$ the descriptor's is $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/descriptor/n8}{relative_gap}$, where the standard form's has fallen below double precision (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}). The classical reference forms neither Hamiltonian, so a small relative gap costs the filter's degree, not the resolvability of the ground state. Per query the two encodings also differ in how often one post-selected application succeeds, which compares the encodings and not the preparation (\Cref{app:haar-success}).

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:descriptor-summary}

Carrying the derivatives instead of eliminating them makes the residual banded with its solution unchanged, and has a price.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Keeping the banded factors $\Bt$ and $\Dt$ of $\G$ and carrying the derivatives as unknowns, as a chain for equations of order two and above or in mass form for first-order equations and systems, makes the residual banded, apart from the rank-one constraint rows both forms share, with its kernel exactly the standard form's (\Cref{thm:kernel-equivalence}).
    \item Its block encoding is one flat \gls{lcu} with $\lceil\log_2(n+4)\rceil+8$ ancilla for R2's system rows, the least order for an exact \gls{lcu} that expands its diagonals in Pauli strings, and its subnormalisation tends to the norm on regular one-axis problems (\Cref{prop:descriptor-cost}). Its price is a larger state, by the chain's block register, and, on the constant-coefficient problems, more gates per query at the sizes reached (\Cref{sec:results-scaling}).
    \item The Hamiltonian's norm is $\Theta(N^2)$, and for a one-axis equation whose leading coefficient does not vanish the filter's degree is $\tilde\Theta(N)$, against the standard form's $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ (\Cref{eq:descriptor-degree}). Neither is polylogarithmic.
    \item \emph{The limit.} The carried derivatives shrink the gap where the solution's derivatives are large, and make it fall with $N$ where the leading coefficient vanishes and on two axes. An a priori block rescaling restores it on one axis at no gate cost, at the price of a larger norm where the leading coefficient vanishes (on R3, which vanishes at both ends, the standard form's norm falls too); on two axes the gap still falls, and a double zero is beyond any block rescaling.
\end{enumerate}
All of this concerns one real scalar field and a linear equation, the scope of Wu et al.'s construction, and the gain comes from replacing the dense $\G$ by banded factors wherever the residual differentiates in Chebyshev coefficients. Coupled fields, complex-valued fields and nonlinear terms each change what the residual must carry, and the nonlinear route Wu et al.\ propose gives the filter no unique ground state to find. \Cref{sec:extensions} takes the reformulation there; \Cref{sec:results-scaling,sec:results-gap} test the cost and the gap on the benchmark problems.
