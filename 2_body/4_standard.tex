Does the total number of queries originate in the particular encoder circuits, or in the Hamiltonian itself? This is the first question of \Cref{sec:pihm-assessment}, and the two are separable because a filter's degree depends on the encoding only through its subnormalisation $\alpha_H$, which no block encoding brings below $\|H\|$ (\Cref{eq:degree}). We call the ratio $\alpha_H/\|H\|\ge1$ the \emph{encoder's cost} and $\|H\|$ itself the \emph{Hamiltonian's cost}: the first belongs to the circuit, the second to the operator. Since the degree is linear in $\alpha_R=\alpha_H^{1/2}$, the encoder's cost enters it as $\alpha_R/\|R\|$. The construction that separates them is that of Wu et al.\ \cite{wu2025pihm}, built on the Chebyshev latent space and lifts of Paine et al.\ \cite{paine2023physicsinformed}. We call it the \emph{standard form}: the state holds the field alone, and every derivative is eliminated through the dense differentiation matrix $\G$, so that a term $f^{(j)}$ acts as $\G^j$ on the field's coefficients and the equation is one block of a stacked residual. Each departure from the printed method is argued where it occurs and collected in \Cref{tab:deviations}. \Cref{sec:standard-model,sec:standard-operators,sec:standard-constraints,sec:standard-form} build the form, \Cref{sec:standard-factorisation} encodes its derivative, and \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian} shows what no encoding removes.

\subsection{The Model}
\label{sec:standard-model}
% ~0.6 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. The paper's model unchanged (eq:pihm-chebyshev-feature-map, eq:pihm-fq); its prefactors
%      as the basis functions varphi_k (discretely orthonormal at the nodes); f_N the N-term
%      truncation of f with coefficients b; f_N = <tau(x)|b> = ||b|| <tau(x)|psi_e>, psi_e =
%      b/||b|| the ideal state (not psi_g, not psi_ref): the model represents f_N exactly at
%      (psi_e, ||b||^2).
%   2. Orthogonal with common norm pi/N; Parseval gives eta_e = ||b||^2 (eq:eta-e, OURS), the
%      ideal value of the paper's eta, growing like N.
%   3. The sign: sqrt(eta) negative when the ground state's sign opposes the datum's; eta > 0 a
%      convention; the decoded field carries the sign (one clause).
%   4. tau kept unnormalised, as the paper's derivation uses it: ||tau(x)||^2 varies with x
%      (reaching (2N-1)/N at the ends); every row inherits the weight; normalising point by
%      point would change the rows' relative weights and the least-squares solution.
%   5. Several axes: tau(x) (x) tau(y), Kronecker order, each axis its own n.
%   6. R2a introduced by its equation, the running example.
% CHECKLIST  eta_e derived in two sentences (proof in App. B/C); the raw-tau reason given as
%   mathematics, not history (no "the legacy code").
% TRAPS  Quoting T6 (App. K); calling eta_e "the printed eta".

We use the paper's model, \Cref{eq:pihm-chebyshev-feature-map,eq:pihm-fq}, unchanged, and write it in the orthonormal basis that makes its scale explicit. The prefactors of the feature map define the basis functions $\varphi_0=T_0/\sqrt{N}$ and $\varphi_k=T_k/\sqrt{N/2}$ for $1\le k<N$, which are orthonormal under the discrete inner product on the $N$ Chebyshev nodes, and $\bra{\tau(x)}=(\varphi_0(x),\dots,\varphi_{N-1}(x))$ is the row of their values at $x$. A truncated solution $f_N=\sum_{k<N}b_k\varphi_k$ is then $f_N(x)=\braket{\tau(x)|b}=\|b\|\braket{\tau(x)|\psi_e}$ with $\ket{\psi_e}=b/\|b\|$, so the model $f_Q$ of \Cref{eq:pihm-fq} represents $f_N$ exactly at $\ket{\psi}=\ket{\psi_e}$ and $\eta=\eta_e$, where, by Parseval's identity under the Chebyshev weight (\Cref{app:eta-e}),
\begin{equation}
    \eta_e=\|b\|^2=\frac{N}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{f_N(x)^2}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx .
    \label{eq:eta-e}
\end{equation}
The state carries the direction of $b$ and the scale its length, and $\eta_e$ is fixed by the solution and the resolution alone, growing with $N$ for a smooth solution. This is representability, not a requirement on the prepared state: the ground state $\ket{\psi_g}$ is set by the residual's kernel and approaches $\pm\ket{\psi_e}$ only as far as $f_N$ satisfies the constraints. The regular datum recovers the scale and its sign: $y_s/\braket{\tau(x_s)|\psi_g}$ is negative when the ground state, defined only up to sign, opposes the datum, so the paper's $\eta>0$ is a convention and the decoded field carries the sign.

The feature vector is kept unnormalised, as the paper's derivation uses it. Its squared norm oscillates about one and reaches $(2N-1)/N$ at $x=\pm1$ (\Cref{app:eta-e}), and every constraint row of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints} inherits that weight. Where the residual has no exact kernel the ground state is a least-squares compromise between its rows, and normalising $\tau(x)$ point by point, which gives the paper's $\tilde\tau(x)$, would change the rows' relative weights and with them the solution; it would also make collapse rows at different points disagree. The rows are therefore built, and block-encoded, from the unnormalised vector. A function of several variables is carried on one register per axis, each with its own $n$, with the feature vector $\bra{\tau(x_1)}\otimes\bra{\tau(x_2)}\otimes\dots$ in Kronecker order. The running example of this chapter is the paper's Fig.~3b: the critically damped oscillator $f''+4f'+4f=0$ with $f(-1)=0$ and $f(0)=\tfrac12$, whose solution is $f=\tfrac12(1+x)e^{-2x}$; in the benchmark set of \Cref{tab:ladder} it is labelled R2a.

\subsection{Differentiation and Multiplication}
\label{sec:standard-operators}
% ~0.9 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. 𝔾's entries (eq:pihm-G-entries, label kept), exact on the represented space (olver2013);
%      the paper prints the transpose (T-16, once); strictly upper triangular, nilpotent, ker 𝔾^j
%      the polynomials of degree < j; ||𝔾||_2/N^2 -> 1/(2 j_{-3/4,1}) (App. B, App. E);
%      orthogonally similar to the nodal differentiation matrix; ||𝔾^k||_2 ~ N^{2k}.
%   2. The lift (Paine et al.'s; the paper's Eq. 5): exact iff deg a <= N; a_0 M_1; ||M_a|| <=
%      sqrt2 sup|a|. The standard form keeps the lift, and the descriptor applies the same lift
%      (assemble/descriptor.py); the square form is only App. G's gap-study control.
%   3. The equation block (eq:standard-equation), the source as the rank-one |r-hat><tau(x_s)|/f(x_s).
%   4. DEPARTURE (sources): Maclaurin to p-bar -> Chebyshev coefficients; R4d's order-7 series
%      off by more than the source itself at x = -1 (checked: 130 %); a floor -> spectral.
%   5. DEPARTURE (axes): two -> any number, Kronecker over axes (app:pihm-multi-axis); two run.
% CHECKLIST  Each fact about 𝔾 points to its proof; the lift credited.
% TRAPS  "Our differentiation matrix"; citing trefethen2000spectral for ||𝔾|| (Gauss-Lobatto
%   nodes, not these).
% REVISE: the R4d "more than the source itself" clause is a hand computation (order-7 series
%   -12.89 against -5.59 at x = -1); keep it numberless, or add a derived key.

In the normalised basis the differentiation matrix has the entries
\begin{equation}
    [\G]_{k,p} =
    \begin{cases}
        2p, & p > k \ge 1,\ p-k\ \text{odd}, \\
        \sqrt{2}\,p, & k = 0,\ p\ \text{odd}, \\
        0, & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:pihm-G-entries}
\end{equation}
and it is exact on the represented space, since the derivative of a polynomial of degree below $N$ stays in it \cite{olver2013}. $\G$ is upper triangular, dense and nilpotent. $\ker\G^{j}$ is the space of polynomials of degree below $j$, and about half the positions above the diagonal are non-zero. Its norm grows as $N^2$: $\|\G\|_2/N^2$ converges to $1/(2j_{-3/4,1})\approx0.472$, with $j_{-3/4,1}$ the first positive zero of the Bessel function $J_{-3/4}$, and $\|\G^k\|_2/N^{2k}$ converges to a positive constant for every $k$. Because the basis is orthonormal at the Chebyshev nodes, $\G$ is orthogonally similar to the matrix that differentiates nodal values, so the growth is not an artefact of the normalisation, and its order is the classical one for dense Chebyshev differentiation \cite{olver2013,canuto2006spectral}. The structure, the similarity and the bounds $N^2/(4\sqrt2)\le\|\G\|_2\le N^2/\sqrt2$ are proved in \Cref{app:cheb-G-norm}, and the two limits in \Cref{app:G-limits}.

A polynomial coefficient $a(x)$ acts by the lift of Paine et al.\ \cite{paine2023physicsinformed}, $x^p\bra{\tau(x)}_n=\bra{\tau(x)}_{n+1}\M_{x^p}$ (the paper's Eq.~5), extended by linearity to $\M_a=\sum_pa_p\M_{x^p}$. It is exact whenever $\deg a\le N$, a constant enters as $a_0\M_1=a_0\sqrt2\,[\,I;\,0\,]$, and $\|\M_a\|_2\le\sqrt2\sup|a|$ (\Cref{prop:lift}). The equation $\sum_ja_j(x)f^{(j)}=r(x)$ then has the equation block
\begin{equation}
    \Astd=\sum_{j}\M_{a_j}\G^{j}-\M_r\,\Dzero(x_s), \qquad \M_r\,\Dzero(x_s)=\frac{\ket{\hat r}\bra{\tau(x_s)}}{f(x_s)},
    \label{eq:standard-equation}
\end{equation}
in which the source enters through the collapse row of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints}, with $\hat r$ the coefficients of $r$ on the lifted register. The subscript marks the standard form, defined in \Cref{def:standard-form}.

The paper expands a transcendental source in a Maclaurin series truncated at order $\bar p$ (\Cref{sec:pihm-residual}). A truncated source poses a different equation, whose solution the ground state then approximates. The order-7 series of the source $x\,e^{-2x}\sin4x$ of the paper's Fig.~5d (R4d, $f''+4f'+20f=x\,e^{-2x}\sin4x$) differs from the source by more than the source itself at $x=-1$, and the resulting error is a floor that no increase in $n$ removes. The standard form loads the Chebyshev coefficients of the source instead, through the same collapse row, so the source is as accurate as the series that represents the solution, geometrically convergent for a source analytic on the interval \cite{trefethen2013approximation}.

The paper treats at most two variables. On any number of axes a term is the Kronecker product over the axes of $\M_{a}\G^{j_a}$, each axis lifted if any term on it needs the exact product, and a constant coefficient on a lifted axis enters through $\M_1$ (\Cref{app:pihm-multi-axis}). The benchmark problems use at most two.

\subsection{Constraints and Data}
\label{sec:standard-constraints}
% ~0.9 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. The paper's rows (Eqs 9-11): B(x) maps a series to the constant f_N(x); B(x)𝔾^j vanishes
%      exactly where f^{(j)}(x) = 0; D0(x_s) maps the solution to 1 whatever its scale; both
%      homogeneous; the regular datum fixes the scale (§4.1).
%   2. DEPARTURE (constraint points): rounded zeros/extrema (R4a-R4d, Fig. 5) -> the problem's
%      point, exact; a zero the solution lacks contradicts the equation, the error floors; one
%      pair of numbers (R4a: floor against the exact point's best). The exact point is problem
%      data inherited from the paper's statement, NOT answer-independent. Both printed choices

%   3. The uniqueness count (OURS, prop:uniqueness-count): rank k-1 on the solution space plus
%      one regular datum, for a leading coefficient without zeros; the computed kernel decides

%   4. DEPARTURE (PDE data -> datum slices): the Laplace modes argument; the kernel's dimension
%      at n = (5,5) (one number); heat and wave read off the solution; the slice rows
%      S_a(x*) - g-hat tau(x_s)^T / f(x_s) (app:datum-slice); answer-independent, well posed at
%      every n, both forms; the heat panel at t = -1 (forward).
%   5. DEPARTURE (double zero): R3c, R3d (Fig. 4c, d); the branch point; untrusted at the printed

%   6. The Laplace typo (A-05), one sentence; vector data -> ratio rows (sec:systems-data).
% CHECKLIST  Every departure a defect and a property; no "we fixed"; the T-27 rule (no variant

The constraints are built from the paper's rank-one rows (its Eqs~9--11). The zero row $\Brow(x)=\sqrt{N}\ket{0}\bra{\tau(x)}$ maps a series to the constant function $f_N(x)$, so $\Brow(x)\G^{j}$ annihilates exactly the series whose $j$-th derivative vanishes at $x$. The collapse row $\Dzero(x_s)=\Brow(x_s)/f(x_s)$ maps the solution to the constant $1$ whatever its scale, which is what lets a source enter \Cref{eq:standard-equation}. The scale is fixed after preparation (\Cref{sec:standard-model}).

A zero row is exact only at a point where the solution's derivative of that order vanishes. The paper places its inhomogeneous examples' invariant constraints at zeros and extrema of the known solution rounded to two decimal places ($f'(-0.2)=0$ on R4a, the paper's Fig.~5a, $(x-1)f''-xf'+f=(x-1)^2$ with $f(0)=\tfrac12$ and $f'(0)=-\tfrac18$, for an extremum at $x\approx-0.196$), so the row demands a zero the solution lacks, least squares trades it against the equation, and the error floors: \res[2,sci]{build/R4a/faithful/standard/floor}{high} on R4a for every $n$ from 4 to 10, against \res[2,sci]{build/R4a/standard/best}{field_err_linf} at $n=\res{build/R4a/standard/best}{n}$ at the exact point. The standard form takes the exact point, the root of the solution's derivative nearest the paper's value. That root is set-up that depends on the answer: we compute it from the reference solution, outside the method, so a new problem must supply its constraint points with its data. We keep the invariant row, rather than impose the problem's stated data, so that the residual keeps the paper's rows and only the defect changes. The stated data could enter without the answer: R4a's $f'(0)=-\tfrac18$, for instance, is a point datum row of \Cref{def:standard-form}, which no run here uses. On R4b to R4d the rounded point and the truncated source each floor the error on their own, and the error converges only with both replaced (\Cref{app:repro-triage}).

For an ordinary equation of order $k$ whose leading coefficient has no zero on $[-1,1]$, the invariant constraints fix the solution up to its scale, which the regular datum then fixes, exactly when they have rank $k-1$ on the equation's $k$-dimensional solution space (\Cref{prop:uniqueness-count}). The rank is that on the solutions, not of the functionals, so $k-1$ constraints chosen without regard to the equation can fail. Where the count does not apply, for an inhomogeneous equation or a vanishing leading coefficient, and for the gap at a given $n$, the computed kernel decides (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}).

A partial differential equation has an infinite-dimensional solution space and no such count. The paper's invariant sets (\Cref{sec:pihm-residual}) omit the function that fixes the solution: on Laplace's equation (R5a), every mode $\sin\tfrac{\ell\pi(x+1)}{2}\sinh\tfrac{\ell\pi(y+1)}{2}$ satisfies the equation and its three homogeneous sides, and only $\ell=1$ matches the omitted top side (\Cref{app:uniqueness-count}). Truncation hides the other modes at the printed resolution, where the printed set leaves a trusted one-dimensional kernel, and admits them as $n$ grows: at $n=(5,5)$ the computed near-kernel has dimension $\res{build/R5a/faithful/standard/n5x5}{kernel_dimension}$. The sets of the heat equation (R5b) and the wave equation (R5c), read off the known solutions, fail in the same way. The standard form imposes the initial or boundary function $g$ itself, on its slice $x_a=x^*$, by the \emph{datum-slice} rows
\begin{equation}
    \Slice_a(x^*)-\frac{\hat g\,\bra{\tau(x_s)}}{f(x_s)},
    \label{eq:datum-slice}
\end{equation}
with $\Slice_a(x^*)$ the zero row $\Brow(x^*)$ on axis $a$ and the identity on the others, $\hat g$ the coefficients of $g$ broadcast along axis $a$, and $x_s$ a regular point. They vanish on the solution and its multiples and leave the scale free (\Cref{prop:datum-slice}), are built from the problem's statement, serve both forms, and on R5a leave a one-dimensional kernel that is trusted at every resolution computed from $n=(3,3)$ to $(6,6)$, with gap ratio $\res[2,sci]{build/R5a/standard/n6x6}{gap_ratio}$ at the last. The heat problem is posed forward from $t=-1$, with the paper's solution evaluated there as its initial function, because the paper's $t=0$ lies inside $[-1,1]$ and would make $[-1,0]$ a backward heat problem, ill posed (\Cref{app:datum-slice}); like R4's constraint points, these data are set up from the known solution.

The paper's associated Legendre examples R3c and R3d (its Figs~4c and~4d, $l=5$ and $6$ with $m=1$) multiply the equation through by $1-x^2$, which leaves a leading coefficient $(1-x^2)^2$ with a double zero at each end and a solution $P_l^1=-\sqrt{1-x^2}\,P_l'$ \cite[\S14.6]{dlmf} with a branch point there, which a Chebyshev series approximates only algebraically \cite{trefethen2013approximation}: at the printed $n=5$ neither problem has a trusted ground state, and the error on R3c is \res[2,sci]{build/R3c/faithful/standard/n5}{field_err_linf}. The standard form solves for $v=f/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ instead, whose equation has a leading coefficient with simple zeros and the polynomial solution $v=-P_l'$, represented exactly once $N>l-1$ (\Cref{prop:legendre-substitution}): the error on R3c is \res[2,sci]{build/R3c/standard/best}{field_err_linf} at $n=\res{build/R3c/standard/best}{n}$ (\Cref{app:legendre-substitution}). It is also the form the descriptor can run (\Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}).

Two smaller points complete the data. The paper prints the solution of its Laplace example as $\operatorname{csch}\pi\,\cosh(\pi x/2)\sinh(\pi(y+1)/2)$, which does not satisfy its boundary conditions; the solution, and the reference used throughout, is $\cos(\pi x/2)\sinh(\pi(y+1)/2)/\sinh\pi$. Vector data, such as the initial values of a coupled system, enter as ratio rows, defined with systems in \Cref{sec:systems-data}.

The equation block and the constraint rows are stacked into one residual,
\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{std}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Astd\\ C_1\\ \vdots\\ C_L\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \Hstd=R_{\mathrm{std}}^{\dagger}R_{\mathrm{std}}=\Astd^{\dagger}\Astd+\sum_iC_i^{\dagger}C_i ,
    \label{eq:standard-stacked}
\end{equation}
every block at weight one, so that $\Hstd$ is a sum of Gram matrices of the paper's form, \Cref{eq:pihm-hamiltonian}, with the datum slices among the $C_i$. The ground state of $\Hstd$ is the smallest right singular vector of $R_{\mathrm{std}}$. The classical reference computes it from $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ by a singular value decomposition, or by Lanczos iteration beyond its reach (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}), and never forms $\Hstd$, which would square the residual's condition number \cite{trefethenbau1997}; on R2a the relative gap of $\Hstd$ has fallen below double precision by $n=8$ (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}). The circuit likewise encodes $R_{\mathrm{std}}$, and $\Hstd$ is never formed (\Cref{sec:pipeline-walk}).

Written this way, $\Hstd$ is positive semi-definite, and in the very case the method aims at, a solution the truncation represents exactly, it is not definite, contrary to the paper's description (\Cref{sec:pihm-residual}). When the solution is a polynomial of degree below $N$, any source is represented exactly and every constraint holds exactly at its point, $\G$ differentiates the solution and the lifts multiply it exactly, so its coefficients are annihilated by $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ and give $\Hstd$ an exact zero eigenvalue; Legendre's equation $(1-x^2)f''-2xf'+20f=0$ (R3a, the paper's Fig.~4a), whose solution is $P_4$, has one from $n=3$. Otherwise the truncated solution leaves a residual, $\lambda_0>0$, and the ground state minimises the total squared residual: the construction is least squares. Nor need the bottom of the spectrum be distinct, as the paper's partial differential sets show (\Cref{sec:standard-constraints}). The thesis relies on neither property; it relies on an isolated ground state, measured as a trusted kernel (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}).

\begin{definition}[The standard form]
\label{def:standard-form}
For a linear problem on one or more Chebyshev axes, the \emph{standard form} is the stacked residual $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ of \Cref{eq:standard-stacked}, whose equation block is \Cref{eq:standard-equation}, lifted on every axis where a coefficient or a source needs the exact product, and whose constraint rows, each at weight one, are built from four kinds of row: zero rows $\Brow(x^*)\G^{j}$, at a point or on a slice, for data that vanish; the collapse row $\Dzero(x_s)$, which carries a non-zero point datum $f^{(j)}(x^*)=y^*$ as the row $\Brow(x^*)\G^{j}-y^*\Dzero(x_s)$, as it carries a source into the equation block; ratio rows for vector data (\Cref{sec:systems-data}); and datum slices (\Cref{eq:datum-slice}) for initial and boundary functions. Its Hamiltonian is $\Hstd=R_{\mathrm{std}}^{\dagger}R_{\mathrm{std}}$, and its solution is the smallest right singular vector of $R_{\mathrm{std}}$, scaled by the regular datum $f(x_s)=y_s$.
\end{definition}

\Cref{tab:constraint-kinds} lists the four kinds as model inputs, and a benchmark label such as R2a names the problem with the data of this section. \Cref{tab:deviations} collects every departure from the printed method: those argued above, and those of the pipeline, argued where they are built. The departures in the residual change the data it carries, never $\G$, the lifts or the Gram structure, so the standard form's Hamiltonian has the paper's norm to leading order, the constraint rows growing more slowly than the equation block (\Cref{app:G-limits}). The one exception is R3c and R3d, whose substitution changes the equation; their cost is compared in the form the standard form runs. The departures in the pipeline do change the cost, and in one direction: the structured encoding of \Cref{sec:standard-factorisation} is far cheaper than the paper's. Measured against the paper's own method, the baseline is therefore not weakened: it has the paper's Hamiltonian, the cheapest exact encoding built, and better accuracy. How its encoding compares with the descriptor's, and how every comparison corrects for the difference, is declared with the design of the experiment (\Cref{sec:pipeline-design}).

\subsection{Encoding the Derivative}
\label{sec:standard-factorisation}
% ~1.6 pp in three parts. The paper's encoding (the encoder's cost made visible); the exact
% factorisation (holds it to a constant); its circuits and cost (within a constant of the norm).
% Lead-in, one sentence: 𝔾 is the term whose encoding decides the encoder's cost.

Every term of the standard form is a product of lifts, rank-one rows and powers of $\G$. The rank-one rows have exact circuits whose subnormalisation is fixed by their own norm, so the term whose encoding decides the encoder's cost is $\G$.

The paper's circuit for $\G$ (its Fig.~9; \Cref{sec:pihm-encoding}) selects each entry $(k,p)$ by a register subtraction over a superposition of offsets and loads the column value $2p$ as an amplitude through a ladder of small controlled rotations. Its claims about gates and entry errors hold, the first as a bound and the second to leading order (\Cref{app:published-build}). The claim that decides its cost is the prefactor of \Cref{eq:pihm-published-prefactor}, which is valid but not compared with $\|\G\|_2$. Since $\|\G\|_S=\|\G^{\top}\G\|_1$ grows as $N^4/3$, the prefactor grows as $N^5/2$, against $\|\G\|_2\sim N^2/(2j_{-3/4,1})$, so its looseness tends to $j_{-3/4,1}N^3$ with $j_{-3/4,1}\approx1.06$; at $n=7$ it is \res[2,sci]{derivative/n7}{published_ratio} (exact classical computation, \Cref{app:alpha-G}). A circuit with polynomially many gates is cheap to apply once, but the filter applies it a number of times set by its subnormalisation (\Cref{eq:degree}): the encoding is efficient in gates and inefficient in subnormalisation. To cost it beside the others we rebuild its mechanism. The rebuilt circuit's own subnormalisation is a third of the printed prefactor, so every cost quoted for the paper's encoding is the kinder reading; its entries are approximate, with an error of order $N^{-5}$ (\Cref{prop:epsilon-G}), so it enters resource estimates only, composed into the same stacked residual as the exact encoding below. The rebuild, its subnormalisation, its error bound and the verdict on each of the paper's circuit claims are given in \Cref{app:published-build}.

\subsubsection{An Exact Factorisation}
\label{sec:standard-exact}
% ~0.5 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. The idea in words: each non-zero entry is a product of three factors, each of one index
%      (row weight, parity-selected offset, column value 2p).
%   2. prop:G-factorisation (C-01), proof in App. E.
%   3. The measured error as a check on the implementation, not as evidence.
% REVISE: 3.4e-13 is a one-off measurement (circuit block vs the assembled 𝔾, n <= 7), typed as
%   \prov; replace with a derived key or delete the number (plan §2.1).

The paper's circuit is approximate, and its prefactor large, because it loads the column value of each entry as a rotation amplitude. The structure of $\G$ allows a different route. Every non-zero entry of \Cref{eq:pihm-G-entries} is a product of three factors, each depending on one index alone: a row weight, equal to $1/\sqrt2$ in row~0 because of the normalisation of $T_0$ and to one elsewhere; a selector depending only on the offset $p-k$, non-zero when $p>k$ and $p-k$ is odd; and the column value $2p$, the weight with which differentiation passes $T_p$ to the lower polynomials. Each factor has an exact block encoding with no amplitude loaded by rotation.

\begin{proposition}[Exact factorisation of the differentiation matrix]
\label{prop:G-factorisation}
For every $n \ge 1$ the differentiation matrix on the normalised Chebyshev coefficients factorises as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
        \G = R_0\,\bigl(2\Uodd\bigr)\,\Lambda, \\
        R_0 = I - \Bigl(1 - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Bigr)\ket{0}\bra{0},
        \qquad
        \bigl(\Uodd\bigr)_{kp} = \bigl[\,p > k,\ p - k\ \text{odd}\,\bigr],
        \qquad
        \Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(p) ,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:G-structured}
\end{equation}
where $\Uodd$ is the sum of the $N/2$ truncated shifts by odd offsets.
\end{proposition}
The proof compares the two sides entry by entry with \Cref{eq:pihm-G-entries} and is given in \Cref{app:proof-G-factorisation}. As a check on our implementation, not on the proposition, the block of the composed circuit matches the independently assembled $\G$ entrywise to $\prov{3.4\times10^{-13}}$ (absolute) for $n\le7$.

\subsubsection{Circuits and Cost}
\label{sec:standard-cost}
% ~0.7 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. Lambda as a Z-string LCU (its display eq:lambda-lcu moved to App. E in review,
%      2026-10-12), alpha = ||Lambda||; R_0 as two terms, alpha = 1.
%   2. U_odd: PREPARE over odd offsets, SELECT an in-place subtraction with a borrow flag,
%      post-selected; alpha = N/2; F3.
%   3. prop:standard-cost (alpha_G = N(N-1) -> 2 j_{-3/4,1} ||𝔾||_2; O(n^2) gates; n + 4
%      ancilla, n - 1 the selector); measured ratios (tab:alpha-G-short); where the 2.117 comes
%      from.
%   4. The n - 1 selector qubits: forced by an L-term LCU (chakraborty2025qsvt); the descriptor
%      never forms the sum of shifts (O(log n)).
%   5. The rest of the residual from the same pieces (lifts, rows, sources, powers on one

% REVISE: the two-qubit gate counts at n = 2 and 5 are \prov (transpiled once, not exported).

Each factor is encoded as a linear combination of unitaries \cite{childs2012lcu} whose terms are unitary already, so no part of the encoding is approximate. The column index $\Lambda$ is a sum of $n+1$ Pauli-$Z$ strings, encoded at $\alpha_\Lambda=N-1=\|\Lambda\|_2$, and the row weight $R_0$ is a positive combination of the identity and the reflection about $\ket{0}$, encoded at $\alpha_{R_0}=1=\|R_0\|_2$ (\Cref{app:proof-G-factorisation}). The sum of shifts is the one factor that needs arithmetic (\Cref{fig:uodd-select}). Its PREPARE puts an offset register in the uniform superposition over the $N/2$ odd offsets $\ell$, and its SELECT is the in-place subtraction $p\leftarrow p-\ell$ of a ripple-carry circuit \cite{cuccaro2004adder}, which sets a borrow flag when $p<\ell$. Post-selecting the flag on zero removes exactly the terms that would wrap around, so the block is $\tfrac{2}{N}\bigl[p>k,\ p-k\ \text{odd}\bigr]$: an exact encoding of $\Uodd$ at $\alpha=N/2$ with $O(n)$ Toffoli gates, in which one PREPARE serves all $N/2$ terms because SELECT is a permutation (\Cref{app:uodd-select-detail}).

The three encodings multiply, and so do their subnormalisations. Their product is an exact block encoding of $\G$ at $\alpha_{\G}=1\cdot N\cdot(N-1)$, with the following cost.

\begin{proposition}[Cost of the structured encoding]
\label{prop:standard-cost}
The factorisation of \Cref{prop:G-factorisation} compiles to an exact block encoding of $\G$ on $n$ system qubits with
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\G} = N(N-1),
    \qquad
    \frac{\alpha_{\G}}{\|\G\|_2} \longrightarrow 2j_{-3/4,1} \approx 2.117,
    \qquad
    O(n^2)\ \text{gates},
    \qquad
    n + 4\ \text{ancilla} ,
    \label{eq:standard-cost}
\end{equation}
of which $n-1$ ancilla are the selector of the $N/2$-term combination $\Uodd$.
\end{proposition}
The limit is that of $\|\G\|_2/N^2$ (\Cref{sec:standard-operators}). The ancilla are the $n+2$ of $\Uodd$'s offset register, carry and borrow, and one flag for each of the two products, the other factors' selectors reusing those qubits; the count is exact in the circuit for $n=2$ to $8$. The gate count assumes an ancilla-free synthesis of multi-controlled gates and falls to $O(n\log n)$ with a linear-cost synthesis that borrows idle qubits \cite{barenco1995elementary}; the transpiled two-qubit gate counts are available only at small $n$ (\prov{109} at $n=2$ and \prov{471} at $n=5$), consistent with the bound but too few to fit it. The proofs are given in \Cref{app:proof-standard-cost}. The limit is asymptotic, and the sizes we reach are measured: the ratio $\alpha_{\G}/\|\G\|_2$ is $\res[3]{derivative/n2}{structured_ratio}$ at $n=2$ and rises to $\res[3]{derivative/n10}{structured_ratio}$ at $n=10$ (\Cref{tab:alpha-G-short}), against a ratio for the paper's encoding that grows as $N^3$. $R_0$ and $\Lambda$ are encoded at their own norms, so the limiting factor is $\pi/2$ from $\Uodd$ and $4j_{-3/4,1}/\pi\approx1.35$ from the product (\Cref{lem:uodd-norm}).

The $n-1$ qubits that hold the offsets' superposition are the price of writing $\Uodd$ as one combination of its $N/2$ shifts; what that implies for the growth of the ancilla, against the descriptor's, is argued in \Cref{sec:descriptor-cost}.

\label{sec:standard-hamiltonian}

For constant coefficients the structured encoding holds the encoder's cost of the whole residual, as well as that of $\G$, to a constant. On R2a at $n=8$ the stacked residual's encoding has $\alpha_R=\res[3,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_R}$ against $\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2=\res[3,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{norm_R}$, both computed exactly from the built circuit and the assembled residual, a ratio of $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$. From $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n2/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ at $n=2$ the ratio rises to $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n6/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ at $n=6$, levelling off towards the looseness of the residual's dominant term, $\G^2$, whose ratio $\alpha_\G^2/\|\G^2\|_2$ tends to a constant (\Cref{app:G-limits}); no limit is proved for the whole residual. Since the degree is linear in $\alpha_R$, this ratio is the whole of the encoder's cost in the degree; in $\alpha_H=\alpha_R^2$ it is squared, $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{hamiltonian_ratio}$ at $n=8$. With the paper's encoding the same ratio is $\res[2,sci]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n7/T3/filter=minimax/encoder=published}{alpha_ratio}$ at $n=7$ and $\res[2,sci]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax/encoder=published}{alpha_ratio}$ at $n=8$: it grows as $N^{3k}$, a factor $N^3$ for each of the $k$ factors of $\G$ in the leading term.

No encoder can change the norm it encodes. For an equation of order $k$ with constant coefficients and $a_k\ne0$, $\|\G^k\|_2/N^{2k}$ tends to a positive constant, so $\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2=\Theta(N^{2k})$ and $\|\Hstd\|=\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2^2=\Theta(N^{4k})$, the constraint rows growing more slowly (\Cref{app:G-limits}). The gap is measured; no proof is given. On R2a it is $\res[3]{build/R2a/standard/n3}{gap}$ at $n=3$ and $\res[3]{build/R2a/standard/n10}{gap}$ at $n=10$, and on $f''-2f'-3f=0$ (R2b, the paper's Fig.~3c) it is $\res[3]{build/R2b/standard/n4}{gap}$ at $n=4$ and $\res[3]{build/R2b/standard/n10}{gap}$ at $n=10$, unchanged to the figures shown at every $n$ between, while $\|\Hstd\|$ on R2a grows from $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/n3}{hamiltonian_norm}$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/n10}{hamiltonian_norm}$. Because no block encoding has $\alpha_H$ below the norm, \Cref{eq:degree} gives
\begin{equation}
    \|\Hstd\| = \Theta\bigl(N^{4k}\bigr),
    \quad
    \Delta = \Theta(1)\ \text{(measured)}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    d = \tilde\Omega\bigl(N^{2k}\bigr)\ \text{for every block encoding},
    \label{eq:standard-wall}
\end{equation}
and $d=\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ for the structured encoding, whose $\alpha_R/\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2$ stays bounded. At second order, the order of the paper's examples, $\|\Hstd\|=\Theta(N^8)$ and no block encoding brings the degree below $\tilde\Omega(N^4)$. This is the Hamiltonian's cost, and its growth can be measured without any encoding. Every block encoding of $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ has $\alpha_R\ge\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2$, and the minimax filter's degree does not increase as $\alpha_R$ falls, so the degree at $\alpha_R=\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2$, which we call the \emph{ideal-encoding bound}, is the least this filter needs on this residual through any encoding of it (\Cref{prop:ideal-degree}). At that bound the degree grows as $N^{2k}$ on the constant-coefficient one-axis problems measured, from $n=7$ to $8$: as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R1/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$ on the first-order R1 ($f'+2f=0$ with $f(-1)=1$) and as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$ on R2a, as \Cref{eq:standard-wall} predicts with a flat gap. These degrees are the minimax edge filter's, which prepares every state in this thesis; for polynomial filters in general, the floor they set holds only as far as the optimality of Lin and Tong's construction supports \cite{lintong2020groundstate}. At $n=8$ the estimated degree for R2a is $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$ as built and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$ at the ideal-encoding bound, both resource estimates far beyond what exact emulation reaches; their ratio is the encoder's cost of the previous paragraph.

The polynomial gate count that Wu et al.\ report for their operators \cite{wu2025pihm}, and that the rebuilt circuits bear out (\Cref{app:published-build}), is the cost of one query. The filter makes $\tilde\Omega(N^{2k})$ queries, exponentially many in $n$, however few gates each takes. The paper's claim of efficiency therefore holds for the circuit and says nothing about the method, consistent with its leaving the gap to future work.

The growth of the degree between $n=7$ and $n=8$, again from resource estimates, shows the two costs apart. With the paper's encoding the degree grows as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax/encoder=published}{degree}}$, with the structured encoding as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}}$, and at the ideal bound as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$. The difference between the first two, $N^{3k}$ at $k=2$, is the encoder's cost, whose growth the structured encoding removes; the common exponent is the Hamiltonian's. That exponent is set by the order of the derivatives. The degree grows as the square root of $\alpha_H/\Delta$, and for a ground value near zero $\Delta\approx\sigma_1^2$, so $\sqrt{\|\Hstd\|/\Delta}\approx\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2/\sigma_1$: the filter pays the conditioning of the residual, and the squared conditioning of $\Hstd=R_{\mathrm{std}}^\dagger R_{\mathrm{std}}$ is paid only by an eigensolver acting on $\Hstd$ \cite{trefethenbau1997} (\Cref{sec:standard-form}). What makes $\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2$ grow as $N^{2k}$ is that each of the $k$ derivatives is eliminated through $\G$, at a factor $N^2$ each. A residual whose blocks each carry one derivative would grow only as the norm of one derivative operator, whatever the order, and less again if that operator were banded. These are the classical remedies: first-order-system least squares carries the derivatives as unknowns so that no block is of high order \cite{cai1994fosls}, and the ultraspherical method makes differentiation banded \cite{olver2013} (\Cref{sec:bg-fosls,sec:bg-ultraspherical}).

The conclusion has a limited scope. The structured encoding bounds $\alpha_H/\|\Hstd\|$ for constant coefficients only, and is loose for a variable coefficient that is lifted. On R3a the circuit pays for the product $\M_a\G^2$ as $\|\M_a\|\,\|\G^2\|\gg\|\M_a\G^2\|$, because the coefficient $1-x^2$ vanishes at the ends of the interval, where the derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials are largest. For the same reason the residual's norm is smaller than the constant-coefficient law gives: $\|\Hstd\|$ is $\res[3,sci]{build/R3a/standard/n7}{hamiltonian_norm}$ at $n=7$ and $\res[3,sci]{build/R3a/standard/n8}{hamiltonian_norm}$ at $n=8$, growing as $N^4$ where the constant-coefficient law gives $N^{4k}=N^8$. The encoder's cost reappears: between $n=7$ and $8$ the as-built degree grows as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}}$, while at the ideal bound it grows as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$, both from resource estimates. The comparison with the descriptor on this problem is made in \Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}, under the convention of \Cref{sec:pipeline-design} that quotes every cost both as built and at the ideal bound.

This answers the first question. For constant-coefficient problems the cost has two parts: the encoder's, a factor growing as $N^{3k}$ in the subnormalisation of the residual under the paper's encoding, which the structured encoding holds to a constant; and the Hamiltonian's, $\tilde\Omega(N^{2k})$ in the filter's degree, which no encoding removes because it is the residual's norm, a factor $N^2$ for each derivative eliminated through $\G$. \Cref{sec:results-scaling} tests both on the benchmark problems. What remains open to change is the residual itself, and \Cref{sec:descriptor} changes it by carrying the derivatives as unknowns instead of eliminating them into the dense $\G$.
