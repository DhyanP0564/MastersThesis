This chapter answers the first question of \Cref{sec:pihm-assessment}: does the method's total number of queries originate in the particular encoder circuits, or in the Hamiltonian itself? The two are separable because a filter's degree depends on the encoding only through its subnormalisation, $d=\Theta\bigl(\sqrt{\alpha_H/\Delta}\,\log(1/\varepsilon)\bigr)$ (\Cref{eq:degree}), and every block encoding of $H$ has $\alpha_H\ge\|H\|$. We call the ratio $\alpha_H/\|H\|\ge1$ the \emph{encoder's cost} and $\|H\|$ itself the \emph{Hamiltonian's cost}: the first belongs to the circuit, the second to the operator. Separating them needs the operator stated exactly. The construction is that of Wu et al.\ \cite{wu2025pihm}, built on the Chebyshev latent space and lifts of Paine et al.\ \cite{paine2023physicsinformed}. We state it once, in the form the rest of the thesis runs, which we call the \emph{standard form}; where it departs from the printed method we give the reason at the point of departure, and \Cref{tab:deviations} collects the departures. \Cref{sec:standard-model,sec:standard-operators,sec:standard-constraints,sec:standard-form} build the standard form, \Cref{sec:standard-factorisation} encodes its derivative exactly, and \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian} shows what no encoding removes.

\subsection{The Model}
\label{sec:standard-model}
% ~0.6 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. The paper's model unchanged (eq:pihm-chebyshev-feature-map, eq:pihm-fq); its weights as
%      the normalised basis phi_k; f_N = <tau(x)|b>, psi = b/||b||.
%   2. Orthogonal with common norm pi/N; Parseval gives eta_e = ||b||^2 (eq:eta-e, OURS), the
%      ideal value of the paper's eta, growing like N.
%   3. The sign: sqrt(eta) negative when the ground state's sign opposes the datum's; eta > 0 a
%      convention; the decoded field carries the sign (one clause).
%   4. tau kept unnormalised, as the paper's derivation uses it: ||tau(x)||^2 varies with x
%      (reaching (2N-1)/N at the ends); every row inherits the weight; normalising point by
%      point would change the rows' relative weights and the least-squares solution.
%   5. Several axes: tau(x) (x) tau(y), Kronecker order, each axis its own n.
%   6. R2a introduced by its equation, the running example.
% CHECKLIST  eta_e derived in two sentences (proof in App. B/C); the raw-tau reason given as
%   mathematics, not history (no "the legacy code").
% TRAPS  Quoting T6 (App. K); calling eta_e "the printed eta".

We use the paper's model, \Cref{eq:pihm-chebyshev-feature-map,eq:pihm-fq}, unchanged. Its weights define a normalised basis, $\phi_0=T_0/\sqrt{N}$ and $\phi_k=T_k/\sqrt{N/2}$ for $1\le k<N$, in which $\bra{\tau(x)}$ is the row $(\phi_0(x),\dots,\phi_{N-1}(x))$ and a truncated series $f_N=\sum_kb_k\phi_k$ is the overlap $\braket{\tau(x)|b}$, with $\ket{\psi}=b/\|b\|$. With $x=\cos\theta$ the Chebyshev weight turns $\int T_jT_k(1-x^2)^{-1/2}\,dx$ into $\int_0^\pi\cos j\theta\cos k\theta\,d\theta$, so the basis is orthogonal with the common squared norm $\pi/N$, and Parseval's identity gives the ideal value of the paper's scale factor,
\begin{equation}
    \eta_e=\|b\|^2=\frac{N}{\pi}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{f_N(x)^2}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx .
    \label{eq:eta-e}
\end{equation}
It is fixed by the solution and the resolution alone and grows like $N$ for a smooth solution, and it is the value the regular datum returns, to within the truncation's error at $x_s$, when the prepared state is $b/\|b\|$. The datum also fixes the sign: $\sqrt{\eta}=y_s/\braket{\tau(x_s)|\psi_g}$ is negative when the ground state, which is defined only up to sign, has the opposite sign to the datum, so the paper's $\eta>0$ is a convention and the decoded field carries the sign.

The feature vector is kept unnormalised, as the paper's derivations use it. Its squared norm, $\bigl(1+2\sum_{k\ge1}T_k(x)^2\bigr)/N$, oscillates about one and reaches $(2N-1)/N$ at $x=\pm1$, and every constraint row of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints} inherits that weight. Normalising $\tau(x)$ point by point would change the rows' relative weights, and with them the least-squares solution, so the circuits load the unnormalised vector. A function of several variables is carried on one register per axis, each with its own $n$, with the feature vector $\tau(x)\otimes\tau(y)$ in Kronecker order. The running example of this chapter is the paper's Fig.~3b, the critically damped oscillator $f''+4f'+4f=0$ with $f(-1)=0$ and $f(0)=\tfrac12$, whose solution is $f=\tfrac12(1+x)e^{-2x}$; in the benchmark set of \Cref{tab:ladder} it is R2a.

\subsection{Differentiation and Multiplication}
\label{sec:standard-operators}
% ~0.9 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. 𝔾's entries (eq:pihm-G-entries, label kept), exact on the represented space (olver2013);
%      the paper prints the transpose (T-16, once); strictly upper triangular, nilpotent, ker 𝔾^j
%      the polynomials of degree < j; ||𝔾||_2/N^2 -> 1/(2 j_{-3/4,1}) (App. B, App. E);
%      orthogonally similar to the nodal differentiation matrix; ||𝔾^k||_2 ~ N^{2k}.
%   2. The lift (Paine et al.'s; the paper's Eq. 5): exact iff deg a <= N; a_0 M_1; ||M_a|| <=
%      sqrt2 sup|a|. The standard form keeps the lift; the descriptor's square form is Ch. 5's.
%   3. The equation block (eq:standard-equation), the source as the rank-one |r-hat><tau(x_s)|/f(x_s).
%   4. DEPARTURE (sources): Maclaurin to p-bar -> Chebyshev coefficients; R4d's order-7 series
%      off by more than the source itself at x = -1 (checked: 130 %); a floor -> spectral.
%   5. DEPARTURE (axes): two -> any number, Kronecker over axes (app:pihm-multi-axis); two run.
% CHECKLIST  Each fact about 𝔾 points to its proof; the lift credited.
% TRAPS  "Our differentiation matrix"; citing trefethen2000spectral for ||𝔾|| (Gauss-Lobatto
%   nodes, not these).
% REVISE: the R4d "more than the source itself" clause is a hand computation (order-7 series
%   -12.89 against -5.59 at x = -1); keep it numberless, or add a derived key.

In the normalised basis the differentiation matrix has the entries
\begin{equation}
    [\G]_{k,p} =
    \begin{cases}
        2p, & p > k \ge 1,\ p-k\ \text{odd}, \\
        \sqrt{2}\,p, & k = 0,\ p\ \text{odd}, \\
        0, & \text{otherwise},
    \end{cases}
    \label{eq:pihm-G-entries}
\end{equation}
and it is exact on the represented space, since the derivative of a polynomial of degree below $N$ stays in it \cite{olver2013}; the paper prints its transpose (\Cref{sec:pihm-residual}). $\G$ is strictly upper triangular and nilpotent, $\ker\G^{j}$ is the space of polynomials of degree below $j$, and about half the positions above the diagonal are non-zero. Its norm grows as $N^2$: $\|\G\|_2/N^2$ converges to $1/(2j_{-3/4,1})\approx0.472$, with $j_{-3/4,1}$ the first positive zero of the Bessel function $J_{-3/4}$, and $\|\G^k\|_2/N^{2k}$ converges to a positive constant for every $k$. Because the basis is orthonormal at the Chebyshev nodes, $\G$ is orthogonally similar to the matrix that differentiates nodal values, so the growth is not an artefact of the normalisation. The structure and the bounds are proved in \Cref{app:cheb-G-norm}, and the limits in \Cref{app:proof-standard-cost}.

A polynomial coefficient $a(x)$ acts by the lift of Paine et al.\ \cite{paine2023physicsinformed}, $x^p\bra{\tau(x)}_n=\bra{\tau(x)}_{n+1}\M_{x^p}$ (the paper's Eq.~5), extended by linearity to $\M_a=\sum_pa_p\M_{x^p}$. The lift is exact whenever $\deg a\le N$, because the product of a series of degree below $N$ with $a$ then has degree below $2N$ and fits the wider register without truncation. A constant enters as $a_0\M_1=a_0\sqrt2\,[\,I;\,0\,]$, the $\sqrt2$ being the ratio of the normalisations on $n+1$ and $n$ qubits, and $\|\M_a\|_2\le\sqrt2\sup|a|$. The standard form keeps the paper's lift; the descriptor form of \Cref{sec:descriptor} uses the square Galerkin truncation instead. The equation $\sum_ja_j(x)f^{(j)}=r(x)$ then has the equation block
\begin{equation}
    \Astd=\sum_{j}\M_{a_j}\G^{j}-\M_r\,\Dzero(x_s), \qquad \M_r\,\Dzero(x_s)=\frac{\ket{\hat r}\bra{\tau(x_s)}}{f(x_s)},
    \label{eq:standard-equation}
\end{equation}
in which the source enters through the collapse row of \Cref{sec:standard-constraints}, with $\hat r$ the coefficients of $r$ on the lifted register.

The paper expands a transcendental source in a Maclaurin series truncated at order $\bar p$ (\Cref{eq:pihm-A-source}). A truncated source poses a different equation, whose solution the ground state then approximates. The order-7 series of the source $x\,e^{-2x}\sin4x$ of the paper's Fig.~5d (R4d, $f''+4f'+20f=x\,e^{-2x}\sin4x$) differs from the source by more than the source itself at $x=-1$, and the resulting error is a floor that no increase in $n$ removes. The standard form loads the Chebyshev coefficients of the source instead, through the same collapse row, so the source is as accurate as the series that represents the solution.

The paper treats at most two variables. On any number of axes a term is the Kronecker product over the axes of $\M_{a}\G^{j_a}$, each axis lifted if any term on it needs the exact product, and a constant coefficient on a lifted axis enters through $\M_1$ (\Cref{app:pihm-multi-axis}). The benchmark problems use at most two.

\subsection{Constraints and Data}
\label{sec:standard-constraints}
% ~0.9 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. The paper's rows (Eqs 9-11): B(x) maps a series to the constant f_N(x); B(x)𝔾^j vanishes
%      exactly where f^{(j)}(x) = 0; D0(x_s) maps the solution to 1 whatever its scale; both
%      homogeneous; the regular datum fixes the scale (§4.1).
%   2. DEPARTURE (constraint points): rounded zeros/extrema (R4a-R4d, Fig. 5) -> the problem's
%      point, exact; a zero the solution lacks contradicts the equation, the error floors; one
%      pair of numbers (R4a: floor against the exact point's best). The exact point is problem
%      data inherited from the paper's statement, NOT answer-independent. Both printed choices
%      floor alone on R4b-R4d; only both replaced converge (pointer app:repro-triage).
%   3. The uniqueness count (OURS, prop:uniqueness-count): rank k-1 on the solution space plus
%      one regular datum, for a leading coefficient without zeros; the computed kernel decides
%      otherwise (trusted, sec:pipeline-reference).
%   4. DEPARTURE (PDE data -> datum slices): the Laplace modes argument; the kernel's dimension
%      at n = (5,5) (one number); heat and wave read off the solution; the slice rows
%      S_a(x*) - g-hat tau(x_s)^T / f(x_s) (app:datum-slice); answer-independent, well posed at
%      every n, both forms; the heat panel at t = -1 (forward).
%   5. DEPARTURE (double zero): R3c, R3d (Fig. 4c, d); the branch point; untrusted at the printed
%      n; v = f / sqrt(1 - x^2) (app:legendre-substitution); spectral; the descriptor's form.
%   6. The Laplace typo (A-05), one sentence; vector data -> ratio rows (sec:systems-data).
% CHECKLIST  Every departure a defect and a property; no "we fixed"; the T-27 rule (no variant
%   names here).
% TRAPS  "answer-independent" for the exact points; restating the paper's sets at length
%   (§3.2 has them); the heat problem "corrected".

The constraints are built from the paper's rank-one rows (its Eqs~9--11). The zero row $\Brow(x)=\sqrt{N}\ket{0}\bra{\tau(x)}$ maps a series to the constant function $f_N(x)$, so $\Brow(x)\G^{j}$ vanishes exactly on the series whose $j$-th derivative vanishes at $x$. The collapse row $\Dzero(x_s)=\Brow(x_s)/f(x_s)$ maps the solution to the constant $1$ whatever its scale, which is what lets a source enter \Cref{eq:standard-equation}. Both are homogeneous in the scale, so neither fixes it; the regular datum does, after preparation (\Cref{sec:standard-model}).

A zero row is exact only at a point where the solution's derivative of that order vanishes. The paper's inhomogeneous examples (its Fig.~5) place their invariant constraints at zeros and extrema of the known solution printed to two decimal places: R4a, $(x-1)f''-xf'+f=(x-1)^2$, imposes $f'(-0.2)=0$ for an extremum at $x\approx-0.19598$. At a rounded point the row demands a zero the solution does not have, so least squares trades the equation against the constraint and the error floors. On R4a, whose source is a polynomial and so represented exactly, the error stays at \res[2,sci]{build/R4a/faithful/standard/floor}{high} for every $n$ from 4 to 10, against \res[2,sci]{build/R4a/standard/best}{field_err_linf} at $n=\res{build/R4a/standard/best}{n}$ with the exact point. The standard form takes the point the problem defines, the zero or extremum the paper rounded, exactly. That point is problem data, inherited from the paper's statement, not something the method computes: a new problem must supply its constraint points with its data. On R4b to R4d the rounded point and the truncated source each floor the error on their own, and the error converges only with both replaced (the triage is in \Cref{app:repro-triage}).

How many constraints a problem needs is a property of the continuous equation. For a homogeneous equation of order $k$ whose leading coefficient has no zero on $[-1,1]$, the solutions form a $k$-dimensional space $S$, and those that satisfy the invariant constraints form a subspace of dimension $k-r$, where $r$ is the rank of the constraints restricted to $S$. The solution is unique up to the scale the regular datum fixes exactly when $r=k-1$: fewer leave a degenerate kernel, and $r=k$ leaves only $f=0$ (\Cref{prop:uniqueness-count}). The rank is that on $S$, not that of the functionals, so $k-1$ constraints chosen without regard to the equation can fail. Whether the truncated residual then has a one-dimensional kernel with a usable gap at a given $n$ is decided by computing it, and so is uniqueness for an inhomogeneous equation or a leading coefficient that vanishes (the trusted kernel, \Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}).

A partial differential equation has an infinite-dimensional solution space, and the count has no analogue. The paper's invariant sets (\Cref{sec:pihm-residual}) leave open what fixes the solution. On Laplace's equation (R5a) the modes $\sin\tfrac{\ell\pi(x+1)}{2}\,\sinh\tfrac{\ell\pi(y+1)}{2}$, $\ell\ge1$, all satisfy the equation and its three homogeneous sides, and only $\ell=1$ matches the omitted top side, which reaches the model through the regular datum alone. At the printed resolution the truncation excludes the others; as $n$ grows they become representable, and the kernel grows to dimension \res{build/R5a/faithful/standard/n5x5}{kernel_dimension} at $n=(5,5)$. The sets of the heat equation (R5b) and the wave equation (R5c) degenerate in the same way at larger $n$, and their interior points are read off the known solutions. The standard form imposes the initial or boundary function itself, on its slice. For a function $g$ on the slice $x_a=x^*$ the rows are
\begin{equation}
    \Slice_a(x^*)-\frac{\hat g\,\bra{\tau(x_s)}}{f(x_s)},
    \label{eq:datum-slice}
\end{equation}
with $\Slice_a(x^*)$ the zero row $\Brow(x^*)$ on axis $a$ and the identity on the others, and $\hat g$ the coefficients of $g$ broadcast along axis $a$. On a multiple $cf$ of the solution they return $c\,(f|_{x_a=x^*}-g)$, so they are homogeneous in the scale and vanish exactly on the solution and its multiples (\Cref{app:datum-slice}). These datum slices need nothing but the problem's data and serve both forms, and the kernel no longer degenerates as $n$ grows: once the resolution represents the solution, it is one-dimensional and trusted at every larger $n$ computed, to $n=(6,6)$. The heat equation's natural datum, its initial function at $t=0$, lies inside the time domain $[-1,1]$, and imposing it there would pose a backward heat problem on $[-1,0]$, which is ill posed; the standard form imposes the initial function at $t=-1$, chosen so that the solution is the paper's, and the problem runs forward.

The paper's associated Legendre examples R3c and R3d (its Figs~4c and~4d, $l=5$ and $6$ with $m=1$) multiply the equation through by $1-x^2$ to clear its $m^2/(1-x^2)$ term. That leaves the leading coefficient $(1-x^2)^2$, with a double zero at each end, and a solution $P_l^1=-\sqrt{1-x^2}\,P_l'$ with a branch point at each end, which a Chebyshev series approximates only algebraically: at the printed $n=5$ neither problem has a trusted ground state, and the error on R3c is \res[2,sci]{build/R3c/faithful/standard/n5}{field_err_linf}. The standard form solves for $v=f/\sqrt{1-x^2}$ instead, whose equation $(1-x^2)v''-4xv'+\bigl(l(l+1)-2\bigr)v=0$ has polynomial coefficients, a leading coefficient with simple zeros and a polynomial solution, so the series converges spectrally, to \res[2,sci]{build/R3c/standard/best}{field_err_linf} on R3c (\Cref{app:legendre-substitution}). It is also the form the descriptor can run (\Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}).

Two smaller points complete the data. The paper prints the solution of its Laplace example as $\operatorname{csch}\pi\,\cosh(\pi x/2)\sinh(\pi(y+1)/2)$, which does not satisfy its boundary conditions; the solution, and the reference used throughout, is $\cos(\pi x/2)\sinh(\pi(y+1)/2)/\sinh\pi$. Vector data, such as the initial values of a coupled system, enter as ratio rows, defined with systems in \Cref{sec:systems-data}.

\subsection{The Standard Form}
\label{sec:standard-form}

%   3. Definition (def:standard-form). tab:constraint-kinds as the model inputs.
%   4. Table T10 (tab:deviations) and the fairness sentence (data change, 𝔾 and the Gram
%      structure do not: the paper's norm and cost, better accuracy; conservative comparison).
%   5. The validation paragraph (the ONLY body trace of the reproduction; T-27 allows the
%      variant here): SM matrices; eleven ODE scale factors within one unit of the last printed

%      settled; pointer app:reproduction.
% CHECKLIST  The definition formal and complete; T10 every row a defect and a property; the
%   validation names the failures as well as the successes.
% TRAPS  A claims audit (App. K); "eigh" numbers beyond one; "R2a as printed" outside the

The equation block and the constraint rows are stacked into one residual,
\begin{equation}
    R_{\mathrm{std}}=\begin{pmatrix}\Astd\\ C_1\\ \vdots\\ C_L\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \Hstd=R_{\mathrm{std}}^{\dagger}R_{\mathrm{std}}=\Astd^{\dagger}\Astd+\sum_iC_i^{\dagger}C_i ,
    \label{eq:standard-stacked}
\end{equation}
every block at weight one, so that $\Hstd$ is the paper's sum of Gram matrices, \Cref{eq:pihm-hamiltonian}, with the datum slices among the $C_i$. The ground state of $\Hstd$ is the smallest right singular vector of $R_{\mathrm{std}}$. The classical reference computes it from $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ by a singular value decomposition, or by Lanczos iteration beyond its reach (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}), and never forms $\Hstd$, which would square the residual's condition number: on R2a at $n=8$ the relative gap of $\Hstd$ is \res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/n8}{relative_gap}, below double precision. The circuit likewise encodes $R_{\mathrm{std}}$, not $\Hstd$ (\Cref{sec:pipeline-walk}).

Written this way, $\Hstd$ is positive semi-definite, and in the very case the method aims at, a solution the truncation represents exactly, it is not definite, contrary to the paper's description (\Cref{sec:pihm-residual}). When the solution is a polynomial of degree below $N$, $\G$ differentiates it and the lifts multiply it exactly, so its coefficients are annihilated by $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ and give $\Hstd$ an exact zero eigenvalue; Legendre's equation $(1-x^2)f''-2xf'+20f=0$ (R3a, the paper's Fig.~4a), whose solution is $P_4$, has one from $n=3$. Otherwise the truncated solution leaves a residual, $\lambda_0>0$, and the ground state minimises the total squared residual: the construction is least squares. Nor need the bottom of the spectrum be distinct, as the paper's partial differential sets show. The thesis relies on neither property; it relies on an isolated ground state, measured as a trusted kernel (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}).

\begin{definition}[The standard form]
\label{def:standard-form}
For a linear problem on one or more Chebyshev axes, the \emph{standard form} is the stacked residual $R_{\mathrm{std}}$ of \Cref{eq:standard-stacked}, whose equation block is \Cref{eq:standard-equation}, lifted on every axis where a coefficient or a source needs the exact product, and whose constraint rows are of four kinds, each at weight one: zero rows $\Brow(x^*)\G^{j}$ at a point or on a slice; the collapse row $\Dzero(x_s)$, through which sources enter; ratio rows for vector data (\Cref{sec:systems-data}); and datum slices (\Cref{eq:datum-slice}) for initial and boundary functions. Its Hamiltonian is $\Hstd=R_{\mathrm{std}}^{\dagger}R_{\mathrm{std}}$, and its solution is the smallest right singular vector of $R_{\mathrm{std}}$, scaled by the regular datum $f(x_s)=y_s$.
\end{definition}

\Cref{tab:constraint-kinds} lists the four kinds as model inputs, and a benchmark label such as R2a names the problem with the data of this section. \Cref{tab:deviations} collects every departure from the printed method: those argued above, and those of the pipeline, argued where they are built. Each changes the data a residual carries, or how the residual is solved, encoded, filtered or read, and none changes $\G$, the lifts or the Gram structure. The standard form therefore has the paper's Hamiltonian norm to leading order, and with it the paper's cost, and is only more accurate, so a comparison against it is conservative. The one exception is R3c and R3d, whose substitution changes the equation itself; their cost is compared in the form the standard form runs.

\subsection{Encoding the Derivative}
\label{sec:standard-factorisation}
% ~1.6 pp in three parts. The paper's encoding (the encoder's cost made visible); the exact
% factorisation (removes it); its circuits and cost (within a constant of the norm).
% Lead-in, one sentence: 𝔾 is the term whose encoding decides the encoder's cost.

Every term of the standard form is a product of lifts, rank-one rows and powers of $\G$. The rank-one rows have exact circuits whose subnormalisation is fixed by their own norm, so the term whose encoding decides the encoder's cost is $\G$.

\subsubsection{The Paper's Encoding}
\label{sec:standard-published}

% CHECKLIST  "Published" nowhere in visible text; the rebuild framed as costing the paper's

The paper's circuit for $\G$ (its Fig.~9; \Cref{sec:pihm-encoding}) selects each entry $(k,p)$ by a register subtraction over a superposition of offsets and loads the column value $2p$ as an amplitude through a ladder of small controlled rotations. Its claims about gates and entry errors hold, the first as a bound and the second to leading order (\Cref{app:published-build}). The claim that decides its cost is the prefactor of \Cref{eq:pihm-published-prefactor}, which is valid but not compared with $\|\G\|_2$. Since $\|\G\|_S=\|\G^{\top}\G\|_1$ grows as $N^4/3$, the prefactor grows as $N^5/2$, against $\|\G\|_2\sim N^2/(2j_{-3/4,1})$, so its looseness tends to $j_{-3/4,1}N^3$ with $j_{-3/4,1}\approx1.06$; at $n=7$ it is \res[2,sci]{derivative/n7}{published_ratio} (exact classical computation, \Cref{app:alpha-G}). A circuit with polynomially many gates is cheap to apply once, but the filter applies it a number of times set by its subnormalisation (\Cref{eq:degree}): the encoding is efficient in gates and inefficient in subnormalisation. To cost it like the others, we rebuild its mechanism with registers of our own, the offsets restricted to odd values, since an even offset would load values onto entries of $\G$ that vanish. The rebuilt circuit's own subnormalisation is a third of the printed prefactor, $N\|\G\|_S/2$ against $3N\|\G\|_S/2$, so every cost this thesis quotes for the paper's encoding is the kinder reading. Its entries are approximate, with an error of order $N^{-5}$ (\Cref{prop:epsilon-G}), so no state is prepared with it; it enters the resource estimates only, composed into the same stacked residual as the exact encoding below. The rebuilt circuit, its error bound and the verdict on each of the paper's circuit claims are given in \Cref{app:published-build}.

\subsubsection{An Exact Factorisation}
\label{sec:standard-exact}
% ~0.5 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. The idea in words: each non-zero entry is a product of three factors, each of one index
%      (row weight, parity-selected offset, column value 2p).
%   2. prop:G-factorisation (C-01), proof in App. E.
%   3. The measured error as a check on the implementation, not as evidence.
% REVISE: 3.4e-13 is a one-off measurement (circuit block vs the assembled 𝔾, n <= 7), typed as
%   \prov; replace with a derived key or delete the number (plan §2.1).

The paper's circuit is approximate, and its prefactor large, because it loads the column value of each entry as a rotation amplitude. The structure of $\G$ allows a different route. Every non-zero entry of \Cref{eq:pihm-G-entries} is a product of three factors, each depending on one index alone: a row weight, equal to $1/\sqrt2$ in row~0 because of the normalisation of $T_0$ and to one elsewhere; a selector depending only on the offset $p-k$, non-zero when $p>k$ and $p-k$ is odd; and the column value $2p$, the weight with which differentiation passes $T_p$ to the lower polynomials. Each factor has an exact block encoding with no amplitude loaded by rotation.

\begin{proposition}[Exact factorisation of the differentiation matrix]
\label{prop:G-factorisation}
For every $n \ge 1$ the differentiation matrix on the normalised Chebyshev coefficients factorises as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{gathered}
        \G = R_0\,\bigl(2\Uodd\bigr)\,\Lambda, \\
        R_0 = I - \Bigl(1 - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}\Bigr)\ket{0}\bra{0},
        \qquad
        \bigl(\Uodd\bigr)_{kp} = \bigl[\,p > k,\ p - k\ \text{odd}\,\bigr],
        \qquad
        \Lambda = \operatorname{diag}(p) ,
    \end{gathered}
    \label{eq:G-structured}
\end{equation}
where $\Uodd$ is the sum of the $N/2$ truncated shifts by odd offsets.
\end{proposition}
The proof compares the two sides entry by entry with \Cref{eq:pihm-G-entries} and is given in \Cref{app:proof-G-factorisation}. As a check on our implementation, not on the proposition, the block of the composed circuit matches the independently assembled $\G$ entrywise to $\prov{3.4\times10^{-13}}$ (absolute) for $n\le7$.

\subsubsection{Circuits and Cost}
\label{sec:standard-cost}
% ~0.7 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. Lambda as a Z-string LCU (eq:lambda-lcu), alpha = ||Lambda||; R_0 as two terms, alpha = 1.
%   2. U_odd: PREPARE over odd offsets, SELECT an in-place subtraction with a borrow flag,
%      post-selected; alpha = N/2; F3.
%   3. prop:standard-cost (alpha_G = N(N-1) -> 2 j_{-3/4,1} ||𝔾||_2; O(n^2) gates; n + 4
%      ancilla, n - 1 the selector); measured ratios (tab:alpha-G-short); where the 2.117 comes
%      from.
%   4. The n - 1 selector qubits: forced by an L-term LCU (chakraborty2025qsvt); the descriptor
%      never forms the sum of shifts (O(log n)).
%   5. The rest of the residual from the same pieces (lifts, rows, sources, powers on one
%      ancilla register); one reflection (sec:pipeline-walk), verified (sec:pipeline-verification).
% REVISE: the two-qubit gate counts at n = 2 and 5 are \prov (transpiled once, not exported).

Each factor is encoded as a linear combination of unitaries \cite{childs2012lcu} whose terms are unitary already, so no part of the encoding is approximate. The column index is a sum of Pauli strings,
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda = \frac{N-1}{2}\,I - \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} 2^{j-1} Z_j ,
    \label{eq:lambda-lcu}
\end{equation}
since $p=\sum_j2^j(1-z_j)/2$ in terms of the eigenvalues $z_j$ of $Z_j$. Its $n+1$ terms need $\lceil\log_2(n+1)\rceil$ selector qubits and $O(n)$ gates, and its subnormalisation $\alpha_\Lambda=N-1$ equals $\|\Lambda\|_2$. The row weight is the two-term combination $R_0=aI+b\,(I-2\ket{0}\!\bra{0})$ with $a=\tfrac12(1+1/\sqrt2)$ and $b=\tfrac12(1-1/\sqrt2)$, both positive, so $\alpha_{R_0}=1=\|R_0\|_2$.

The sum of shifts is the one factor that needs arithmetic (\Cref{fig:uodd-select}). Its PREPARE puts an offset register in the uniform superposition over the $N/2$ odd offsets, by an $X$ on the lowest qubit and Hadamards on the rest. Its SELECT is the in-place subtraction $p\leftarrow p-d$ of a ripple-carry circuit \cite{cuccaro2004adder}, which sets a borrow flag when $p<d$. The terms that would wrap around carry a borrow of one, and post-selecting the flag on zero removes them exactly. The block is therefore $\tfrac{2}{N}\bigl[p>k,\ p-k\ \text{odd}\bigr]$, an exact encoding of $\Uodd$ at $\alpha=N/2$ with $O(n)$ Toffoli gates, and one PREPARE serves all $N/2$ terms because SELECT is a permutation (\Cref{app:uodd-select-detail}).

The three encodings multiply, and so do their subnormalisations. Their product is an exact block encoding of $\G$ at $\alpha_{\G}=1\cdot N\cdot(N-1)$, with the following cost.

\begin{proposition}[Cost of the structured encoding]
\label{prop:standard-cost}
The factorisation of \Cref{prop:G-factorisation} compiles to an exact block encoding of $\G$ on $n$ system qubits with
\begin{equation}
    \alpha_{\G} = N(N-1),
    \qquad
    \frac{\alpha_{\G}}{\|\G\|_2} \longrightarrow 2j_{-3/4,1} \approx 2.117,
    \qquad
    O(n^2)\ \text{gates},
    \qquad
    n + 4\ \text{ancilla} ,
    \label{eq:standard-cost}
\end{equation}
of which $n-1$ ancilla are the selector of the $N/2$-term combination $\Uodd$.
\end{proposition}
The limit is that of $\|\G\|_2/N^2$ (\Cref{sec:standard-operators}); the gate count assumes an ancilla-free synthesis of multi-controlled gates, and falls to $O(n\log n)$ with a linear-cost synthesis that borrows idle qubits \cite{barenco1995elementary}. The proof is given in \Cref{app:proof-standard-cost}. The ancilla count is exact in the circuit for $n=2$ to $8$. The transpiled two-qubit gate counts are available only at small $n$ (\prov{109} at $n=2$ and \prov{471} at $n=5$), consistent with the bound but too few to fit it. The limit is asymptotic, and the sizes we reach are measured: the ratio $\alpha_{\G}/\|\G\|_2$ is $\res[3]{derivative/n2}{structured_ratio}$ at $n=2$ and rises to $\res[3]{derivative/n10}{structured_ratio}$ at $n=10$ (\Cref{tab:alpha-G-short}), against a ratio for the paper's encoding that grows as $N^3$. The structured encoding is this close to the norm because $\alpha_{R_0}$ and $\alpha_\Lambda$ equal their factors' norms. Its looseness comes from $\Uodd$, whose $\alpha$ exceeds its norm by at most $\pi/2$, and from the product, in which the large entries of $\Lambda$ meet the columns of $\Uodd$ with few non-zero entries.

The $n-1$ selector qubits are not an artefact of this circuit. A combination of $L$ terms needs $\lceil\log_2L\rceil$ selector qubits, and lower bounds of this kind hold for exact block encodings built from $L$-term decompositions \cite{chakraborty2025qsvt}, so the count is the price of encoding the $N/2$ shifts of $\Uodd$ as one combination. It marks a structural difference between the two forms: the descriptor form of \Cref{sec:descriptor-block-encoding} never forms the sum of shifts, and its ancilla grow as $\log n$.

The remaining terms of the residual are built from the same pieces. A coefficient $a(x)$ is encoded through its lift as a sum of powers of the single-$x$ lift, itself a combination of truncated shifts built by the same post-selected subtraction, so its subnormalisation is the sum of the absolute monomial coefficients weighted by powers of that lift's (\Cref{app:lifts-folds}). The rank-one rows $\Brow(x)$ and $\Dzero(x_s)$ are the paper's exact circuits, and a source given by its Chebyshev coefficients is loaded by one rotation tree. A power $\G^k$ is a product of encodings that share one ancilla register, with a failure flag per product, so $\G^2$ costs one qubit more than $\G$. The subnormalisation of a product is the product of the subnormalisations, so a power compounds the constant above while $\|\G^k\|_2<\|\G\|_2^k$. The stacked residual is then assembled into a single reflection (\Cref{sec:pipeline-walk}), and the composed circuit is verified against the independently assembled Hamiltonian (\Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}).

\subsection{What Remains: The Standard-Form Hamiltonian}
\label{sec:standard-hamiltonian}
% ~1.2 pp. LAND, in order:
%   1. Constant coefficients: alpha_R/||R|| bounded on R2a (looseness keys, n = 2..8); alpha_H
%      = alpha_R^2. The encoder has done its job.
%   2. ||H_std|| = Theta(N^{4k}) (proved for constant coefficients, a_k != 0, constraints of
%      lower order; app:proof-standard-cost) against a gap measured flat: R2a and R2b (Fig. 3c)
%      directly; the ideal-bound degree's exponent 2k on R1, R2 and the constant-coefficient R4.
%      eq:standard-wall; the Hamiltonian's cost; the floor for the minimax filter, hedged to
%      Lin & Tong. The n = 8 degrees as built and at alpha = ||R|| (T3).
%   3. One query against the number of queries; the paper leaves the gap to future work.
%   4. The degree's growth with the paper's, the structured and the ideal encoding (growth
%      keys); the classical reading (normal equations; cai1994fosls; §2.3).
%   5. The scope: R3's lifted multiplier (C-02); the ideal bound beside every as-built cost;
%      R3's reversal reported.
%   6. Close: the answer to Q(i); Ch. 5 changes the Hamiltonian.
% CHECKLIST  "measured" for the gap, "proved" for the norm; every degree a resource estimate.
% TRAPS  Claiming the gap flat in general; a floor "for any filter" without the hedge; "wall".

For constant coefficients the structured encoding removes the encoder's cost from the whole residual, not only from $\G$. On R2a at $n=8$ the structured encoding of the stacked residual has $\alpha_R=\res[3,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_R}$ against $\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2=\res[3,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{norm_R}$, a ratio of $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ (a resource estimate; the circuit at this size is not simulated). The ratio rises from $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n2/T3/filter=minimax}{alpha_ratio}$ at $n=2$ with increments that roughly halve at each added qubit, so it is levelling off over $n=2$ to $8$, although we have not proved a limit for the whole residual. The Hamiltonian is encoded at $\alpha_H=\alpha_R^2$, so $\alpha_H/\|\Hstd\|$ is the square of that ratio, from $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n2/T3/filter=minimax}{hamiltonian_ratio}$ to $\res[3]{looseness/stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{hamiltonian_ratio}$, whereas the paper's encoding of $\G$ alone is loose by a factor growing as $N^3$.

No encoder can change the norm it encodes. For an equation of order $k$ with constant coefficients, a non-zero leading coefficient and constraints of lower order, $\|\G^k\|_2/N^{2k}$ tends to a positive constant, so $\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2=\Theta(N^{2k})$ and $\|\Hstd\|=\|R_{\mathrm{std}}\|_2^2=\Theta(N^{4k})$ (\Cref{app:proof-standard-cost}). The factor $N^2$ per derivative is the norm of $\G$, and the exponent doubles because the Hamiltonian is the Gram product $R^\dagger R$. The gap, by contrast, is measured, not proved. On R2a and on $f''-2f'-3f=0$ (R2b, the paper's Fig.~3c) it does not grow with $N$: on R2a $\Delta=\res[3]{build/R2a/standard/n7}{gap}$ at $n=7$ and $\res[3]{build/R2a/standard/n8}{gap}$ at $n=8$, while $\|\Hstd\|$ grows from $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/n7}{hamiltonian_norm}$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/n8}{hamiltonian_norm}$. Since $\alpha_H\ge\|\Hstd\|$ for every block encoding, \Cref{eq:degree} gives
\begin{equation}
    \|\Hstd\| = \Theta\bigl(N^{4k}\bigr),
    \qquad
    \Delta = \Theta(1)\ \text{(measured)}
    \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
    d = \tilde\Theta\bigl(N^{2k}\bigr),
    \label{eq:standard-wall}
\end{equation}
which at second order, the order of the paper's examples, is $\|\Hstd\|=\Theta(N^8)$ and $d=\tilde\Theta(N^4)$ whatever the block encoding. This is the Hamiltonian's cost. The degree at the ideal-encoding bound $\alpha=\|R\|_2$, which contains no encoding, grows as $N^{2k}$ on the constant-coefficient one-axis problems measured, from $n=7$ to $8$: as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R1/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$ on the first-order R1 and as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$ on R2a, as \Cref{eq:standard-wall} predicts with a flat gap. The floor is stated for the minimax filter that prepares every state in this thesis; for polynomial filters in general it is what the optimality of Lin and Tong's construction supports \cite{lintong2020groundstate}, and we claim no more. At $n=8$ the estimated degree for R2a is $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$ as built and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$ at the ideal-encoding bound, both resource estimates far beyond what exact emulation reaches. The degree is linear in $\alpha_R$, so the ratio of the two is the encoder's looseness and the second is the Hamiltonian's share.

The polynomial gate count that Wu et al.\ report for their operators \cite{wu2025pihm}, and that the rebuilt circuits bear out (\Cref{app:published-build}), is the cost of one query to the block encoding. The cost of the method is that cost times the number of queries, and \Cref{eq:standard-wall} fixes the number of queries by the norm, however few gates a query takes. The paper does not claim otherwise: it leaves the gap, and so the preparation time, to future work. Its claim of efficiency is therefore true of the circuit and silent on the method, and polynomial gates per query do not make the standard form efficient, since its filter makes $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ queries or more, which is exponential in $n$.

The growth of the degree between $n=7$ and $n=8$, again from resource estimates, shows the two costs apart. With the paper's encoding the degree grows as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax/encoder=published}{degree}}$, with the structured encoding as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}}$, and at the ideal bound as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$. The first difference is the encoder's cost, which the structured encoding removes; the common exponent is the Hamiltonian's. It belongs to the least-squares functional, not to the circuits: $\Hstd=R^\dagger R$ is the normal-equations form of the least-squares problem for an operator of order $k$, and such a problem squares the conditioning of the operator it minimises. The classical remedies are to recast the equation as a first-order system \cite{cai1994fosls} or to use a basis in which differentiation is banded (\Cref{sec:bg-fosls,sec:bg-ultraspherical}).

The conclusion has a limited scope. The structured encoding bounds $\alpha_H/\|\Hstd\|$ for constant coefficients only, and is loose for a variable coefficient that is lifted. On R3a the circuit pays for the product $\M_a\G^2$ as $\|\M_a\|\,\|\G^2\|\gg\|\M_a\G^2\|$, because the coefficient $1-x^2$ vanishes at the ends of the interval, where the derivatives of Chebyshev polynomials are largest. The measured norm of this residual grows as $N^2$, not as $N^{2k}=N^4$, and the encoder's cost reappears: the as-built degree grows as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}}$, while at the ideal bound it grows as $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/standard/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$, the exponent the descriptor's degree also has there ($N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/descriptor/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}}$ at the ideal bound). At $n=8$ the as-built degree is $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R3a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$ against $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R3a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$ at the ideal bound, both resource estimates. For this reason \Cref{sec:results-scaling} quotes the ideal-encoding bound beside every as-built cost, and where the comparison between the forms reverses at that bound, as it does on R3, reports the reversal as a finding.

This answers the first question. For the constant-coefficient problems of the paper's regime the cost of the method has two parts: the encoder's, a factor growing as $N^3$ in the subnormalisation of the paper's encoding of $\G$, which the structured encoding removes; and the Hamiltonian's, $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ in the filter's degree, which no encoding removes. \Cref{sec:results-scaling} tests both on the benchmark problems. What remains open to change is the Hamiltonian itself, through what its residual carries, and \Cref{sec:descriptor} changes it by carrying the derivatives as unknowns instead of eliminating them into the dense $\G$.
