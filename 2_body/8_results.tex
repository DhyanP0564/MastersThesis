The formulation chapters made their claims about operators. For constant coefficients the standard form's norm grows as $N^{4k}$ against a flat gap; the descriptor's falls to $\Theta(N^2)$ at fixed block weights, at the price of a gap its derivative blocks erode; and a Carleman lift prepares where the doubled space projects. Each claim predicts a filter degree through \Cref{eq:degree}, and none of those degrees has yet been measured. The measurements below, on the benchmark problems of \Cref{tab:ladder} with the pipeline and conventions of \Cref{sec:pipeline}, follow the order in which the predictions depend on one another. The pipeline is shown to compute what it claims before any cost is compared; the standard form's two costs are separated before the descriptor is set against them; the gap is measured where the norm's advantage could be lost; and the lifts come last, since the question of advantage arises only once a nonlinear equation is prepared at all. Every result is a classical computation and names its tier, T1, T2 or T3 (\Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}); a gap, a norm or a reference's error is an exact classical computation on the assembled residual. The results for every problem in both forms are tabulated in \Cref{app:catalogue-results}; the reproduction of Wu et al.'s own panels, which tests the published method rather than the three questions, is recorded in \Cref{app:reproduction}.

Solved classically through its reference (\Cref{sec:pipeline-reference}), the standard form converges spectrally on every linear benchmark problem: the smallest trusted error of the decoded field is $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/best}{field_err_linf}$ on R2a at $n=\res{build/R2a/standard/best}{n}$, $\res[2,sci]{build/R4b/standard/best}{field_err_linf}$ on the inhomogeneous R4b at $n=\res{build/R4b/standard/best}{n}$, and $\res[2,sci]{build/R5b/standard/best}{field_err_linf}$ on the heat equation at $n=\res{build/R5b/standard/best}{n}$ per axis. The descriptor, whose kernel is the standard form's (\Cref{thm:kernel-equivalence}), reaches $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/descriptor/best}{field_err_linf}$ on R2a. Beyond its best resolution the standard form's error rises again, to $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/standard/n10}{field_err_linf}$ on R2a at $n=10$, where the descriptor's is $\res[2,sci]{build/R2a/descriptor/n10}{field_err_linf}$. The rise is consistent with rounding error amplified by the conditioning of a residual whose norm grows as $N^4$, an early sign of the Hamiltonian's cost of \Cref{sec:results-scaling}.

\paragraph{The circuits encode the operators.} Every verified circuit meets its mode's tolerance (\Cref{tab:verification-modes,fig:verification-accuracy}) by at least four orders of magnitude. The exact mode's worst entrywise error is $\res[2,sci]{encode/R6a/standard/n3}{verify_max_error}$, on a reflection of the doubled space, and its widest verification is a whole reflection on $\res{encode/R4d/faithful/standard/n3}{n_qubits_total}$ qubits, of Wu et al.'s own R4d panel, whose Maclaurin source widens the register, at $\res[2,sci]{encode/R4d/faithful/standard/n3}{verify_max_error}$. The probe mode's worst is $\res[2,sci]{encode/R10c-M12/descriptor/n2/carleman=2/verify=probe}{verify_max_error}$, on a Navier--Stokes lift's reflection of $\res{encode/R10c-M12/descriptor/n2/carleman=2/verify=probe}{n_qubits_total}$ qubits, and the \gls{ir} mode verifies the tensor layout's coupling on $\res{lift/R10c-M316/standard/n0/carleman=2/lift=tensor/verify=ir}{modes}$ modes to $\res[2,sci]{lift/R10c-M316/standard/n0/carleman=2/lift=tensor/verify=ir}{verify_max_error}$. Every composed Hamiltonian of at most 24 qubits is therefore verified whole, and every lift and coupling by its terms at any size. The wider reflections, those of the two-axis descriptor problems from $\res{encode/R5b/descriptor/n2x2/verify=none}{n_qubits_total}$ qubits and of the larger lifts, are built and counted but not independently verified (\Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}).

\paragraph{The circuit computes the emulation.} On every circuit simulation with the minimax filter the simulated output agrees with the exact emulation's before normalising, within the bar of $10^{-10}$. The worst difference is $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R4a/descriptor/n3/T1/filter=minimax}{filtered_agreement}$ in the descriptor (R4a at $n=3$, degree $\res{stateprep/R4a/descriptor/n3/T1/filter=minimax}{degree}$), $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R4a/standard/n2/T1/filter=minimax}{filtered_agreement}$ in the standard form (R4a at $n=2$, degree $\res{stateprep/R4a/standard/n2/T1/filter=minimax}{degree}$), and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R9c-M4/descriptor/n2/T1/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{filtered_agreement}$ on a Carleman lift (Burgers on four modes, $\res{stateprep/R9c-M4/descriptor/n2/T1/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits), each the rounding of a statevector carried through hundreds to thousands of controlled walks. This confirms, where both run, the argument of \Cref{sec:pipeline-verification} that the exact emulation is the circuit's output, which is what licenses the emulation beyond the widths simulated. The widest circuit simulated for a problem as run has $\res{stateprep/R4d/descriptor/n3/T1/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits; the two-axis descriptor circuits, from $\res{stateprep/R5b/descriptor/n2x2/T1/filter=minimax}{n_qubits}$ qubits, are beyond the simulation budget, the narrowest estimated at $\res[2]{stateprep/R5b/descriptor/n2x2/T1/filter=minimax}{predicted_seconds}$\,s against a budget of $\res[2]{stateprep/R5b/descriptor/n2x2/T1/filter=minimax}{budget_seconds}$\,s, which is a limit of the classical simulation and not of the circuit. The imaginary-time filter's circuit simulations, run in the reproduction, meet the same bar on all but three records, at degrees of order $10^5$; others at higher degrees meet it with room to spare, so the rounding depends on the problem as well as the degree. The three are reported in \Cref{app:repro-t1}.

\paragraph{Every prepared state meets its target.} Every trusted preparation by exact emulation meets $\varepsilon$ and its a priori bound, in both forms, on every linear problem and every Carleman lift, and its decoded field meets the error budget of \Cref{eq:error-budget}; the values per problem are given in \Cref{tab:catalogue-results}. The bound is tight: on R2a in the descriptor at $n=8$ the infidelity is $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{infidelity}$ against the bound $(1-\gamma^2)\varepsilon^2/\gamma^2=\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{infidelity_bound}$ of \Cref{sec:pipeline-filter}. \Cref{fig:decoded-field} carries the state's error to the field. The field's error falls spectrally with $n$ until it meets the filter's contribution, and then holds: on R2a in the descriptor it is $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n4/T2/filter=minimax}{field_err_linf}$ at $n=4$ and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{field_err_linf}$ at $n=8$, while the discretisation's own error falls to $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{eps_disc}$. From $n=4$ the prepared field's accuracy is therefore set by $\varepsilon$: a more accurate field needs a smaller $\varepsilon$, at a degree logarithmic in $1/\varepsilon$ (\Cref{eq:minimax-degree}), where a larger $n$ gains nothing. The budget itself, $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R2a/descriptor/n8/T2/filter=minimax}{field_budget}$ there, is conservative by more than an order of magnitude, consistent with a readout bound that takes the state's worst direction rather than its actual one.

\paragraph{What answer independence costs.} The initial state and the spectral edge are the two inputs chosen without the solution. The geometric state of \Cref{eq:geometric-state} guesses that the solution's coefficients decay geometrically, and it is good where they do: on R1 at $n=3$ its overlap is $\gamma^2=\res[3]{stateprep/R1/standard/n3/T2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$, against $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R1/standard/n3/T2/kind=uniform/filter=minimax/initial-state-control}{gamma2}$ for the uniform state and $\res[2]{stateprep/R1/standard/n3/T2/kind=random/filter=minimax/initial-state-control}{gamma2}$ for a random one. It is poor where the solution is a polynomial of low degree: on Legendre's equation R3a, whose solution is $P_4$, the uniform state's $\gamma^2$ is $\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/standard/n3/T2/kind=uniform/filter=minimax/initial-state-control}{gamma2}$ against the geometric state's $\res[3]{stateprep/R3a/standard/n3/T2/filter=minimax}{gamma2}$ (exact emulation, T2). The degree does not depend on the state, so every control meets $\varepsilon$ at the same degree, and a poor guess costs the factor $1/\gamma$ of amplification in \Cref{eq:total-cost}. An underestimated edge costs degree, as an inverse square root of the gap (\Cref{eq:minimax-degree}), and keeps the target, since it only widens the interval suppressed; a study on R2a bears this out, and in it Wu et al.'s own time rule falls short of $\varepsilon$ (\Cref{app:edge-study}).

\paragraph{The filter study.} The comparison of the two filters, at three strengths of like error, is tabulated in \Cref{app:filter-study}. At equal suppression with the ground state at the spectrum's edge, imaginary time needs $\prov{1.67}$--$\prov{1.92}$ times the minimax filter's degree on synthetic spectra, whatever the gap, and every trusted reference has its ground state at the edge, so on the benchmark problems the certified ratio lies in that range in both forms. At equal measured infidelity the gain is smaller, because imaginary time suppresses the higher excited states more strongly than the equiripple bound assumes, and at the loose $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$ imaginary time needs fewer degrees on three of the eight cases. Because the ratio does not depend on the gap, the choice of filter changes the degree's constant and not its growth with $N$.

In the standard form on one axis, with constant coefficients, the degree grows as $N^{2k}$ as built and at the ideal bound alike: the structured encoding holds the encoder's cost to a constant, and what remains is the Hamiltonian's. The stiff R2c is the exception as built, at $N^{\res[3]{stateprep/R2c/descriptor/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}}$, because its degree is still rising towards the asymptotic regime at $n=8$; its ideal-bound exponent is already $\res[3]{stateprep/R2c/descriptor/growth/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$. Where the leading coefficient vanishes (R3, R4a) the descriptor's degree grows as about $N^2$, the price of the rescaling's $\zeta\propto N$ (\Cref{sec:descriptor-rescaling}). On two axes it grows faster than $N^2$ per axis yet more slowly than the standard form's. These are slopes from $n=4$ to $5$ per axis, the largest resolutions estimated, and the least settled in the table; on the wave equation R5c, whose reference is trusted only from $n=5$ per axis, they are indicative only.

\paragraph{The reversals.} Two comparisons reverse, and both are findings. The first is R3 at the ideal bound, where the standard form needs the smaller degree on all four problems (\Cref{tab:catalogue-t3}): on R3a at $n=8$, $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R3a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$ against the descriptor's $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R3a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$. As built the order is the other way, $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R3a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$ against $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R3a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{degree}$, because the structured encoding pays for R3's lifted multiplier in full; the as-built advantage there is the encoder's, not the Hamiltonian's. The reversal belongs to R3 rather than to every vanishing coefficient. R3's coefficient $1-x^2$ vanishes at both ends of the interval, where the derivatives of the Chebyshev polynomials are largest, and its standard-form norm grows only as $N^4$, from $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/standard/n7}{hamiltonian_norm}$ at $n=7$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R3a/standard/n8}{hamiltonian_norm}$ at $n=8$. The coefficient $x-1$ of R4a vanishes at one end only; its $\|\Hstd\|$ grows as $N^8$, from $\res[2,sci]{build/R4a/standard/n7}{hamiltonian_norm}$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R4a/standard/n8}{hamiltonian_norm}$, and there the descriptor's degree is the lower at the ideal bound too, $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R4a/descriptor/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$ against $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R4a/standard/n8/T3/filter=minimax}{ideal_degree}$. That the endpoint concentration is what lowers R3's norm is an inference from these two problems, consistent with \Cref{sec:standard-hamiltonian}, not a theorem. The second reversal is the heat equation at small $n$, taken up with its gap in \Cref{sec:results-gap}.

\paragraph{Queries and gates.} The degree is not the whole cost. On R2a one query of the descriptor costs more two-qubit gates than one of the structured standard form at every resolution built and verified, $\res{encode/R2a/descriptor/n2}{cx_count}$ against $\res{encode/R2a/standard/n2}{cx_count}$ at $n=2$ and $\res{encode/R2a/descriptor/n5/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ against $\res{encode/R2a/standard/n5/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ at $n=5$ (\Cref{fig:verification-accuracy}), having more terms and a block register; its count grows more slowly, and the estimates at $n=8$ put the two at parity (\Cref{tab:headline}). Wu et al.'s encoding is the cheapest of the three per query and the loosest. On R3a, whose lifted multiplier the structured encoding pays for in full, the descriptor's query is the cheaper from $n=4$: $\res{encode/R3a/descriptor/n4/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ against $\res{encode/R3a/standard/n4/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ at $n=4$ and $\res{encode/R3a/descriptor/n5/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ against $\res{encode/R3a/standard/n5/verify=probe}{cx_count}$ at $n=5$. The ancilla's growth, $\mathcal{O}(\log n)$ against $n$ (\Cref{sec:descriptor-cost}), is not yet visible at these sizes: the whole reflection on R2a needs $\res{encode/R2a/descriptor/n2}{n_anc_qubits}$ ancilla in the descriptor at $n=2$ and $\res{encode/R2a/descriptor/n5/verify=probe}{n_anc_qubits}$ at $n=5$, against $\res{encode/R2a/standard/n2}{n_anc_qubits}$ and $\res{encode/R2a/standard/n5/verify=probe}{n_anc_qubits}$ in the standard form. On the constant-coefficient problems the descriptor's advantage is therefore in the number of queries, and that number dominates the whole preparation's gate count (\Cref{tab:headline}). No number at $n=8$ is a simulation: the degrees are resource estimates (\Cref{sec:pipeline-design}); the descriptor's agree with exact emulation on R2a through $n=8$, while the standard form's lies far beyond the $2^{20}$ that exact emulation reaches (\Cref{sec:pipeline-verification}), and its size is itself the quantity under test.

The answer to the first question is therefore measured as well as argued: with Wu et al.'s encoding the cost is mostly the encoder's, an exact encoding removes that part, and what remains, $\tilde\Theta(N^{2k})$ in the degree for constant coefficients on one axis, is the Hamiltonian's. The descriptor removes the Hamiltonian's part on one-axis problems whose leading coefficient does not vanish; where it vanishes the descriptor's degree still grows as $N^2$, and on R3 at the ideal bound the standard form's is the lower.

\paragraph{Burgers (R9): a trusted kernel can hide a diverging lift.} \Cref{fig:nonlinear-burgers} measures the lift's convergence on viscous Burgers from the data $a\sin x$, against the strength $a/\nu$ and the number of sine modes $M$, with $\nu=1$. The lift's error, its exact solution against the Galerkin system's, falls by a factor of about $a/(5\nu)$ an order: at $a/\nu=1$ from $\res[2,sci]{build/R9c-M4/descriptor/n5/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=2$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R9c-M4/descriptor/n5/carleman=5}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=5$, and at $a/\nu=3$ from $\res[2]{build/R9d-M4/descriptor/n5/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ to $\res[2]{build/R9d-M4/descriptor/n5/carleman=5}{lift_err_linf}$, still converging. The lift converges at $\rho$ far above one, and its convergence follows $a/\nu$, not $\rho$. At $a/\nu=10$ it diverges: the error grows from $\res[3]{build/R9e-M4/descriptor/n5/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=2$ to $\res[3]{build/R9e-M4/descriptor/n5/carleman=5}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=5$, against an amplitude of $10$. Its kernel stays one-dimensional and trusted at every order, the gap ratio $\res[2,sci]{build/R9e-M4/descriptor/n5/carleman=5}{gap_ratio}$ at $K=5$, and both forms prepare it, with infidelity $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R9e-M4/descriptor/n5/T2/carleman=5/filter=minimax}{infidelity}$ in the descriptor and $\res[2,sci]{stateprep/R9e-M4/standard/n5/T2/carleman=5/filter=minimax}{infidelity}$ in the standard form by exact emulation, while the decoded field is wrong by $\res[3]{stateprep/R9e-M4/descriptor/n5/T2/carleman=5/filter=minimax}{field_err_linf}$. This is a negative finding, analysed in \Cref{sec:carleman}: trust measures the kernel's isolation, not the lift's accuracy. The boundary lies between $a/\nu=3$ and $10$; where the per-order factor $a/(5\nu)$ reaches one, near $a/\nu=5$, is an extrapolation from these strengths, not a measurement. As on R8, the descriptor's time axis lowers the degree: at $a/\nu=1$, $K=2$ and $n_t=5$ it is $\res{stateprep/R9c-M4/descriptor/n5/T2/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{degree}$ against the standard form's $\res{stateprep/R9c-M4/standard/n5/T2/carleman=2/filter=minimax}{degree}$ (T2).

\paragraph{Validation.} The Taylor--Green vortex (R10a), whose two modes do not interact, has an exact lift, and the pipeline returns it to rounding: the lift's error is $\res[2,sci]{build/R10a-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ on twelve modes and $\res[2,sci]{build/R10a-M80/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ on eighty. \paragraph{The vortex pair.} A counter-rotating pair of norm $\|w_0\|=a$ excites modes of several $|\mathbf k|$, so its triads are active. The truncation's own error against a direct simulation, about $\prov{4\times10^{-3}}$, $\prov{4\times10^{-2}}$ and $\prov{0.2}$ on twelve modes at $a/\nu=3$, $10$ and $30$, is a property of the Galerkin system, not of the lift. The lift's error is set by the strength, as on Burgers (\Cref{fig:navier-stokes}); on twelve modes at $a/\nu=10$ it falls from $\res[2,sci]{build/R10c-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=2}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=2$ to $\res[2,sci]{build/R10c-M12/descriptor/n4/carleman=3}{lift_err_linf}$ at $K=3$. \subsection{Summary of Findings}
\label{sec:results-summary}
% ~0.5 pp. LAND: six numbered findings, AT LEAST TWO NEGATIVE (2, 3, 5 and 6 carry negative parts),
%   then the spine sentence (ThesisPlanning.md §3.3). No new numbers.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The standard form's cost is its Hamiltonian's.} With Wu et al.'s encoding the filter's degree on R2a grows as about $N^{10}$, mostly the encoder's cost; an exact encoding removes that part, and on one-axis problems with constant coefficients the degree still grows as $N^{2k}$, both as built and at the ideal-encoding bound (\Cref{tab:headline,tab:growth}).
    \item \textbf{The descriptor's degree advantage depends on the class of problem.} Its degree grows as about $N$ on one-axis problems whose leading coefficient does not vanish, against $N^{2k}$, and as about $N^2$ where the coefficient vanishes; on two axes it grows faster than $N^2$ per axis. Per query its circuit costs more than the structured encoding's at small $n$ and about the same at $n=8$. \emph{Negative:} the comparison reverses at the ideal bound on R3, whose coefficient vanishes at both ends, and on the heat equation the descriptor gains nothing at the resolutions estimated (\Cref{sec:results-scaling,sec:results-gap}).
    \item \textbf{The descriptor's gap erodes where a carried derivative is uncontrolled, and block rescaling restores it on one axis only.} On every one-axis problem run the rescaled gap is $\mathcal{O}(1)$ and the field block regains its weight, at no gate cost. \emph{Negative:} on two axes the rescaled gap still falls with $N$ (\Cref{sec:results-gap}).
    \item \textbf{Every verified circuit is its operator, and every trusted preparation meets its target.} Every composed Hamiltonian of at most 24 qubits is verified whole and every lift and coupling by its terms, the simulated circuit equals the exact emulation within $10^{-10}$ on every minimax record, and every trusted preparation meets $\varepsilon$, its a priori bound and its field budget. Wider reflections are built and counted but not independently verified (\Cref{sec:results-faithful}).
    \item \textbf{Coupled systems and Carleman lifts are prepared in both forms, but a trusted kernel can hide a diverging lift.} The mass form's gap is about twice the chain's on R7, and the descriptor's time axis lowers the degree on every lift compared. \emph{Negative:} on Burgers at $a/\nu=10$, and on Navier--Stokes at $a/\nu=30$ from $28$ modes, the lift diverges while its kernel stays one-dimensional and trusted (\Cref{sec:results-nonlinear,sec:results-ns}).
    \item \textbf{Navier--Stokes is reached by exact emulation and resource estimate, not by circuit simulation.} Its narrowest prepared circuit is wider than any simulated; the tensor layout's coupling is verified to $316$ modes and prepares nothing (\Cref{sec:results-ns}).
\end{enumerate}

Within its stated scope the central claim holds as measured: carrying the derivatives instead of eliminating them keeps the kernel and lowers the Hamiltonian's norm from $\Theta(N^8)$ to $\Theta(N^2)$ on a second-order equation, and with it the degree from about $N^4$ to about $N$ on a one-axis equation whose leading coefficient does not vanish; the price is a block register and a gap the derivative blocks erode, which an a priori rescaling restores on one axis at no gate cost but not on two. Where the leading coefficient vanishes the degree still grows as $N^2$, and on R3 at the ideal bound the standard form needs fewer queries. These are measurements on the problems run, between stated resolutions: the growth exponents are measured, not proved, and the descriptor's rescaled gap on one axis is measured, not derived. \Cref{sec:discussion} supplies the mechanism behind each finding and asks what the measured exponents imply for an end-to-end algorithm and for problems outside the benchmark set.
